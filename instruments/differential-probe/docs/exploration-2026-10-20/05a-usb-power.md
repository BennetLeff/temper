## Verdict and load budget

**Pursue scope-USB power as an optional next revision, retaining post-regulation initially. Reject a raw 5 V rail plus small inverter as a drop-in replacement.** Removing both batteries and the mechanical power switch is realistic; removing the low-noise LDOs simultaneously adds risk without much volume benefit. No performance or USB compatibility is established by this study.

At approximately ±4.8 V, two [ADA4817s](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ADA4817-1_4817-2.pdf), using the ±5 V table, consume 38 mA typical and 42 mA maximum quiescent. Three [OPA810s](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa810.pdf), using the 10 V table, add 11.1 mA typical / 13.8 mA maximum. These currents flow through **each rail**; they are not split between rails. Thus amplifiers alone require 49.1 / 55.8 mA per rail. Each 1 kΩ shunt-reference feed adds about 2.3 mA; comparator supply, adjustment networks, latch and warning LED add further loads. Rev B's 60–80 mA-per-battery allowance is plausible, but remains an estimate. Gain-network current, cable charging and saturation/clamp current prevent treating 80 mA as a maximum. Start converter design at ≥100 mA continuous per rail, with measured overload margin and explicit current limiting.

Rail headroom matters: a +3 kV tip creates +1.5 V at its tap; ADA4817's upper input limit is approximately `V+ − 2.8 V`, leaving only 0.5 V nominal margin at +4.8 V. The extended output target is ±3 V. Lowering supplies requires fresh gain, clipping, recovery and warning qualification. Raising directly to ±5 V also consumes the ADA4817's 10 V recommended total-supply ceiling before tolerance/ripple.

## USB contract and converter choices

“5 V USB” is not a precision rail. Qualify a conservative 4.35–5.5 V connector envelope, including cable drop, startup and interruption; the low endpoint is a robustness target, not a promise that every USB topology supplies the required power there. The [USB-IF specification package](https://www.usb.org/document-library/usb-20-specification) includes the 2014 VBUS-maximum ECN. [USB-IF compliance guidance](https://compliance.usb.org/index.asp?Format=Standard&UpdateFile=Policies) limits standard USB 2.0 peripherals to 500 mA; before configuration the ordinary unit-load allowance is 100 mA. A power-only plug does not enumerate. Use an explicitly supported scope accessory-power port, USB enumeration, or a correctly detected charging/Type-C current source. Two Type-C pull-downs alone do not authorize arbitrary current; [ST AN5225](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00536349-usb-typec-power-delivery-using-stm32-mcus-and-mpus-stmicroelectronics.pdf) explains default, 1.5 A and 3 A advertisements.

At 60–80 mA per rail, useful analog power is 0.576–0.768 W. Illustratively, ±6 V preregulation at the same load and 80% converter efficiency requires about 180–240 mA at 5 V, or 207–276 mA at 4.35 V, before overload. A documented 500 mA source is reasonable; a 100 mA assumption fails.

A concrete candidate is **LT3471EDD#PBF**, a 3 × 3 mm dual boost/inverter: 2.4–16 V input, fixed 1.2 MHz switching, independent soft-start, and a manufacturer ±7 V example rated +350/−250 mA. Configure approximately ±6 V, then retain ADP7142/ADP7182 at the present rails. This is a design candidate, not a validated schematic; verify magnetics, compensation, minimum input/current limit and thermal behavior. See the [LT3471 datasheet and example](https://www.analog.com/en/products/lt3471.html). At 80 mA the LDOs dissipate roughly 96 mW each versus roughly 336 mW with ideal fresh 9 V inputs. Their [positive](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ADP7142.pdf) and [negative](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ADP7182.pdf) datasheets provide dropout/PSRR requirements; the two polarities need separate noise checks.

The [LTC3260](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ltc3260.pdf) illustrates why “charge pump” is insufficient: 100 mA inverter capability, but only 50 mA per integrated LDO and no voltage boost. Its tabulated 32 Ω typical pump impedance is at 12 V, not a guaranteed USB value; at 80 mA that resistance alone implies 2.56 V droop. Its ripple equation gives approximately 8 mVpp at 80 mA, 500 kHz, 10 µF and 1 µs charge time, before ESR/spikes. That calculation does not restore lost DC headroom. [TPS65133](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps65133.pdf) offers regulated ±5 V/250 mA, but its recommended input stops at 5.0 V, and its output tolerances can exceed 10 V total: reject direct substitution.

## Grounding, validity and size

The probe is already BNC/earth referenced, but USB **adds a parallel ground return and loop**. Same-scope USB ground can carry converter return current through the BNC shield; a PC adds its chassis/peripheral network. A floating, isolated host supply removes a DC earth path only while other connections remain absent; isolation capacitance still conducts switching current. Isolated DC/DC can break the USB return loop, provided its shield does not bypass isolation, but adds transformer area and capacitance. It does not isolate the HV inputs. Bond cable shields at the enclosure entry and keep switching/displacement current out of tap and gain-reference returns. These mechanisms follow [ADI MT-095](https://www.analog.com/media/en/training-seminars/tutorials/MT-095.pdf). [Keysight's N2791A manual](https://prc.keysight.com/Content/PDF_Files/N2791-97008.pdf) demonstrates USB-powered, ground-referenced differential probes; [Pico's guide](https://www.picotech.com/download/manuals/ta041-ta042-ta043-ta044-ta057-differential-probes-users-guide.pdf) separately cautions that BNC measurement ground is not protective safety ground.

USB replaces battery depletion with cable brownout/unplugging. Add independent monitoring of both final rails, delayed VALID assertion, immediate invalid indication and controlled discharge. Check single-rail startup, clamps/references with DUT voltage present, back-powering through inputs/output, USB-only/BNC-only connection orders, and latch reset. Existing rail-reversal diodes and overload LED do not establish validity.

Acceptance must compare battery and USB noise spectra, 35–60 kHz gain/phase, 5 MHz bandwidth, CMRR with high-dV/dt common mode, overload recovery and cable placement. LDO PSRR does not suppress radiated magnetic coupling or ground error. Switching at 1.2 MHz remains inside the probe bandwidth.

Two [Energizer 522 cells](https://data.energizer.com/pdfs/522_ap.pdf) alone occupy 42 cm³ and weigh 92 g. A provisional 20 × 25 × 6 mm power region is 3 cm³; connector, protection, filter and retention require more. A 0.5–1 m, 3–4 mm cable occupies roughly 3.5–12.6 cm³ geometrically, plus plugs; allow approximately 10–30 g cable mass pending selection. Expect substantial body/service improvement, not a shorter HV ladder or guaranteed PCB shrink: enclosure and battery placement are not yet designed.
