# Bring-up, calibration and qualification

This procedure requires a DMM, oscilloscope and known AC impedance standards. No hardware was available during design. An unpowered visual inspection and continuity check are the first physical tests; successful CAD checks cannot substitute for them.

1. Inspect IC orientation and soldering against `previews/assembly-top.svg` and the native pad-1 audit. Fit J2, J3 and JP1–JP4 by hand. Install **one** 2.54 mm jumper cap on JP1 with USB unplugged. Use insulated standoffs in the four M3 holes.
2. With no DUT, check VBUS-to-ground and both 3.3 V rails for shorts. J3 pin1 is ground; pin2 is analog3.3 V. J2 is exclusively the DUT connector, not a power input. Never jumper J3.
3. Connect a current-monitored USB supply/computer link. Verify enumeration as the factory MCP2221A and run `temper-lcr list`. Check 3V3D and3V3A close to3.3 V, GP2 high after enumeration, and analog power removal during suspend. Investigate unexpected current or heating before proceeding. Do not change factory bridge configuration.
4. Check 16 MHz at R7 pin2/MCLK, and valid I²C traffic at R6 pin1/SDA and R5 pin1/SCL. Use a low-capacitance probe on MCLK. Check TIA_BIAS at C12 pin1 near 1.65 V. Measure differential DC at J2 before attaching sensitive parts; an ideal midpoint match is not a guaranteed zero-offset source.
5. Capture a 35–60 kHz open sweep. Scope DRIVE at R12 pin1 and TIA_OUT at R13 pin1 relative to J3 pin1. With a 10 Ω resistor attached, check sinusoidal shape, settling, clipping and any high-frequency oscillation. DUT_HI voltage is attenuated by source impedance. A conventional grounded oscilloscope probe must never ground DUT_LO; use differential measurement or two probes with both ground clips at J3 ground.
6. Repeat source/TIA waveform checks with 5 Ω,10 Ω,40 Ω,100 nF,0.5 µF, coil/pan and representative long leads. The generic model showed frequency peaking, so check ringing and distortion directly. Stop and revise compensation if oscillation or clipping occurs. Software DFT saturation heuristics do not detect every analog failure.
7. Keep leads short, close together and mechanically fixed. Capture open, measured10 Ω and40 Ω standards at the DUT contact plane using identical CLI settings. Fit calibration and verify a separate20 Ω standard. Repeat captures to establish repeatability. Validate capacitance sign with a known capacitor (negative reactance) and inductance sign with a known coil (positive reactance).
8. Compare against a calibrated reference LCR meter across the planned frequencies, ranges and representative capacitive/inductive loads. Record magnitude, phase and real-part error separately. Establish an ESR floor from phase uncertainty and fixture repeatability; do not infer it from good capacitance agreement.
9. Qualify each other range separately. Begin with 100/400 Ω and a separate200 Ω check;1/4 kΩ and2 kΩ check;10/40 kΩ and20 kΩ check. Measure actual AC reference impedances where parasitics matter. Never substitute an excited short for a reference.
10. Treat1–10 kHz r20 operation as experimental. Test a known9 µF standard and several resistive loads at 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 kHz; compare against the external instrument. A fixed16 MHz clock gives few cycles in the converter's1024-point record at the low end. If the error is unacceptable, use a revised lower/selectable clock design, not an undocumented correction factor.

Before every stage53 measurement: disconnect all power, discharge externally, verify residual voltage with a separate DMM, and isolate the component from parallel paths. The meter has no active discharge, voltage detector, fuse-protected DUT input or high-energy clamps. Do not use it as the discharge tool. A practical bench acceptance threshold is less than 10 mV residual differential voltage before connection; this is an operating precaution, not a survivable-input rating.

The first coil/pan sweep should document coil position, pan material, pan distance, lead layout and temperature. Repeatability across those conditions matters more than a single displayed L number. Do not use small-signal measurements to assert mains safety, power-stage switching behavior or high-current saturation.

Calibration standards, USB cable, leads and jumper cap are accessory purchases; they are not fitted to the PCB by this BOM. A single 2-pin2.54 mm shunt fits the range headers. Use measured low-inductance precision resistors as removable fixtures at J2; record their identity and characterized frequency response.
