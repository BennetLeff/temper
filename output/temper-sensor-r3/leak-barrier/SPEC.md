# T1 — proposed element-parallel leak barrier

Status: **proposed; not a qualified safety barrier**. Release requires supplier, fixture, seal and ageing evidence. The owner’s requirement is prevention of the first pan crossing above 300 °C for every leak whose untripped steady pan temperature would exceed that bar. This study reproduces a sampled model; it does not certify that universal requirement. [source: `production.release_status`, `hazard.criterion`, `hazard.continuous_status`]

## Construction and margins

- Maintain insulation resistance ≥ 10 MΩ at 300 °C throughout the hot lead length, between otherwise isolated conductors, at the element terminations and across the element **as a parasitic path excluding the intended platinum resistance**. Include the bore exits, welds, seal contamination paths and hot-to-cold transition, not just the capillary body. [source: `insulation.minimum_ohm`, `insulation.test_C`]
- Use separate inorganic bores/sleeves through the hot zone, with strain relief and clean, separated terminations; demonstrate the complete thermal profile keeps every organic insulator and seal inside its supplier rating. A capillary material figure does not bound surface contamination or a damaged assembly. [source: `insulation.candidate_status`, `production.release_status`]
- The sampled worst hazardous resistance is 1732.1 Ω. The design minimum has 5773.34× resistance margin; a 100 MΩ part would have 57733.39×. These are model sensitivity ratios, not demonstrated lifetime or reliability factors. [source: `hazard.highest_sampled_hazardous_ohm`, `insulation.margin_to_sampled_hazard`]
- Do not label the next sampled resistance, 2319.9 Ω, safe: its largest final-window mean is 299.8 °C, just 0.2 K below the bar, but its largest transient is 304.2 °C. There is no derived continuous hazardous cutoff. Using that next sample only as a sensitivity denominator reduces the minimum-insulation ratio to 4310.53×. [source: `hazard.next_sample_ohm`, `hazard.next_sample_max_steady_C`, `hazard.next_sample_steady_headroom_K`, `hazard.next_sample_max_transient_C`, `hazard.continuous_status`, `insulation.margin_to_next_sample_sensitivity_only`]
- Do not credit the proposed 4‑bore fused-silica value of approximately 100 MΩ at 350 °C as evidence until the supplier confirms geometry, atmosphere, voltage, cleanliness, lot variability and ageing. Alumina remains an unqualified alternative. [source: `insulation.candidate_bores`, `insulation.candidate_supplier_ohm`, `insulation.candidate_supplier_C`, `insulation.candidate_status`]

## Per-cartridge production tests

- Test 100% of cartridges with serial-number-linked results; quarantine failures and investigate process cause. Retest after repair through the complete sequence. Retain raw currents/resistances, temperatures, fixture calibration, dwell, polarity, serial and lot traceability. [source: `production.coverage_percent`, `production.release_status`]
- **Before electrically joining the element:** test every isolated lead pair in the actual lead/separator subassembly at 25 °C and with the hot zone at 300 °C, applying 100 V DC, current limited to 1 mA, reading after 60 s, in both polarities. Preserve the intended cold-end thermal profile. Guard fixture leakage without excluding a real DUT surface path. Test exposed terminations as far as this stage permits. [source: `production.ir_temperatures_C`, `production.ir_voltage_Vdc`, `production.ir_current_limit_mA`, `production.ir_dwell_s`]
- The IR lower bound must be ≥ 10 MΩ. With the proposed error bound of 5% of indicated resistance, require displayed resistance ≥ 10.53 MΩ (rounded upward), including fixture/background and temperature effects in that bound. The voltage and dwell are proposed process settings; confirm they are nondestructive with the supplier before release. This is an IR test, not a dielectric-withstand or reinforced-insulation certification. [source: `insulation.minimum_ohm`, `production.ir_relative_error_bound`, `production.ir_relative_error_basis`, `production.ir_display_accept_min_ohm`, `production.ir_voltage_status`]
- **After assembly:** tie the Pt100 leads together and test that bundle to any conductive sheath/disc and other electrically isolated accessible circuits using the same IR conditions, only where the design is intended to be insulated. This detects common-mode leakage to the body, not differential leakage across the element. Never apply the IR voltage between terminals of the connected Pt100 or through the converter. [source: `production.ir_voltage_status`, `production.ir_temperatures_C`]
- **Hot accuracy:** compare the assembled cartridge’s independent Kelvin bridge reading against a traceable reference at the element location. Use a low-current bridge, ≤ 1 mA, an isothermal fixture that represents the hot end, and record the complete first warm-up and cool-down at 25, 150, 250, 300.0 °C. Require a reference range ≤ 0.05 K over 5 min at the hot comparison, as well as demonstrated spatial uniformity. Do not use the cooker’s Pt100-derived control temperature as the reference. [source: `production.accuracy_temperatures_C`, `production.max_readout_current_mA`, `production.accuracy_stability_range_K`, `production.accuracy_stability_window_min`]
- Accept the final hot **measured** difference only within ±0.5 K, without fitting a gain/offset at this final test. Reference allowance ±0.1 K, fixture gradients ±0.1 K, and bridge/readout plus residual self-heating allowance ±0.05 K must be independently validated. Their linear sum is 0.25 K; thus this measured acceptance supports only a ±0.75 K true-error bound. If a true ±0.5 K accuracy specification is required, use the tighter measured acceptance in the first row below. Record the other temperatures and reject intermittent discontinuities; do not claim a quantified guarantee at them from the hot acceptance alone. [source: `production.hot_measured_acceptance_K`, `metrology`, `accuracy_acceptance`, `production.accuracy_other_points_status`]
- An intrinsic positive element error can conceal leakage. The budget carries +0.75 K at the hot point, from the existing element-class allowance. Retain this intrinsic-element bound after ageing using independent supplier data and unshunted element witnesses; a positive element drift cancelling a shunt cannot be separated by the assembled accuracy test alone. A final-test adjustment could absorb the defect and is prohibited. For an out-of-class element, a nonlinear/voltage-dependent shunt, or a fixture outside these bounds, the detection calculation does not apply. [source: `metrology.positive_element_bound_K`, `metrology.status`, `accuracy_acceptance`]

- Detection means rejection of a parallel resistance **strictly below** the listed boundary under the stated masking bounds; equality may pass. The reference-only column assumes no other positive error. The full-budget column includes the element’s possible positive error as well as measurement allowances, stacked linearly. These are bounds, not an RSS or a statistical confidence claim. [source: `metrology`, `accuracy_acceptance`]
- The assembled hot-accuracy test is a coarse shunt screen: it cannot certify the design IR limit across the connected element. That parasitic path is electrically inseparable from the platinum resistance. Preassembly IR, supplier process control and representative witness structures therefore carry the inaccessible insulation claim; their representativeness and assembly damage controls are release prerequisites. A passing accuracy reading cannot close that evidence gap. [source: `insulation.minimum_ohm`, `accuracy_acceptance`, `production.release_status`]
- Repeat functional checks under the actual service bias and on the final MAX31865 channel, against the independent reference, to expose voltage- or bias-dependent leakage. Establish that channel’s own uncertainty budget before applying an accuracy acceptance; the bridge allowance is not a MAX31865 specification. The nominal firmware conversion illustration in the JSON is not hardware metrology. [source: `conversion.meaning`, `metrology.status`, `production.release_status`]

## Seal and insulation qualification

- Use production-representative assemblies from 3 independent lots, with 24 assemblies per group below, balanced across lots. Keep 12 unstressed controls. Add 36 assemblies for ageing, evenly split across the ageing temperatures and lots. Total proposed assembly count: 192. Retain matched, electrically open insulation witnesses for the otherwise inaccessible differential paths; they are additional coupons, not substitutes for assemblies. [source: `qualification.lots`, `qualification.per_group_assemblies`, `qualification.controls`, `qualification.ageing_assemblies`, `qualification.total_assemblies`]

- The standard texts were **not read**. Only method names are cited. A competent laboratory must specify severity, dwell, ramp, humidity, condensation, recovery and sequencing from the licensed standards and measured service environment before a qualification claim. The wash and oil values above are engineering proposals, not requirements attributed to IEC. [source: `qualification.standard_text_read`, `qualification.standard_severities`]
- Evaluate as-exposed wet/cold readings and the first heating trajectory before any drying bake; also inspect cooling and post-dry readings. Maintain logging during exposure where feasible: a dry post-test measurement alone may erase a moisture-driven leak. Stress the intended hot-face/seal gradient, not an entire cartridge at a temperature beyond its cold-end materials’ ratings. Reject cracks, tracking, compromised seals or intermittent shorts. [source: `qualification.methods`, `production.accuracy_temperatures_C`, `production.release_status`]
- Pass requires **0 failures**, IR ≥ 10 MΩ at 300 °C on measurable assembly paths and matched differential witnesses, and assembled hot measured accuracy within ±0.5 K under the production metrology budget. All controls must remain stable; failure analysis must distinguish fixture drift from DUT deterioration. No re-zeroing after stress. Record the unobservable across-element IR limitation even when all tests pass. [source: `qualification.allowed_failures`, `qualification.post_IR_ohm`, `qualification.post_IR_C`, `qualification.post_accuracy_acceptance_K`, `metrology`]
- Age at 250, 275, 300 °C hot-zone temperatures and read at 1000, 3000, 10000 h, with the cold end held at its service profile. Measure IR at the common reference temperature before and after each interval. Fit a degradation rate and activation energy with uncertainty; use upper damage-rate/lower initial-IR bounds for mission-life calculations. Do not accelerate beyond a material transition or extrapolate across a changed failure mechanism. [source: `qualification.ageing_temperatures_C`, `qualification.ageing_read_hours`, `ageing.law`, `ageing.supplier_rate_or_life_anchor`]
- The lifetime sensitivity floor of hazardous resistance × 10 is **not** a relaxed qualification acceptance: the post-stress IR requirement remains the design minimum. The ageing table in NOTES is conditional, with no supplier rate anchor. [source: `ageing.target_R300_ohm`, `qualification.post_IR_ohm`, `ageing.supplier_rate_or_life_anchor`]
- With no failures among 24 independent units, the one-sided 95% binomial upper failure fraction is still 11.73%. Do not pool unlike stress groups or claim a field failure rate from this sample plan. [source: `qualification.per_group_assemblies`, `qualification.confidence_level`, `qualification.zero_failure_binomial_upper_failure_fraction`]

## Residual risks and next layers

| Residual | Next available layer | What remains uncovered | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanical damage or contamination after production | Leak-chain latch and setpoint-relative rim-limit latch; independent CR-5 hardware rim trip | A fast pan rise can outrun every rim layer; there is no universal first-crossing guarantee. | `residual_layers.coverage`, `hazard.latched_before_bar`, `hazard.hazardous_runs` |
| Leak exists only hot or during wet heating | Hot production/qualification trajectories; leak-chain, rim-limit and CR-5 in service | Cold soak can miss thermally activated conduction; a dry hot test can erase moisture. | `production.accuracy_temperatures_C`, `qualification.methods`, `residual_layers.coverage` |
| Leak already present at a genuinely cold start | Cold-soak latch at PREHEAT entry and existing low-resistance window | Cold soak skips without qualified history/reference and misses moderate or hot-only leakage; rail validity changes its threshold. | `residual_layers.cold_soak_unknown_rail_threshold_K`, `residual_layers.cold_soak_measured_rail_threshold_K` |
| Warped pan/cool glass, cold bead, or glass lag | Rim-limit and CR-5 remain independent layers where their signals reach threshold | Glass and bead uncertainty can leave the rim cool while the pan crosses the bar. | `residual_layers.glass_offset_K`, `residual_layers.bead_stack_at_250C_K`, `residual_layers.cr5_original_glass_trip_band_C` |
| Correlated seal ageing, non-ohmic or bias-dependent shunt | Qualification at actual gradients/bias, process controls, then the same latches | No measured ageing-rate anchor or proven detection coverage for these mechanisms. | `ageing.supplier_rate_or_life_anchor`, `production.release_status` |
| Contact loss, estimator bias, power-stage failure, or a pan hotspot away from the sensor | Relevant contact/supervisor and independent hardware protections need their own evidence | Insulation is only a barrier against element-parallel leakage; this study does not qualify other faults. | `provenance.scope`, `residual_layers.coverage` |

- Keep leak-chain, rim-limit, cold-soak and CR-5 enabled; none may be credited as a universal fallback for failure of this barrier. Verify the fitted CR-5 variant on the actual controller: the JSON records the original band used by the sweep, not proof of the later proposed divider change. A production pass is not authorization to declare the owner’s bar met over life. [source: `residual_layers.coverage`, `residual_layers.cr5_original_glass_trip_band_C`, `production.release_status`]
