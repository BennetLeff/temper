R2.1 SENSOR VALIDATION PLAN — NO SENSOR GEOMETRY CHANGE

PURPOSE
Replace the assumed thermal conductances with measured behavior before changing
cap thickness, spring preload or seal architecture. The previous 216 scenarios
were sensitivity examples, not observed or predicted product accuracy.

1. COLD FORCE / TRAVEL
Use the adjustable sensor fixture with a displacement gauge and calibrated load
cell. Record complete-cartridge force at 0,0.3,0.6,0.9,1.2 mm loading/unloading.
Test with the diaphragm installed, because its force is absent from the spring
formula. Establish allowable fixture/glass loads before choosing force levels.
Record preload, friction/hysteresis, full-stroke clearance and repeatability.
The spring-only model predicts force INCREASE, not total pan contact force.

2. THERMAL CONTACT IDENTIFICATION
Use an externally heated reference coupon initially; the earlier metal fixture
was not an induction-coil fixture. Record independently measured temperature at
or adjacent to the cap contact location, with the spatial reference offset and
instrument uncertainty documented. Do not use stove setpoint as ground truth.
Record raw RTD value, cap/reference temperature, housing/guide temperature, force,
travel, bond material/thickness, lead route and ambient. Keep time bases aligned.
Run repeated settled holds and temperature steps. Choose temperatures and force
levels only within the selected components' documented ratings and cold-test
limits. Repeat under distinct allowable contact forces and surface conditions.
Fit effective pan conductance and parasitic losses using multiple temperatures,
housing conditions and transients; one settled reading cannot identify them all.
Hold out at least one condition/pan from fitting to test predictive usefulness.

3. ACCURACY / RESPONSE
Calculate steady error against the local calibrated reference, hysteresis and
repeatability. Report t90 to the SENSOR'S final value separately from time to a
specified band around true pan temperature. Include reference-placement gradients
and calibration/readout error in the uncertainty budget. Agree numeric product
accuracy/response targets before declaring any configuration acceptable.

4. NEXT SIMULATION INPUTS
Cap roof/lip: actual alloy/temper, preform, tooling radii, support and load cases.
Diaphragm: compound, temperature-dependent elastic data, squeeze/friction and life.
Spring: supplier force-length curve, free length, end treatment and tolerances.
Wire: strand construction, minimum bend radius, slack/strain relief and cycles.
Thermal model: measured contact vs force, bond properties and housing heat leaks.
EM model: actual coil drive, frequency/current, ferrites, materials and geometry.

DECISION RULE
Change geometry only when a measured result or bounded requirement supports it.
Examples: excessive lag with sufficient contact -> examine cap/attached mass;
bias dependent on housing temperature -> reduce parasitic conduction; force
variation -> revise spring/guide/diaphragm stack. Preserve cap capture and groove
wall until strength/forming evidence supports an alternative.

No leakage, fatigue, insulation, thermal accuracy or forming qualification is
claimed by this plan or by the existing CAD interference checks.
