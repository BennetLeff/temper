# Evidence behind the manufacturing-review method

Numbers below are the slide labels printed in the linked MIT PDFs. They are teaching examples, not Temper specifications. Links point to primary sources; this is an original summary.

| Source-derived teaching | Primary source |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing decisions involve quality, cost, rate, and flexibility. Injection-mold part geometry must account for wall consistency, reinforcement, draft, ejection, and process window. | [MIT 2.008 Injection Molding I, slide 4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec02.pdf); [Injection Molding II, slides 2, 7, 16–18](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec03.pdf) |
| Assembly involves feeding, handling, mating, visibility, access, orientation, and lead-ins. Disassembly for repair/inspection can justify a separate part; a better assembly metric can increase functional risk. | [MIT 2.875 Design for Assembly, slides 12–20](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/319a0f903d70a1a731d869d2a1c70c7c_class16_dfa04.pdf) |
| Assembly sequence is a design choice; tests, reorientation, and temporary disassembly can be steps in that sequence. | [MIT 2.875 Assembly Sequence Analysis, slides 3–7](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/aa4c12b52f7d971af7028cbed79513a2_cls10_asy_seq_04.pdf) |
| An assembly-level characteristic depends on the location strategy and variation through its parts and joints; mean shift differs from spread. | [MIT 2.875 Variation in Assemblies, slides 2, 4–6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/290ff5175c9d657c882a3be78ed8453d_cls9_assy_var04b.pdf) |
| Quality requires a measurable characteristic and specification; process capability and control limits describe observed process behavior and differ from product specification limits. | [MIT 2.008 Variation and Quality, slides 4–6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec11.pdf); [Statistical Process Control, slides 4–5, 15–16](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec12.pdf) |

**Engineering extensions for Temper, not claims made by these courses:** Carry exact electronic part, footprint, and substitute identity into procurement and inspection; distinguish production screens from qualification; identify retest after assembly changes; include a record of requirement source, method, acceptance, result, and traceability. The lectures support measuring and controlling variation, but Temper-specific limits, process capabilities, and release decisions require product evidence.
