# MIT mechanical source notes

These are method sources. PDF page numbers below are one-based file pages, not printed slide numbers. The linked original files should be checked before making a numerical design rule; no course supplies Temper acceptance values.

- **Functional requirements and error budget.** 2.70 *Deterministic Design Process*, [PDF pp. 21–26, 31](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2001/Topic%201%20Deterministic%20Design%20Process.pdf), classifies geometric, thermal, load-induced, and process errors; apportions allowable error; and distinguishes linear sum from root-sum-square for uncorrelated sources. For Temper, carrying these categories into coil registration or enclosure interfaces is an engineering extension.
- **Key characteristics.** 2.875 *Key Characteristics*, [PDF pp. 2, 5, 15](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/ccd7fd17028fa75457996083cf90f8d2_class_kcs_04.pdf), limits special attention to requirements both important and threatened by process variation, then flows product requirements into assembly/manufacturing controls. A proposed Temper characteristic needs its own requirement and measurement method.
- **Datum flow before tolerance arithmetic.** 2.875 *The Datum Flow Chain*, [PDF pp. 2, 4–6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/d39cbc40396e52ddb6eab3e879f25f2d_cls11_13_dfc_04.pdf), maps dimensional relationships through parts, fixtures, and assembly sequence; p. 5 explicitly distinguishes that map from later tolerance analysis.
- **Tolerance versus variation.** 2.875 *Variation Buildup in Single Parts*, [PDF p. 3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/8badde647439f7a92165d8b8135817d8_cls8_part_var04a.pdf), defines tolerance as specified acceptance limits, variation as what real parts do, and clearance as space between surfaces.
- **Constraint and thermal growth.** 2.72 *Lecture 06: Constraints*, [PDF pp. 5, 15–17, 20](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-72-elements-of-mechanical-design-spring-2009/22c0cb8444c787d92b49afe5ed97ff7b_MIT2_72s09_lec06.pdf), contrasts under/exact/overconstraint and shows misalignment and thermal growth addressed with directional compliance. 2.70 *FUNdaMENTAL Principles*, [PDF pp. 20, 34, 37](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2002/Topic%202%20Fundamental%20Principles.pdf), warns that assembly and expansion can force deformation. These are machine examples; a PCB mounting prescription must be checked against Temper's actual geometry and loads.
- **Stiffness and joints.** 2.70 *Structural Design 1*, [PDF pp. 6, 10](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2003/PMD%20Topic%209a%20Structural%20design%201.pdf), distinguishes static stiffness from dynamic response. 2.70 *Design of Joints and Bearing Rails*, [PDF pp. 3, 14–16](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2006/PMD%20Topic%2013%20Joint%20design.pdf), relates bolt preload, contact, joint stiffness, and cyclic preload loss. Its precision-machine heuristics are not universal cooker fastener values.
- **Assembly and service.** 2.875 *Design for Assembly*, [PDF pp. 3, 5, 14](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/319a0f903d70a1a731d869d2a1c70c7c_class16_dfa04.pdf), favors stable, self-aligning parts, forgiving fits, visible/reachable steps, and clear orientation; p. 3 gives a snap-fit/drop-test counterexample. Applying this to service access and seal reassembly is a Temper-specific inference that needs prototype testing.
