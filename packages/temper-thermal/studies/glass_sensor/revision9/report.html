What is settled now

  1. Do not treat the R7 bond and short leads as fabrication instructions

    Keep R7 as the numerical comparison. The exact 908 sheet does not establish a mandatory >300°C cure—the cited complete cure belongs to 903HP—but its thin-layer process remains undocumented. The standard-569 open witness is a better-supported experimental branch.

  2. Use accessible geometry and full leads before miniaturizing

    The first witness has only cap, bond and bare alumina, allowing inspection and sectioning. A later M222 article retains delivered native leads and remote electrical connections, after exact bond-face and compatibility disposition. Neither is the complete R7 cartridge.

  3. Resolve attachments as well as capture clearances

    The baseline fingers have only a seated butt face. The separate C9 candidate replaces them with 316L brackets and nominal M1.6 screw/nut connections. Radial bracket insertion precedes housing installation. The model checks bearing surfaces and sampled assembly access; it does not establish preload, hot strength or lifetime.

Any bolted-bracket candidate is also separate: added metal and altered carrier geometry require their own thermal and induction assessment.

What still cannot be closed on the desktop

Exact sensor/bond compatibility and received-lot processing need manufacturer information or controlled specimens. 250°C moving-seal force, jam-resistant contact, hot retention strength and induction/endurance need the specified hardware tests. The seal remains unselected and production contact sensing remains unavailable. Physical load, duty, leakage and shutdown criteria must be defined before qualification.

The immediate physical sequence is process witnesses → open M222 with full leads → measured enclosed successor → sealed contact/retention qualification → induction and endurance. Supplier questions are narrowed and drafted; none has been sent.
