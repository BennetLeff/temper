# C9 bracket thermal path: bounded sensitivity, not a cartridge prediction

The C9 CAD has **0.10 mm nominal clearance between the island and each catch bracket at rest**. The new metal path conducts by solid contact only in the raised capture pose. This adapter retains the pinned R7 M222 100 µm thermal network and adds an explicitly hypothetical path from its island node through the C9 retention parts to an external thermal reservoir. The rest row reproduces R7 **by construction**; C9 has different metal and carrier geometry, so that row is not its actual response.

## Geometry and model contract

The [C9 capture audit](../../revision9/mechanical/bolted-candidate-checks.json) gives three raised-pose island contacts of 0.594659 mm² each, bracket-to-head area 4.523893 mm² and nut-to-carrier area 6.323410 mm². Its island contact centroid is at radius 3.621686 mm, the foot begins at radius 4.05 mm, the screw axis is at 7.10 mm, and the vertical distance to the foot is 1.72 mm. The inherited [R2 CAD builder](../../revision2/mechanical/build.py) defines the retained finger upright as 0.5 × 0.8 mm; the C9 foot is 3.4 × 0.3 mm. Those cross sections and straight distances define an **idealized bar resistance**, not a resolved 3D heat-flow solution. Local bends, sharp roots and the actual fraction of section carrying heat could reduce conductance.

For each of three brackets the series path is island contact → 0.40 mm² upright → parallel branches to carrier. One branch crosses the 1.02 mm² foot to the screw head, a nominal Ø1.6 mm, 1.3 mm long shaft, and nut contact. It treats the shaft as a full cylinder and omits thread constriction and screw/nut thread-contact resistance. The second branch is an **independent effective foot-to-carrier contact**. The branches reconverge at a carrier node; three legs act in parallel, followed by a swept carrier-to-reservoir conductance. The direct foot contact area and pressure were not measured by the existing CAD audit, so its conductance is an independent parameter, not an area-derived result. All three explicit interface contacts share a swept coefficient for compactness; they need not be equal in hardware.

The network eliminates the carrier node as a pure resistance. The `reservoir60/120/200` columns vary the temperature **downstream of the carrier-to-reservoir resistance**. They are not assigned carrier temperatures. Pan/glass/other body boundaries remain at 200/80/60°C for steady underread. The step test keeps all sinks at 25°C, and all R7 capacities are fixed. Steel conductivity 5/15/30 W/mK, interface coefficients 1,000/10,000/100,000 W/m²K or ideal, direct-foot conductance 0/0.001/0.01 W/K per bracket, and carrier-to-reservoir conductance 0.001/0.01 W/K are **hypothetical sensitivity values**. The 15 W/mK center value matches an inherited steel proxy; no hot 316L or A4 value has been verified here.

## Result and decision

At the middle assumed coefficients (10,000 W/m²K, 15 W/mK, 0.01 W/K downstream, 0.001 W/K per direct foot), the engaged path adds 0.003053 W/K from island to reservoir. With a 60°C reservoir, calculated underread moves from the R7 2.474°C control to **2.791°C**. With a 120°C reservoir it is **2.479°C**; with a 200°C reservoir it is **2.062°C**. This sign change makes the downstream temperature as important as the contact conductance. Across the sampled 60°C rows, engaged underread spans about **2.59–2.83°C**; this is a scenario range, not an uncertainty bound or a prediction for C9.

The computed pan-relative t90 is 2.91 s in every row at the model's 10 ms resolution. That only shows that this conductance perturbation does not change the **fixed-R7-capacity** threshold in the sampled cases. The model does not move bracket/screw/nut heat capacity, replace the old ceramic-finger capacity allocation, model gap gas/radiation at rest, calculate carrier temperature, or assess induction self-heating. It cannot establish C9 response time or a total upper bound on leakage. All physical results are **NOT_RUN**.

Before selecting C9 from thermal performance, measure the cold/hot island-to-bracket gap under pan load and capture, carrier and housing temperatures, bracket/foot contact conductance or a calibrated transient heat-flow specimen, and the added part heat capacities/material properties. Those data would support a coupled C9-specific network or 3D transient model. The immediate design action is to keep the 0.10 mm catch gap and foot contact as explicit inspection and thermal test points; no compensation or control change follows from this sensitivity table.

## Reproduce and audit

From this folder run `sh run.sh`, then `sh check_runner.sh`. Set `TEMPER_GLASS_STUDY_ROOT` to an alternate study directory if needed. The runner verifies pinned R5/R6/R7/R9 Rust sources, R7 geometry and C9 CAD/audit sources, snapshots every consumed file, runs the inherited and new Rust tests, builds with standalone `rustc`, and writes `results/run-inputs.sha256` only after a complete run. No Cargo, extension build, CAD export or persistent binary is used. The temporary test/model binaries are deleted by the runner. The negative script confirms a bad source hash and a deliberately corrupted consumed snapshot each invalidate an old receipt without altering a seeded physics CSV. A CSV without a matching success receipt is stale.

`results/c9_conductance_sensitivity.csv` has 27 rows (one open reference, 24 contact/carrier/foot combinations, two steel-conductivity probes). `results/tests.txt` records **54 passing Rust tests**, including inherited checks, matrix parity at zero added path, series-bottleneck behavior, foot-branch monotonicity and independent reservoir-temperature superposition. `results/runner-negative.csv` records the two runner controls. The source pins and output hashes are in `results/run-inputs.sha256`.
