# R10 bond and lead decision — controlled preparation

Status **PREPARATION_ONLY / NOT_RUN / QUESTIONS_NOT_SENT**, 2026-10-05. This resolves the next specimen and measurement definitions; it does not qualify a 250 °C production bond, an RTD face, an electrical joint, or a finished cartridge. Applied the Temper manufacturing and mechanical review methods. Source revision is the committed R9 study; no R7–R9 geometry was changed.

## Decisions now supported

1. **Make the R9 P9-569-OPEN-100 and -150 specimens first.** Both are 316L D6 cap → nominal 2.7 × 2.5 mm standard Ceramabond 569 volume → bare 2.3 × 2.1 × 0.25 mm alumina tile. R9 CAD reports nominal 0.100/0.150 mm gaps and 0.6750/1.0125 mm³ bond volumes; the actual cured gap is an inspection result. These are deliberately *material/process witnesses*, without RTD, leads, cover, seal or retention. Aremco's current general ceramic-adhesive guide recommends 50–200 µm operating glue lines, even pressure and air set followed by the product-specific 569 cure of 2 h at 200 °F (~93 °C). It also warns that dehydration may continue on later heating; its moisture-property table reflects a 700 °F/2 h preconditioning. The low-temperature process therefore cannot be declared a qualified hot wet insulator. [Aremco 25F, pp. 8–11](https://www.aremco.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/A0-Catalog-25F.pdf). The inherited geometry and STEP identity are in R9 `mechanical-resolution.md` and `mechanical/P9-569-OPEN-{100,150}.step`.
2. **Use exact M222 32208550 as the first later live-element article, with both 10 ± 1 mm delivered leads intact.** Do not transfer R7's 1 mm native stubs, hot joins, R0.5 forming assumptions, cover adhesive films or Class A-after-assembly assertion into that build. Place the open article in an external fixture, keeping temporary electrical connections outside the D6 cap. Hold bonding of a real RTD until its exact bonding face is identified by the manufacturer or a documented sacrificial cross-section and engineering review. That face decision does **not** establish chemical compatibility with 569: the first live article is itself a controlled, sacrificial compatibility experiment, pending manufacturer input. A bare-alumina P9 result cannot establish passivation/fixation-glass compatibility. [M222 v03 pp. 1–2](https://yageogroup.com/content/datasheet/asset/file/YAGEO_Nexensos_M222_Datasheet_EN); [IST application note, §§2, 11](https://www.ist-ag.com/sites/default/files/downloads/ATP_E.pdf).
3. **Treat measurement-contact geometry as part of article identity.** The M222 catalog nominal-resistance measuring point is **8 mm from the body**, not at the body or the arbitrary end of a harness. With a nominal 10 mm wire, the stated lead-length tolerance leaves nominally 1–3 mm beyond that point; this is only a geometric subtraction, not a supplier-approved microclip layout. A four-wire split made at the distal native tip removes downstream extension resistance but still includes both native lead segments from the element to the split. If the voltage pickoffs can be made reproducibly at the documented 8 mm point without damaging the leads, record them and compare there. If they cannot, use measured, fixed alternate coordinates and label those readings **assembly datum**, with a same-geometry untreated sensor witness. Never call an alternate-datum reading a catalog tolerance check. [M222 v03 p. 1](https://yageogroup.com/content/datasheet/asset/file/YAGEO_Nexensos_M222_Datasheet_EN).
4. **Keep IST 101941 as a separate geometry branch.** Its exact order table identifies P0K1.308.3K.A.007, 3 × 0.8 × 0.25/0.6 mm and 7 mm Ø0.15 mm leads. The family offers a metallized backside, but the exact row does not specify one. The general IST contact datum is **5 mm from the wire end**; subtracting from nominal 7 mm suggests 2 mm from the body only if the listed wire-length reference is applicable. The exact face, film depth, fixation footprint, exit coordinates and safe forming envelope remain unprovided. R7's 0.8066 mm installed path and near-element bend cannot be approved by this public sheet. [DTP300 E2.4.3 pp. 1–3](https://www.ist-ag.com/sites/default/files/downloads/DTP300_E.pdf); [IST ATP E2.4.4 §§2, 6, 11](https://www.ist-ag.com/sites/default/files/downloads/ATP_E.pdf); [IST handling FAQ](https://www.ist-ag.com/en/faq).
5. **Treat a factory extension as an option requiring a new package study.** YAGEO lists a factory-laser-welded M222-WE-200 Pt100, but the catalog example is **F0.3**, 200 ± 2 mm extension, order 5157675, rather than the selected bare F0.15 32208550. It also offers customizable sensor/length/material. The listed Ni conductor resistance is 2.546 Ω/m: two 0.200 m lengths represent approximately **1.0184 Ω** of series conductor *at the stated per-metre datum* before any distal Kelvin split. The WE sheet does not clearly define whether its declared nominal sensor resistance includes/calibrates those wires; this arithmetic is **not an incremental temperature-error prediction**. Compare actual supplier resistance reference planes and wire temperature gradients before assigning an error. Its length and conductor also change thermal and harness force paths. The CE-series catalog M222 Pt100 likewise uses F0.3 and has a 260 °C assembly limit. Neither is an approved substitution; request a custom F0.15, suitable lead length and defined resistance datum only if local remote joining proves undesirable. [YAGEO WE v04 pp. 1–2](https://www.yageogroup.com/content/datasheet/asset/file/YAGEO_Nexensos_WE-Series_Datasheet_EN); [YAGEO CE v05 pp. 1–2](https://www.yageogroup.com/content/datasheet/asset/file/YAGEO_Nexensos_CE-Series_Datasheet_EN).

## Electrical consequence and measurement contract

```text
element -- native A -- [voltage A] -- native tip A -- [current A]
element -- native B -- [voltage B] -- native tip B -- [current B]
```

The drawing is a measurement concept, not a joining instruction. Current and voltage contacts may need separate non-damaging external fixtures. At fixed current, the measured voltage includes the element plus the two native segments up to the voltage contacts. Force-wire and extension-wire drops beyond them are rejected by the four-wire instrument. When voltage contacts move, the resistance datum moves. When only the native tip can be accessed, both native leads remain in the reading. A disconnected or asymmetric sense node must be recorded as a fault, not “corrected” by a thermal compensation fit.

For every pre/post process comparison, record exact contact distances from a photographed body-edge datum on **both** leads, excitation current, current direction, actual reference temperature and stabilization criterion, instrument/calibration identity, and the raw forward/reversed voltages or resistances. Reverse current or measure zero-current EMF where the instrument supports it so thermoelectric offsets from unlike metals do not masquerade as RTD resistance. Use the M222 sheet's 0.3–1 mA Pt100 measuring-current band as a manufacturer reference and explicitly check self-heating in the installed fixture; do not assume its free-element response or 0 °C self-heating coefficient applies to the bond. Compare like-for-like contact geometry and temperature, including an untouched same-lot witness. [M222 v03 pp. 1–2](https://yageogroup.com/content/datasheet/asset/file/YAGEO_Nexensos_M222_Datasheet_EN).

The M222 sheet lists welding, crimping and brazing as connection technologies. That does **not** approve the R7 Cu-to-Pt-clad-Ni bead, 1 mm stub, fixture force or a particular joining energy. The ≥9 N lead-tensile line is a component property, not an allowable harness load or joint-pull acceptance. The live-element open article can avoid permanent local joins; an enclosed successor needs a separately developed joint/process, installed calibration, hot insulation and harness-load check. [M222 v03 p. 2](https://yageogroup.com/content/datasheet/asset/file/YAGEO_Nexensos_M222_Datasheet_EN).
