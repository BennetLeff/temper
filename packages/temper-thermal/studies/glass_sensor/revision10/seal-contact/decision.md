# R10 seal and contact decision: static bulkhead experiment

Reviewed 2026-10-05. Scope: simulation and test preparation. No hardware, supplier response, induction run, leak test or detector qualification. **PHYSICAL_NOT_RUN.** This is a proposed engineering experiment, not production CAD or permission to heat a pan.

## Decisions after R9

## Boundary and buildability

From cookware downward: **cap/moving cartridge → wet well → stationary lower bulkhead → dry electronics**. The glass aperture is a separate perimeter joint. The bulkhead must keep electronics dry even if the well fills. A drain must discharge away from electronics, controls, coil and touchable electrical parts. A drain is not a pressure reference; its blockage cannot be presumed detected. The cap/island crevice, R9 bracket passages, spring and guides are deliberately wet in this branch. Each needs residue/cleaning tolerance or replacement access. Liquid-level/overflow detection and a debris trap are unselected. Optical witnesses must view through a stationary sealed window or stay wholly behind the bulkhead; a moving rod crossing it reintroduces a moving seal.

There is no added local membrane stiffness/hysteresis, but a flooded well can create hydrostatic load, viscous drag, trapped-gas pressure and deposits. The **complete wet assembly** must still satisfy the inherited 10 mN parasitic development screen and the separate main-stage force/rocking budget. If it cannot, reject the concept. R9's bolted 316L candidate has exposed fasteners and narrow passages; a wet implementation needs a crevice/cleaning and hot-preload review. R9 dry thermal results do not transfer to a wet/revised cartridge.

Public manufacturer evidence supports a feasibility inquiry only. CeramTec describes custom electrically insulated ceramic-to-metal hermetic feedthroughs, a family range of −269 to 450°C and welded/brazed mounting with helium leak checks. It supplies no selected four-wire part fitting this housing, RTD lead joint or aged hot-wet result. [CeramTec Ceramaseal page, accessed 2026-10-05](https://www.ceramtec-industrial.com/en/products-applications/hermetically-sealed-components/ceramaseal-feedthroughs). A tempting Conax TGF assembly allows 482°C *process-wire* exposure but describes only **93.3°C vessel-wall** temperature and standard Viton sealant: it does not qualify an installed 250°C boundary. [Conax TGF page, accessed 2026-10-05](https://www.conaxtechnologies.com/product/conax-high-temperature-instrumentation-wire-compression-seal-feedthrough-tgf/).

For the *static* glass/housing joint, Kalrez 7375 is a named specimen material only. Its maker lists 300°C maximum application temperature and 70 h/260°C compression-set data on size-214 O-rings, with hot-water/steam claims. No Ø18 mm glass gland, compression-stop, cycling or cooker leakage result follows. [Qnity Kalrez 7375 data, accessed 2026-10-05](https://www.qnityelectronics.com/products/kalrez-7375.html). Actual land temperature, hot/cold compression, glass strain, set/reassembly and cleaning chemistry remain unknown. An all-metal static joint is another branch but may load glass. R8's 6375 and custom edge-welded metal membrane remain moving-boundary feasibility branches; the latter's supplier warns lateral offset degrades life and supplies no miniature installed force curve. [Senior Metal Bellows design guide, accessed 2026-10-05](https://www.metalbellows.com/bellows-101/).

**Experimental build order:** open inspectable wet-well coupon and independent drainage/guide-force measurement; stationary four-conductor *dummy* bulkhead feedthrough plus glass surrogate and leak tracer; dry window/optical path; only then integrate R9 cartridge or actual RTD leads. The clamp needs positive compression datums/stops, not torque-as-gland-height. Record material/lot, finish/burrs, as-built gap, fastener torque and reassembly position. This is an unexecuted process order. Build volume and manufacturing capability/cost are undefined.

## Contact observability

R5/R8 downstream witnesses can yield identical readings in different states: **A:** pan presses a free cap; **B:** pan is removed while a jam holds carrier/island position and spring-seat reaction at the same values. Fresh timestamps do not distinguish these states. After LOADED acquisition, the current guard remains valid in B if a driver continues reporting LOADED. R5's retract challenge rejects some rigid jams, but a compliant jam can mimic release/reload. Its AC cap-to-clipped-pan test applies only to controlled bare-metal coupons. A conductive whisker can satisfy an electrical gate with negligible heat flow; enamel can provide useful contact without electrical continuity. These are R5 counterexamples, not firmware bugs.

Use three independent **bench ground truths at the cap location**: (1) external force in the pan-to-cap load path with separate glass-support reaction; (2) side optical/section metrology of actual local pan–cap gap, rather than commanded pan height; (3) pan-bottom and cap/RTD thermal references for effective conductance. Log intended detector channels simultaneously but do not use them as truth. Exercise dry contact, jammed gap with matching downstream readings, force-carrying insulating film, conductive sliver, tilted/warped pan supported by glass, seal drag, wet bridge, loss after acquisition and later induction EMI. A mechanical force pass with unacceptable thermal conductance is a fail datum.

The product detector candidate is a **tip-reaction sensor mechanically upstream of every guide, spring, stop and seal reaction, plus an independent same-region gap/pan-surface channel**. This is a load-path requirement, not a proven package. A spring-seat sensor sits downstream and fails the jam discriminator. Any optical gap channel must reject dirty/opaque/reflective invalid returns; an annular measurement extrapolated beneath a warped pan is insufficient unless its error is bounded. Its 250°C protection, lead force, heat shunt, EMI and fit in a D6 cap remain unknown. If no buildable same-region observable and conductance criterion survive the allowed cookware range, keep the backend unavailable or explicitly narrow that range. Do not infer LOADED from RTD trend or induction pan presence.

## Existing firmware contract and discriminator vectors

Read-only code: firmware/main/main.c:44–79 runs a 10 ms control task and has **no detector backend**. firmware/main/contact_guard.h:7–27 and contact_guard.c:19–69 require 100 ms continuously sampled RELEASED before 100 ms LOADED acquisition; sample age and intersample gap must be strictly under 50 ms. Same-timestamp replay cannot refresh evidence. RELEASED, FAULT, UNAVAILABLE or stale input revokes without loss debounce. firmware/main/state_machine.c:230–238,364–374 gates entry and each update of PAN_DET, PREHEAT and HEATING; PAN_DET's 5% excitation cannot acquire contact. Its lines 103–127 invoke hardware cut and latch the fault. Host tests exercise software commands, not physical gate waveforms.

| Physical/input history | Driver obligation | Guard result / missing evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Boot empty, unloaded return ≥100 ms; real loaded contact ≥100 ms with fresh independent evidence | RELEASED then LOADED | May qualify; threshold distributions and hardware timing UNKNOWN. Pan already depressing probe at boot cannot skip release proof. |
| Acquired; pan lifts; tip force or same-region gap crosses validated loss boundary | RELEASED or FAULT on first diagnosed result | Guard revokes on next control update; sensing and physical gate delay UNKNOWN. |
| Acquired; pan lifts but lower force/positions remain loaded through jam | FAULT/UNAVAILABLE from independent local channels | A bad driver reporting LOADED fools the existing guard. This is the mandatory hardware discriminator. |
| Acquired; no fresh samples | Never renew cached timestamp | Guard invalid at age ≥50 ms; with an on-time 10 ms task, software checks within <60 ms of last sample. This is **not** a physical latency bound. |
| Sensor open/short/saturated, obscured view, contradictory same-time samples, EMI-corrupted diagnosis | FAULT/UNAVAILABLE | Software reset/fault at next update; actual diagnostic coverage UNKNOWN. |
| Force and gap indicate contact but thermal reference shows bad coupling due to film/sliver | Withhold product LOADED until thermal criterion and cookware envelope exist | Mechanical touch alone does not qualify temperature control. |

Maximum allowable **physical** loss-to-output-disable latency, applied heating energy, duty/overshoot, leak rate, drain overflow capacity, cleaning chemistry and service/cycle life are **UNKNOWN product requirements**. The 100/50/10 ms values are engineering budgets, not safety limits. The external watchdog's 1.6 s timeout is a separate stalled-task layer, not a 50 ms contact-cut guarantee. Before enabling hardware, capture one synchronized trace containing independent gap/force/temperature truth, raw detector channels and timestamps, guard state, command, actual gate voltage/current and coil power under worst-case CPU load and induction EMI.

Advance the **static-bulkhead wet-well coupon** and **tip-reaction plus same-region gap coupon** to drawing/fixture review. Compare measured wet-well force/leak/cleaning data with a supplier-designed membrane, not a material rating. If the static architecture violates cooker ingress/cleaning needs, or if no independent observable survives jam/contamination, record an explicit architecture/product requirement change rather than weakening firmware debounce.
