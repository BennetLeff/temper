# IEC 60335-2-6 cl. 29.2: the condition is a *disjunction with an outcome test*, not a sealing requirement. PD3 is nevertheless correct — but PD3 does **not** imply 12.6 mm, and the 12.6 mm figure rests on an unexamined working-voltage row inherited from an abandoned 230 V design

## Verdict up front

**The clause imposes no sealing, gasketing, ingress-rating or airflow-exclusion
requirement.** Read verbatim, it makes PD3 the default and offers **two alternative
means** — the insulation being *enclosed*, **or** the insulation being *located* — each
qualified by a single functional outcome test: that the insulation is **"unlikely to be
exposed to pollution during normal use of the appliance."** The words "sealed",
"gasketed", "hermetic", "IP", and "compartment" do not appear in it.

**The headline, because it is not what the task expected and it matters more than the
pollution degree does:** the task framed this as a PD2-vs-PD3 question where PD2 → 8.0 mm
and PD3 → 12.6 mm. **That framing is itself wrong.** 8.0 mm is reachable *at PD3* — it is
Table 17 **row iii** (>125–250 V), PD3, group IIIa/IIIb = 4.0 mm basic, doubled. The
12.6 mm figure requires **row iv** (>250–400 V), and this is a **120 V** appliance whose
mains↔SELV working voltage is 120–132 V and whose DC bus is ~170 V — both row iii. The
row-iv selection is a fossil of an abandoned 230 V design (230 V×√2 = 325 V *does* land
in row iv), still visible in `pcb_spec.yaml`'s `mains_voltage_v: 230.0`. **This
repository already established that, in an evidence document dated 2026-08-13 that
nothing in the tree references and that the 2026-08-15 decision never cites.** See §5 —
it is the single highest-leverage open question in this file, and it dissolves all three
of the task's "measured facts" without needing PD2 at all.

**On the pollution degree itself, the project's reading is wrong in the way the task
suspected — but not in a way that changes the number.** Two findings, pointing in
opposite directions; both reported because both are true:

1. **The mechanism in-tree encodes a stricter test than the standard's.**
   `packages/temper-design-bundle/src/enclosure.rs` (on
   `feat/enclosure-declaration-derives-pd`) computes
   `qualifies_for_pd2_exception() = sealed && gasketed && outside_forced_air_path`.
   That three-way conjunction is the project's own construction. The standard offers a
   two-way *disjunction* over means, judged by outcome. And IEC 60335-1 Annex M — the
   annex clause 29.2 itself points to — lists **"enclosures, encapsulation or hermetic
   sealing"** as three *distinct* means, which is direct primary-text evidence that
   "enclosed" does not mean "hermetically sealed". The gate implements a **sufficient**
   condition and treats it as the **necessary** one. The thermal analysis that found
   PD2 "unviable" was therefore answering a question the standard never asked: it
   modelled a fully sealed box only, and explicitly put every vented or relocated
   arrangement out of scope.

2. **Correcting the reading does not rescue this design.** The outcome test is
   "unlikely to be exposed to pollution during normal use", and this design's *own
   specified* construction routes **unfiltered kitchen intake air by forced convection
   across the cavity the PCB occupies**, through a coil bracket that is an
   air-permeable baffle by design, with no filtration specified anywhere. Under any
   reading of the clause — strict or generous — insulation deliberately placed in the
   grease/steam/aerosol path of a cooking appliance is not "unlikely to be exposed to
   pollution." **PD3 governs the design as specified.**

So: **PD3 is right, and the pollution-degree route to 8.0 mm is genuinely closed.** The
useful consequences are two, and neither is "relax the bar today":

- **The route to PD2 is much wider than this project believed, and the widest part of it
  has never been evaluated**: the clause is satisfied by *location* as readily as by
  *enclosure*, and a non-hermetic barrier is explicitly contemplated by Annex M. Nobody
  has asked what it would take to put the board out of the pollution path. The
  sealed-box thermal dead-end was self-inflicted by an over-strict reading.
- **PD3 does not mean 12.6 mm.** Pollution degree is only one of the three indices
  cl. 29.2 names. The working-voltage row is a second, it was never derived for this
  120 V design, and it is worth more millimetres than the pollution degree is. **§5.**

**One genuine headwind against any PD2 claim for this appliance class**, which must be
stated because it cuts against the convenient answer: Annex M warns that such means
"may not be effective ... **if in normal use, it generates pollutants itself.**" A
cooktop generates steam and grease aerosol in normal use by definition. That sentence
is very plausibly *why* Part 2-6 flips the default to PD3 for this class, and any PD2
argument has to survive it.

**One escape route checked and closed.** This is a *portable countertop* appliance (a
repurposed RCA 12A3 chassis), and IEC 60335-2-6 is titled "***stationary*** cooking ranges,
hobs, ovens" and explicitly routes portable cooking appliances to **60335-2-9**. So the
governing particular standard may well be 2-9, not 2-6. **It makes no difference:
60335-2-9 cl. 29.2's Addition is character-for-character identical** (§1.4, read from
primary text this session). Two particular standards, two appliance categories, two IEC
base years, one sentence — which also makes it markedly more likely the wording is stable
into current editions.

## 1. The clause text

### 1.1 IEC 60335-2-6 cl. 29.2 Addition — the operative clause — PRIMARY

Read from **IS 302-2-6:2009**, the Bureau of Indian Standards adoption, published under
India's Right to Information Act at
`https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/S05/is.302.2.6.2009.pdf` (950,608 bytes, 25 pages,
sha256 `7819d570080c8266d486e69624af7fb676708a8322b9f23ccb30604fb5aa4aa0`).
This PDF **carries an embedded text layer**, so the quotation below is extracted text,
**not OCR** — a stronger read than the OCR-based route earlier sessions used. Extracted
**twice, with two different extraction algorithms** (`pdftotext -layout` and
`pdftotext -raw`), yielding identical wording; the clause sits at the boundary between
the clause-29 heading and the "29.3 Addition", exactly as a prior session reported.

> **29 CLEARANCES, CREEPAGE DISTANCES AND SOLID INSULATION**
>
> This clause of Part 1 is applicable except as follows:
>
> **29.2 Addition**
>
> The microenvironment is pollution degree 3 unless the insulation is enclosed or
> located so that it is unlikely to be exposed to pollution during normal use of the
> appliance.

**This reproduces, character for character, the text already quoted in-tree** at
`docs/evidence/2026-07-30-pollution-degree-determination.md:83` and
`docs/specs/HIGH_VOLTAGE_CLEARANCE_SPEC.md` §3.2.1. The in-tree quotation was correct.
What was wrong was never the quotation — it was what the project built on top of it.

**Adoption caveats, stated rather than glossed:**

- IS 302-2-6:2009's own foreword (PRIMARY, same file) says: *"This standard is based on
  IEC 60335-2-6 (2004) ... except for the following modification: a) The leakage current
  value is more stringent as compared to IEC Publication, b) Ambient test conditions are
  based on National conditions, and c) Schedule of type and acceptance test added."*
  **None of the three named modifications touches clause 29.** The 29.2 Addition is
  therefore very probably IEC-identical — but that is INFERRED from the absence of a
  declared deviation, not verified against IEC's own text.
- The base is **IEC 60335-2-6:2004**. The current edition is later. **Whether cl. 29.2's
  Addition was reworded in any subsequent edition or amendment is UNOBTAINABLE** — IEC's
  own text is paywalled and every preview reached this session and in prior sessions cuts
  off in front matter. This is a real gap and it sits under the whole analysis.

### 1.2 IEC 60335-1 cl. 29.2 — the clause it modifies — PRIMARY

Read from **IS 302-1:2008** (`https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/S05/is.302.1.2008.pdf`,
5,568,079 bytes, 80 PDF pages). This scan has **no usable text layer** (`pdftotext`
yields 2,105 bytes for the whole file) and `tesseract` is not installed in this
environment. It was therefore read by rendering pages to PNG at 110 dpi with `pdftoppm`
and **reading the page images directly**. Clause 29.2 is on **printed page 52**
(PDF page 57):

> **29.2** Appliances shall be constructed so that creepage distances are not less than
> those appropriate for the working voltage, taking into account the material group and
> the pollution degree.
>
> NOTE — The working voltage for parts connected to the neutral are the same as for
> parts connected to the phase.
>
> **Pollution degree 2 applies unless:**
>
> a) precautions have been taken to protect the insulation, in which case pollution
> degree 1 applies; and
>
> b) the insulation is subjected to conductive pollution, in which case pollution
> degree 3 applies.
>
> NOTE — An explanation of pollution degree is given in Annex M.

And, on the same page, the doubling rule the 12.6 mm figure depends on:

> **29.2.3** Creepage distances of reinforced insulation shall be at least double those
> specified for basic insulation in Table 17.

Also confirmed on the same page (PRIMARY), the material-group/CTI mapping used
downstream: *material group II: 400 ≤ CTI < 600; material group IIIa: 175 ≤ CTI < 400;
material group IIIb: 100 ≤ CTI < 175*. And on printed page 50: *"Appliances are in
overvoltage category II."*

**Note the structure.** Part 1's default is **PD2**. Part 2-6 does not merely add a
gloss — it *inverts the default* for this appliance class. IS 302-2-6's foreword
(PRIMARY) settles precedence: *"Should, however, any deviation exist between IS 302-1
(2008) and this standard, the provisions of the latter shall apply."* So for a hob, the
governing sentence is Part 2-6's, and the burden runs the other way from Part 1: PD3
unless the exception is earned.

### 1.3 IEC 60335-1 Annex M "Pollution degree" — the definitions — PRIMARY

**This is the piece nobody in this repository had read, and it is the piece that
decides the interpretive question.** Annex M is captioned *"(Clause 29.2)"* — it is the
annex clause 29.2 itself points to. Printed page 67 of IS 302-1:2008 (PDF page 72), read
from a 110 dpi page render. Annex M states it is *"extracted from IS 15382 (Part 1)"* —
the BIS adoption of **IEC 60664-1**, which is exactly the 60664-1 definition the task
asked for, reproduced inside the appliance standard.

> **ANNEX M** *(Clause 29.2)*
>
> **POLLUTION DEGREE**
>
> The following information on pollution degrees is extracted from IS 15382 (Part 1).
>
> **Pollution**
>
> The microenvironment determines the effect of pollution on the insulation. The
> macroenvironment, however, has to be taken into account when considering the
> microenvironment.
>
> **Means may be provided to reduce pollution at the insulation under consideration by
> effective use of enclosures, encapsulation or hermetic sealing. Such means to reduce
> pollution may not be effective when the equipment is subjected to condensation or if
> in normal use, it generates pollutants itself.**
>
> Small clearances can be bridged completely by solid particles, dust and water and
> therefore minimum clearances are specified where pollution may be present in the
> microenvironment.
>
> NOTES
> 1 Pollution will become conductive in the presence of humidity. Pollution caused by
> contaminated water, soot, metal or carbon dust is inherently conductive.
> 2 Conductive pollution by ionized gases and metallic depositions occur only in
> specific instances, for example in arc chambers of switchgear or controlgear and is
> not covered by IS 15382 (Part 1).
>
> **Degrees of Pollution in the Microenvironment**
>
> For the purpose of evaluating creepage distances, the following four degrees of
> pollution in the microenvironment are established:
>
> a) **pollution degree 1**: no pollution or only dry, non-conductive pollution occurs.
> The pollution has no influence;
>
> b) **pollution degree 2**: only non-conductive pollution occurs, except that
> occasionally a temporary conductivity caused by condensation is to be expected;
>
> c) **pollution degree 3**: conductive pollution occurs or dry non-conductive pollution
> occurs that becomes conductive due to condensation that is to be expected; and
>
> d) **pollution degree 4**: the pollution generates persistent conductivity caused by
> conductive dust or by rain or snow.
>
> NOTE — Pollution degree 4 is not applicable to appliances.

**The first sentence of Annex M is the answer to the task's framing question.** *"The
microenvironment determines the effect of pollution on the insulation. The
macroenvironment, however, has to be taken into account."* Pollution degree is a
property of the insulation's **microenvironment**, and the room is the
**macroenvironment** — which is not ignored, but is not the thing classified. The
task's hypothesis is confirmed from primary text.

### 1.4 Is 60335-2-6 even the right particular standard? — asked, and it does not matter

A peer session raised a real objection: this may not be a 60335-2-6 appliance at all. It
deserved checking, because the entire PD3 default hangs on which Part 2 applies. **It was
checked, and the answer is that the pollution degree is invariant.** Recording the whole
chain because the negative result is worth as much as a positive one.

**The objection has genuine force.** PRIMARY, from IS 302-2-6:2009's own foreword, the
IEC title is *"Particular requirements for **stationary** cooking ranges, hobs, ovens and
similar appliances."* And its scope clause 1 (PRIMARY, same file) routes portables
elsewhere:

> NOTE 101 — Examples of appliances that are within the scope of this standard are:
> a) griddles, b) grills, **c) induction hobs**, d) pyrolytic self-cleaning ovens, and
> e) steam ovens.
>
> NOTE 103 — This standard does not apply to: … **c) grills, toasters and similar
> portable cooking appliances (IS 302-2-9)** …

**And this design is a portable countertop appliance, not a stationary hob.** REPO: it is
built into *"the vintage RCA 12A3 chassis"* with *"the existing bottom vents of the RCA
12A3"* (`docs/CHASSIS_AIRFLOW_DESIGN.md:4,8`; `docs/ASSEMBLY_GUIDE.md:1,22` — "Remove all
original tube sockets and transformers"). That is a benchtop object with a single coil, an
80 mm fan and a line cord. So **IEC 60335-2-9** (*"electric **portable** appliances for
household purposes that have a cooking function"*, scope NOTE 101 including *"f)
hotplates"* and *"d) cookers"* — PRIMARY, IS 302-2-9:2009 cl. 1) is at least as good a
fit as 2-6, and arguably the better one.

**It changes nothing.** IS 302-2-9:2009 (based on **IEC 60335-2-9:2008**), fetched this
session from `https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/S05/is.302.2.9.2009.pdf` (954,358 bytes,
text layer, `pdftotext -layout`), clause 29:

> **29 CLEARANCES, CREEPAGE DISTANCES AND SOLID INSULATION**
>
> This clause of Part 1 is applicable except as follows.
>
> **29.2 Addition**
>
> The microenvironment is pollution degree 3 unless the insulation is enclosed or
> located so that it is unlikely to be exposed to pollution during normal use of the
> appliance.

**Character for character identical to 60335-2-6's.** Two different particular standards,
two different appliance categories, two different IEC base years (2004 and 2008), the same
sentence. The 2-6-vs-2-9 fork is therefore **moot for pollution degree**, and this route
is closed.

Two things follow, both of which strengthen §1.1 rather than weakening it:

1. **The wording is a stable horizontal position across the 60335-2-x cooking family**,
   not an artifact of one committee or one edition. That materially raises confidence that
   the sentence survives into current IEC editions — still INFERRED, since IEC's own text
   remains unobtainable (§7), but it is a second independent instance now, from a 2008
   base rather than a 2004 one.
2. **It also independently corroborates that the BIS text reproduces IEC faithfully** on
   this clause, since two separately-adopted BIS documents converge on identical wording.

**Genuine defect noticed on the way, flagged not fixed:** `docs/REGULATORY_COMPLIANCE.md:26`
names **UL 858 "Household Electric Ranges"** as *"Primary safety standard for USA."* UL 858
is the *range* standard. For a portable countertop appliance that is very probably the
wrong US standard — a peer's directory sweep found US-listed 120 V countertop induction
splitting between **UL 1026** (household portable) and **UL 197** (commercial), with UL 858
appearing only on 240 V built-ins. **Not independently verified by me** (see §6.4), but the
form-factor mismatch is visible from the repo alone and someone should resolve it.

## 2. What the condition actually requires

### 2.1 Parsing the sentence

> "The microenvironment is pollution degree 3 **unless** the insulation is **[a]
> enclosed** **or** **[b] located** **so that it is unlikely to be exposed to pollution
> during normal use of the appliance.**"

- **Default:** PD3. The burden is on the design to earn anything better.
- **Two alternative means**, joined by *or*: enclosure, **or** location. Either suffices.
- **One outcome test**, governing both: *unlikely to be exposed to pollution during
  normal use of the appliance*. The clause is written as a purposive test — the means
  are named, the *outcome* is what is required.
- **It does not say PD2.** It says the microenvironment is not PD3. Which of PD1/PD2
  then applies falls back to Part 1 cl. 29.2 and Annex M: PD1 only where "precautions
  have been taken to protect the insulation" in the Annex-J/60664-3 coating sense; PD2
  otherwise. So clearing the 2-6 condition lands on PD2, not PD1. **INFERRED** from the
  two clause texts read together — the Addition itself is silent on which degree
  replaces PD3.

### 2.2 What "pollution" means here, and what the PD2/PD3 line actually is

From Annex M (PRIMARY), the discriminator between PD2 and PD3 is **conductivity**, not
quantity:

- **PD2** tolerates non-conductive pollution, plus *occasional temporary* conductivity
  from condensation.
- **PD3** is triggered by *either* conductive pollution occurring, *or* non-conductive
  pollution that becomes conductive because condensation "is to be expected."

Annex M Note 1 makes this a low bar to trip in a kitchen: *"Pollution will become
conductive in the presence of humidity. Pollution caused by contaminated water, soot,
metal or carbon dust is inherently conductive."* Boil-over of salted or starchy water is
contaminated water. Cooking soot is named outright.

### 2.3 The checkable criteria

Someone could check a design against the clause with these. They are derived from the
clause plus Annex M and are labelled as such — **the standard supplies no numeric
threshold, no IP rating, no gasket specification and no test method for this clause.**
Cl. 29.2's own compliance statement is *"Compliance is checked by measurement"* — that
measures the resulting creepage, not the pollution-degree selection. **Selecting the
pollution degree is a construction judgement, and on a certified product it is the
certifying body's judgement.** That is a real limit on how far any in-repo analysis can
go.

| # | Criterion | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Is there a barrier between the insulation and cooking-generated pollution? It **need not be hermetic** — Annex M names "enclosures" separately from "hermetic sealing". | PRIMARY (2-6 29.2; Annex M) |
| C2 | *Alternatively*, is the insulation **positioned** out of the paths pollution travels — boil-over/spill run-down, steam plume, grease aerosol, settling dust, and any airflow carrying them? Location alone suffices. | PRIMARY (2-6 29.2) |
| C3 | Is the pollution that does reach the insulation **non-conductive**, and does it stay non-conductive? Contaminated water and soot are inherently conductive. | PRIMARY (Annex M b/c, Note 1) |
| C4 | Is condensation on the insulation more than "occasional and temporary"? If condensation on polluted surfaces "is to be expected", PD3 regardless of the barrier. | PRIMARY (Annex M b/c) |
| C5 | Does the appliance **generate pollutants itself in normal use**, such that the means is not effective? Explicitly flagged by Annex M and squarely applicable to a cooktop. | PRIMARY (Annex M) |
| C6 | Is the judgement made about the **microenvironment of that specific insulation**, with the macroenvironment taken into account — not about the room? | PRIMARY (Annex M) |

**What is NOT required, on the primary text:** sealing; a gasket; any IP rating;
exclusion from a forced-air path as such; a partition; a documented compartment; a
manufacturing inspection criterion. Every one of those appears in this repository's
gates and specifications as though it came from the standard. **None of them is in the
clause.** They are reasonable engineering *evidence* that C1–C5 hold; they are not the
requirement, and treating them as the requirement is what closed off the design space.

## 3. Where this project's reading diverges from the text

### 3.1 The conjunction — REPO

`packages/temper-design-bundle/src/enclosure.rs`, on
`feat/enclosure-declaration-derives-pd` (commit `94c74c67`), the whole classification:

```
EnclosureFacts::qualifies_for_pd2_exception() = self.sealed && self.gasketed && self.outside_forced_air_path
```

and `elec/enclosure_manifest.yaml`'s header, which states the interpretation in prose:

> "IEC 60335-2-6 cl. 29.2 Addition: PD2 is a conditional exception **requiring a sealed
> compartment**; PD3 is the default for cooking appliances. PD2 is selected only when
> **sealed AND gasketed AND outside_forced_air_path** all hold."

**Compare the clause: "enclosed *or* located so that it is unlikely to be exposed to
pollution during normal use."** The manifest converts a disjunction of two means into a
conjunction of three project-invented facts, and replaces the outcome test with a
construction checklist. `scripts/check_pd2_compartment_evidence.py` goes further,
requiring a cover part number with three positive dimensions, a gasket part number with
perimeter and compression in millimetres, a named KiCad keepout zone that must resolve
in `pcb/temper.kicad_pcb`, and a literal `airflow_routing.duct_crosses_pcb_cavity:
false`. **None of that traces to the clause.**

### 3.2 Why this matters — the sealed-box dead end was self-inflicted

`docs/evidence/2026-07-30-pcb-compartment-thermal-bound.md` states its own scope
(REPO): *"if the PCB compartment were **walled off** from the forced-air duct, would it
overheat?"* — modelling natural convection and radiation off a **"fully sealed box with
no forced mixing"**. Its §4 says, verbatim: *"Explicitly **not proposed**: any vent,
gap, or airflow path between the compartment and the duct — that would reopen the exact
exposure the compartment exists to close, and is out of scope per the task brief."*

That exclusion is only correct if the clause requires sealing. **It does not.** The
verdict "marginal — not comfortably viable" is a verdict about a hermetic box, and a
hermetic box is one of *three* means Annex M names, and enclosure is one of *two* means
the clause names. **The entire non-sealed design space — a non-hermetic barrier, a
baffle, a relocation of the board out of the intake path, ducting the cooling air over
the heatsink only — is unevaluated.** `docs/brainstorms/2026-07-30-pcb-compartment-
pd2-partition-design.md` similarly only ever considers sealed partitions, and correctly
flags its own hardest problem (a conformal gasket around four heatsink-straddling
packages) as *"the single weakest point in the pollution-exclusion argument"* — a
problem that exists *because* sealing was assumed mandatory.

**This is the correction the task was looking for, and it is real.** But it changes the
*option space*, not today's number — see §4.

## 4. Does this design meet the condition?

Assessed against C1–C6. The design is read from committed files; nothing is built, so
much of this is necessarily about what is *specified*.

### 4.1 NOT MET — on the design's own specified construction

These are not "undetermined pending an enclosure." They are affirmative statements in
committed design documents that place the insulation *in* the pollution path.

| Finding | Evidence (REPO) |
|---|---|
| **Unfiltered kitchen air is forced across the PCB cavity by design.** Fails C2 directly, and C5. | `docs/CHASSIS_AIRFLOW_DESIGN.md` §3.2: `Cool Air (Bottom Vents) → [ Intake Plenum ] → [ 80mm PWM Fan ] → [ Transition Duct ] → [ IGBT Heatsink ] → [ Exhaust Vent ]`, routed across the cavity the PCB occupies. |
| **No filtration anywhere in the intake path.** | No filter element appears in `CHASSIS_AIRFLOW_DESIGN.md` §2 requirements, §4 fan selection, or §7 BOM. The only "Gasket" line item seals the *duct joint*. |
| **The coil bracket directly above the board is an air-permeable baffle by design.** | `docs/COIL_BRACKET_DESIGN.md` §4: *"Large triangular cutouts around the central coil ring allow air from the bottom intake to flow directly through the Litz wire strands."* |
| **No barrier of any kind is committed.** Fails C1. | No cover, gasket, partition, or keepout geometry anywhere in-tree; `pcb/temper.kicad_pcb`'s only `Edge.Cuts` polygon is a plain rectangle. PCB mounts on 4× M3 standoffs in an open chassis (`docs/specs/PCB_SPECIFICATION.md` §2.2). `docs/specs/pd2_compartment_evidence.yaml` does not exist. |
| **The appliance generates pollutants in normal use.** Fails C5 — the Annex M caveat applies on its face to a cooktop. | Appliance class; `docs/ENVIRONMENTAL_SPEC.md` §1 declares "indoor household use in a kitchen environment". |

**Conclusion: the design as specified fails the condition under the corrected,
*generous* reading, not merely under the project's strict one.** This is the load-bearing
point for the decision the task is feeding: adopting the correct reading of the clause
does **not** move this board to 8.0 mm. The reinforced bar stays at **12.6 mm**.

### 4.2 UNDETERMINED — until a mechanical enclosure is actually specified

| Open question | Why it is open |
|---|---|
| **Whether a non-hermetic barrier or a relocation would satisfy C1/C2.** | Never evaluated. The only two mechanical studies in-tree (`2026-07-30-pcb-compartment-thermal-bound.md`, `2026-07-30-...-partition-design.md`) both restrict themselves to sealed constructions by explicit scope decision. This is the single largest unexplored option. |

### 6.5 Does the reinforced requirement exist at all? The capacitive-touch question — PRIMARY

A peer read three **filed FCC schematics** for 120 V-class induction cooktops (ZFBC13F,
ZBNC18-13, ZBNTI3B) and reports **all three are non-isolated**: control side referenced to
the rectified-mains negative rail, direct BJT totem-pole gate drive on a common ground,
bare resistive bus and current sensing into the MCU, and aux "flybacks" whose secondary
return ties to the primary switch node. User safety comes from **capacitive touch through
the panel** or membrane switches under an overlay — no conductive path to the user.
*(Second-hand; I did not retrieve these exhibits — see §6.2 for why. The architectural
claim is the peer's, the clause analysis below is mine and is from primary text.)*

The coordinator asked whether 60335-1/-2-6 treats such an interface as removing the
accessible-part requirement that drives reinforced insulation. **It does — and the
requirement does not disappear, it relocates.** The chain, all PRIMARY from IS 302-1:2008
page renders read this session:

**3.6.3 Accessible Part** (printed p.5) — *"Part or surface that can be touched by means of
test probe B of IS 1401, and if the part or surface is metal, any conductive part connected
to it."*

**3.6.4 Live Part** (printed p.5), NOTE 1 — *"Parts, accessible or not, complying with 8.1.4
are not considered to be live parts."*

**8.1.4** (printed p.12) — *"An accessible part is not considered to be live if: a) the part
is supplied at safety extra-low voltage, provided that: 1) for ac, the peak value of the
voltage does not exceed 42.4 V; and 2) for dc, the voltage does not exceed 42.4 V. or b) the
part is separated from live parts by protective impedance."*

**8.2** (printed p.12) — **this is the clause that actually commands reinforced insulation**:

> Class II appliances and Class II constructions shall be constructed and enclosed so that
> there is adequate protection against accidental contact with basic insulation and metal
> parts separated from live parts by basic insulation only.
>
> **It shall only be possible to touch parts which are separated from live parts by double
> insulation or reinforced insulation.**
>
> Compliance is checked by inspection and by applying test probe B of IS 1401 …

**So the trigger is probe-B touchability, not "there is a user interface".** A solid
non-conductive panel with sensing pads behind it presents **no touchable conductive part**:
3.6.3 is not satisfied, so there is no accessible part to protect, and — if the whole
control circuit is mains-referenced — there is no SELV/PELV circuit for anything to be
separated *from*. The mains↔SELV creepage barrier is then absent **because its precondition
is absent**, not because it was waived.

**But the insulation obligation moves rather than vanishing**, and this is the part that
must not be oversold:

- The **panel itself becomes the double/reinforced insulation** demanded by 8.2. That is a
  **solid-insulation** question — cl. **29.3** (*"Supplementary insulation and reinforced
  insulation shall have adequate thickness, or have a sufficient number of layers…"*,
  PRIMARY, printed p.52) plus cl. 8, 21 and 30 — **not a cl. 29.2 creepage question.** The
  problem converts from a 2-D surface-distance problem into a thickness/dielectric one.
  It does not evaporate; it changes discipline.
- **This design is Class I, not Class II.** `elec/src/main.ato:753` bonds `gnd ~ pe` (REPO,
  via `2026-08-13-mains-selv-barrier-derivation.md` §1), and the chassis is a **metal** RCA
  12A3. For Class I, accessible metal is earthed under cl. 27, and live parts still need
  **basic** insulation to that earthed metal — which is still a **cl. 29.2 creepage
  requirement at mains working voltage and PD3**, just not doubled. On the §5.2 table that
  is Table 17 row iii, PD3, IIIa/IIIb = **4.0 mm basic**, undoubled.

**Net:** going non-isolated would remove the mains↔PELV *reinforced* crossing entirely, but
it would **not** remove clause 29.2, and it would not remove PD3. It would replace a 12.6 mm
(or 8.0 mm, §5) reinforced bar with a 4.0 mm basic bar to earthed metal — plus a solid-
insulation case for the panel. **That is a large win if it holds, and it is an architectural
decision far outside this document's authority.**

### 6.6 Does any of this depend on the barrier being intra-board? — No

Asked directly, because the coordinator flagged the premise as now in question.

**Cl. 29.2 governs the microenvironment of *insulation*, wherever that insulation physically
sits.** Annex M's first sentence (PRIMARY, §1.3) — *"The microenvironment determines the
effect of pollution on the insulation"* — is indifferent to how many PCBs the product has.
Splitting the ESP32 onto a second board does not change the pollution degree of anything; it
changes **whether a given creepage path exists at all**. Likewise §5's working-voltage
finding is a property of the *crossing*, not of the board count.

So: **every conclusion in §1–§5 is independent of the single-board premise.** If the
architecture changes, §1–§4 still tell you the pollution degree of whatever barrier survives,
and §5 still tells you the row. What the architecture question changes is *how many barriers
there are to apply them to* — which is exactly why it may be worth more than this document
is. It is not in tension with anything here.

### 6.7 The panel route is authorised by primary text — and here are its numbers

§6.5 argued from clause 3.6.3/8.1.4/8.2 that a non-conductive panel removes the
*accessible conductive part* and so removes the barrier's precondition. **That argument
can now be made positively, from clauses that address the construction directly.** All
four items below were read by me this session from the two BIS PDFs already in hand.

**1. The standard explicitly contemplates the hob glass as the protection against live
parts** — IEC 60335-2-6 cl. **7.12 Addition** (PRIMARY, IS 302-2-6:2009):

> If the hob surface is of glass-ceramic or similar material **and protects live parts**,
> the instructions shall include the substance of the following:
> WARNING — If the surface is cracked, switch off the appliance to avoid the possibility
> of electric shock.

This is a marking clause, so it *presupposes* the construction rather than authorising it —
but a standard does not write a cracked-glass shock warning for a construction it does not
expect.

**2. The construction is verified by impact plus a deliberate conductive-pollution event** —
cl. **21.102** (PRIMARY, IS 302-2-6:2009): a vessel with a flat copper/aluminium base
120 ± 10 mm across, loaded to **1.80 kg ± 0.01 kg**, is *"dropped flat 10 times from a
height of 150 mm onto the cooking zone."* Then:

> A quantity of 1 (+0.1/0) litre of water having a temperature of 20 °C ± 5 °C and
> containing **approximately 1 percent NaCl** is poured steadily over the hob surface. …
> The same quantity of the saline solution is poured over the hob surface after which
> excess water is removed again.
>
> **The hob surface shall not crack and the appliance shall withstand the electric strength
> test of 16.3.**

**Worth pausing on.** That saline pour is exactly the conductive-pollution hazard that
drives the PD3 default in cl. 29.2 — and the standard's answer for the hob surface is not
more creepage. It is *an unbroken solid dielectric, proven by impact and then by dielectric
withstand.* Two different clauses, two different instruments, same hazard.

**3. The numbers for solid insulation** — cl. **29.3.1 / 29.3.2** (PRIMARY, IS 302-1:2008
printed p.54):

> **29.3.1** The thickness of the insulation shall be at least: a) 1 mm for supplementary
> insulation; and b) **2 mm for reinforced insulation.**
>
> **29.3.2** Each layer of material shall withstand the electric strength test of **16.3**
> … Supplementary insulation shall consist of at least 2 layers of material and reinforced
> insulation of **at least 3 layers**.

**So the reinforced figure in the panel architecture is 2 mm of solid thickness (or ≥3
qualifying layers) — against 12.6 mm of PCB creepage in this one.** Both are "reinforced
insulation" under the same clause 29; they are simply different sub-clauses because they are
different physics.

**4. And on glass, creepage collapses to clearance anyway** — Table 17 **Note 2** (PRIMARY,
§5.2): *"For glass, ceramics and other inorganic insulating materials that do not track,
creepage distances need not be greater than the associated clearance."* The tracking
mechanism that creepage exists to resist does not occur on non-tracking inorganics. **This
does not extend to FR-4**, which tracks — it is why the PCB cannot borrow the concession.

**The caveat that decides it, and it is not a small one.** All of this holds only while
**nothing user-accessible is conductive**. The moment a reachable conductive part exists —
a temperature-probe jack, a USB connector, exposed earthed chassis metal, an antenna — there
is a genuine accessible conductive domain, it must be SELV, and the reinforced barrier
returns as a real isolation requirement. **Two facts about *this* design bear directly on
that and neither is favourable:** it is **Class I with `gnd ~ pe` and a metal RCA 12A3
chassis** (§6.5), and it carries an RTD sense chain whose user-accessibility has not been
established here. **Whether anything conductive reaches the user is now the single question
that decides which architecture this project is in**, and it is a design question, not a
standards one.

## 7. Limits of this analysis, stated before the convenient parts

- **The current IEC edition is UNOBTAINABLE.** Everything above rests on BIS adoptions of
  IEC 60335-1 (2008-era, Ed. 4.2 vintage) and IEC 60335-2-6:2004. IEC's own current text
  is paywalled; every preview route tried this session and in prior sessions
  (`docs/evidence/2026-08-13-annex-l-and-ekmq-pulse-current-acquisition.md`) terminates
  in front matter. Later editions may have reworded cl. 29.2. Not reconstructed.
- **IEC 60664-1 itself was not read.** Annex M states it is *extracted from* IS 15382
  (Part 1) = IEC 60664-1, and that extraction is what was read. The parent standard's
  fuller treatment (including any worked guidance on what "enclosed" buys) was not
  reached. IEC 60664-4 likewise remains unobtainable, as the enclosure manifest already
  records.
- **The pollution-degree selection is a certifying body's judgement.** Cl. 29.2's
  "compliance is checked by measurement" governs the creepage measurement, not the
  classification. No amount of in-repo analysis converts an interpretation into a
  determination. `docs/brainstorms/2026-07-30-pcb-compartment-pd2-partition-design.md`
  already flags this as *"a genuine interpretive judgment call, not a settled fact"*, and
  that remains correct.
- **§5 is a fork, not a determination.** It establishes that the enforced row was never
  derived and is contradicted for the mains nets by an in-tree determination. It does
  **not** establish that 8.0 mm is the answer: cl. 3.1.3's definition body is unrecovered,
  the rating-plate voltage is undecided, the row was never worked per crossing, and a
  tank-referenced crossing could need *more* than 12.6 mm. Anyone acting on §5 must do
  the per-site derivation first.
- **US national differences are unread.** Whether UL/CSA 60335-2-6 modifies cl. 29.2 for
  the actual target market is unknown (§6.2). That could change the analysis at the root.
- **This document changes nothing.** `MIN_BARRIER_WIDTH_MM` remains 12.6;
  `HV_CREEPAGE_ENFORCED_MM` remains `HV_CREEPAGE_PD3_MM`; no DRC rule, DRU, ratchet,
  oracle, threshold, config or board file was modified. `pcb/temper.kicad_pcb` sha256
  verified identical before and after (`26981fea…c110b`). **PD3 = 12.6 mm remains
  enforced regardless of anything above**, exactly as the task required.

In priority order, and none of it is "relax the bar":

1. **Derive the working-voltage row per reinforced crossing** (§5.5 item 2). Highest
   leverage, needs no standards purchase for the row arithmetic itself, and the in-tree
   `2026-08-13-mains-selv-barrier-derivation.md` is most of the work already. Read that
   document first — it is on `main` and nothing cites it.
2. **Obtain IEC 60335-1 cl. 3.1.3's definition body and the rated-voltage-range clause**,
   and decide the rating-plate voltage. This is a purchase or a lab question, not a
   reconstruction. It decides row ii vs row iii.
3. **Read the UL/CSA 60335-2-6 national differences** for cl. 29.2 (§6.2).
4. **Specify a laminate with a real CTI.** A third unaudited index, currently at its
   worst-case assumption by default.
5. **If PD2 is still wanted, re-pose the mechanical question correctly** — "can the board
   be *located* out of the pollution path, or given a non-hermetic barrier?" — rather than
   "can we seal and gasket a compartment?" The existing thermal study answers only the
   latter and should not be cited against the former.
6. **Correct `pcb_spec.yaml`'s `mains_voltage_v: 230.0`** (and its known-red
   `pollution_degree: 2`). The 230 V fossil is the likely origin of the row-iv selection
   and it is still sitting in a live config.
7. **Decide the isolation architecture before spending anything more on the creepage
   figure** (§6.2, §6.5). Three filed FCC schematics for 120 V-class induction reportedly
   show **no isolation at all** — mains-referenced control, capacitive touch through the
   panel — and the Traeger/Vollrath unit appears to isolate at a **board-to-board**
   boundary. Verify those exhibits first-hand (I could not), then ask whether this
   project's single-board reinforced barrier is a self-imposed problem. Per §6.5 this
   would **not** escape cl. 29.2 or PD3, but it would replace a reinforced bar with a
   **4.0 mm basic** bar to earthed metal plus a solid-insulation case for the panel.
   **This is the highest-value lead in the file and it is an architectural decision, not a
   standards one.** Note the design is currently **Class I** (`gnd ~ pe`, metal chassis),
   which is a different starting point from a Class II capacitive-touch product.
   **§6.7 now gives this route's primary-text basis and its actual numbers** — cl. 7.12,
   cl. 21.102's impact-plus-saline test, and cl. 29.3.1/29.3.2's **2 mm / ≥3 layers** for
   reinforced solid insulation, versus 12.6 mm of PCB creepage here.
8. **Answer one question first, because it decides everything in item 7: does anything
   user-accessible on this appliance conduct?** Probe jack, USB, exposed earthed chassis
   metal, the RTD sense chain. If **no**, the barrier can live in the panel and §6.7's
   figures apply. If **yes**, that domain must be SELV and the reinforced barrier is real —

   before it is.

   they are easier than PD3 until someone has read them.**
