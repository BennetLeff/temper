# The band is a router-vs-DRC clearance-model disagreement, exactly 0.05mm wide. Fixing it removes 90% of the track-to-track proximity — and does not move `clearance` at all, which is the finding that matters

> **Verdict up front.** The x[40,60) band is not congestion and not a search
> failure. It is **one arithmetic disagreement**: `router_v6/_pipeline_core.py`
> set the router's fallback clearance to **0.15mm** while the rule every one of
> those violations fires against — `generate_kicad_dru.py` RULE 10
> `"Default routing"` — grades at **0.2mm**. Stage 4's A\* reserves
> `trace_width + clearance` on a 0.1mm lattice, so two 0.25mm Default tracks
> landed at exactly 0.40mm centre-to-centre: an edge gap of **0.1500mm** against
> a 0.2000mm bar. **136 of the 505 clearance errors read `actual 0.1500 mm` to
> four decimal places, and another 149 read `0.1972mm`** — the same lattice
> step approached diagonally (4 cells across, 2 down: √(0.4²+0.2²) = 0.4472mm
> centre distance). Together **285 of 505 (56.4%) board-wide, and 165 of the
> band's 205 (80.5%)**. The band is a *parallel signal bus*, not a jam.
>
> The fix is real and it works at the mechanism level. Measured directly on
> board geometry, segment pairs closer than 0.2mm fall **1316 → 130 (−90%)**,
> and DRC track-to-track violations **407 → 135 (−67%)**.
>
> **And the `clearance` count does not move: 501 → 500.** Three independent
> repairs — each removing a different amount of the defect — land at 502, 500,
> 500 against the baseline's 501. What changes is *which* pairs violate:
> track-to-track collapses and pad-to-track rises 58 → 280, **170 of them at
> `actual 0.0000 mm`**, i.e. the plane-backbone MST driving copper straight
> through foreign pads at a hard-coded `OTHER_NET_CLEARANCE_MM = 0.05`, a third
> unfixed instance of the same defect class.
>
> So the honest answer to "resolve the band": **the band is resolved and named;
> the `clearance` regression is not, and is not resolvable by any routing
> parameter.** `clearance` on this placement has now been measured at ~500
> across five materially different copper realisations (2,514–4,497 segments).
> It behaves as a placement invariant, exactly as
> `docs/plans/2026-08-12-001` observed and `#1052` concluded.
>
> Heatsink co-location and the PD2/8.0mm isolation barrier are untouched and
> still hold — nothing here goes near the placer.

## 1. What the band actually is

Measured on the routed heatsink board (`drc_run0`, 505 clearance errors — the
top of the 499–505 range, chosen so nothing is hidden by taking a median).

**Location.** 32 occupied 20mm cells. The band is `x[40,60)`, holding **205
violations, 40.6%** of the board's total, concentrated in two adjacent cells:
`y[180,200)` 79 and `y[140,160)` 70.

**Rule.** 205 of 205 fire `'Default routing'`, `clearance 0.2000 mm`. Not one
netclass-specific rule appears in the band.

**Pair kinds.** Overwhelmingly track-involved, confirming the prior finding:

| pair kind | committed (386) | heatsink band (205) | heatsink board (505) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| track–track | 254 (65.8%) | **174 (84.9%)** | 407 (80.6%) |
| pad–track | 54 (14.0%) | 12 (5.9%) | 58 (11.5%) |
| PTH–track | 6 (1.6%) | 19 (9.3%) | 38 (7.5%) |
| pad–pad | 33 (8.5%) | **0** | 0 |

**Layer.** 350 of 410 band items are on **F.Cu**, 41 on B.Cu. Single-layer.

**Nets.** Two nets carry it. `safety.ovp.r_div_top1-p2` × `safety.ovp.r_div_top2-p2`
is **121 violations by itself**; `rtd_pan.low_window-out` × `safety.ovp.r_div_top2-p2`
adds 38. Only three components appear as pads at all (U11 ×8, SW1 ×3, R40 ×1).

That shape — two nets, one layer, no pads, one rule — is not a congested
region. It is **two traces running parallel down a channel, one lattice step
apart, for millimetres**. Example, straight from the board:

```

safety.ovp.r_div_top2-p2 : (48.75,140.75) -> (53.25,140.75)   w=0.25  4.50mm run
```

## 2. The mechanism, to four decimal places

### 2.1 What the DRC grades

`scripts/generate_kicad_dru.py` RULE 10:

```
(rule "Default routing"
   (condition "A.Type == 'Track' || B.Type == 'Track'")
   (constraint clearance (min 0.2mm)))
```

The condition is `||`. **Any** pair with a track on either side is graded at
0.2mm unless something stricter matches first. 503 of the board's 505 clearance
errors fire it.

### 2.2 What the router reserved

`router_v6/_pipeline_core.py`, added by `051152e7c` (2026-07-12) with the
comment *"lower default clearance to the SSOT Signal netclass (0.15mm)"*:

```python
dr.default_clearance_mm = 0.15
```

That is not an SSOT. `netclass_rules.yaml`'s `Signal` class has **no
counterpart in `pcb/temper.kicad_pro` at all**, and **0 of 684 board-net
occurrences resolve to it** (measured through `get_rules_for_net`). Three
declared sources say the unclassified floor is 0.2mm — `netclass_rules.yaml`'s
`default_clearance_mm`, `temper.kicad_pro`'s `Default` class, and RULE 10. The
0.15 was a global relaxation of the fallback, justified by a class nothing uses.

The commit's own measurement says why it looked good at the time: *"DRC 403 →
381, clearance −5, 0.15mm uses fewer blocked grid cells in A\* occupancy."* On
a 2,514-segment board a looser bar buys completion faster than it costs
violations. At 4,497 segments it inverts.

### 2.3 Why 0.05mm becomes 0.1500 and 0.1972

`OccupancyGrid.mark_path_blocked` → `temper_geometry::mark_segment_rect` is a
**rasteriser**:

```rust
let radius_mm = trace_width / 2.0 + clearance;
let expansion = ceil(radius_mm / cell_size);      // in CELLS
// stamps a SQUARE of ±expansion cells (Chebyshev) around each sampled point
```

The next net's centreline may therefore sit on the first cell centre outside
that square, at `(expansion + 1) * cell_size`. Probed against the real kernel
(`cell_size = 0.1mm`, the production `create_occupancy_grid` default):

| trace width | clearance | radius | expansion | resulting pitch | edge gap |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.25 | **0.15** | 0.2750 | 3 | 0.400mm | **0.1500** ❌ |
| 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.3250 | 4 | 0.500mm | 0.2500 |
| 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.3000 | 4 | 0.500mm | 0.3000 |
| 0.20 | 0.15 | 0.2500 | 3 | 0.400mm | 0.2000 ✅ |
| 0.127 | 0.10 | 0.1635 | 2 | 0.300mm | 0.1730 ❌ |

Row 1 is the board that shipped. `0.400 − 0.25 = 0.1500`, which is what 136
violations report. Row 5 is `FinePitch` — the same defect on a second netclass,
and the source of the residual `0.1106mm` bucket.

The 149 at `0.1972mm` are the same lattice step reached diagonally. The square
stamp blocks `|dx| ≤ 3` **and** `|dy| ≤ 3` cells; the cell at (4, 2) is free, at
Euclidean `√(0.4² + 0.2²) = 0.447214mm`, giving `0.447214 − 0.25 = 0.1972`.
Verified directly against board geometry:

```
A net=RELAY_CTRL                  (95.35,225.55)->(95.25,225.55) w=0.25 F.Cu
B net=safety.ovp.r_adc_top2-p2    (79.75,225.95)->(95.05,225.95) w=0.25 F.Cu
centreline dist = 0.447214
```

An all-pairs sweep of the 4,497 segments confirms the population is discrete,
not a spread: **1316 same-layer different-net pairs closer than 0.2mm, of which
503 sit at exactly 0.1972 and 144 at exactly 0.1500.**

**This is why the band exists and why it is not congestion.** Every adjacent
pair in a parallel bus is one violation, and a bus of two nets running 4.5mm
side by side generates them by the hundred. It also explains the +115 being
placement-independent (+113 on the prior candidate): the arithmetic does not
depend on where anything is.

## 3. Why the refuted routing-search fix does not apply here

`docs/evidence/2026-08-12-corridor-aware-plane-backbones.md` (#1052) refuted
*search quality*: the F.Cu corridor mask fragments into ~94 disconnected
regions, so no A\* can bridge them — *"it is not that A\* fails to find a path,
it is that none exists at the required clearance."*

That argument does not reach this band, and the distinction is measurable, not
rhetorical. #1052's premise is that the paths the router wants are **illegal**.
Here the paths the router found were **legal under its own reserved
geometry** — it reserved 0.40mm and used 0.40mm, with zero forced segments.
The failure is that its bar was 0.05mm below the bar it is graded by. No search
strategy is involved: the same A\*, the same corridor mask, the same placement,
with one constant changed, produces different copper.

The refutation is therefore intact and this is a different defect: not "the
search is too weak", but "the specification handed to the search is wrong".

## 4. τ, re-derived (the plan's numbers have moved)

`docs/plans/2026-08-12-001` reasoned from τ = 0.4mm governing 87.7% of pairs
while a Signal trace needs a 0.5mm channel. Re-derived on today's tree:

* `courtyard_clearance_mm(0.2) = 0.2 + 2 × 0.1` = **τ = 0.40mm, unchanged.**
  `#1061` moved `Power.clearance` 0.25 → 0.5, which changes which *pairs* clear
  τ, not τ itself.
* The plan's channel-width table said Signal needs 0.50mm (`0.2 + 2×0.15`) and
  GND 1.60mm, so a τ = 0.40mm backfill reserves gaps too narrow for any trace.
  **Measured, the achievable Default pitch is 0.400mm** (§2.3, row 4) — the
  reservation is `trace_width + clearance`, not `trace_width + 2×clearance`,
  because each net's own stamp covers one side. τ = 0.40mm is therefore
  *exactly* one Default trace channel, not zero.
* This does not rescue corridor reservation, and it is not why this document
  did not adopt it: at a **43.7% board utilisation** with the damage
  concentrated in two nets on one layer, adding pseudo-components to the
  placement model addresses a shortage that §2 shows was never the cause.

Reported because the plan's own headline arithmetic is the thing that changed,
not to reopen it.

## 5. What was changed

Three commits, each independently a correctness fix, all in the router. None
touches the placer, the board, or the ceiling.

1. **`_pipeline_core.py`**: delete `dr.default_clearance_mm = 0.15`. The parsed
   value (0.2) is kept, matching RULE 10.
2. **`io/_parse_nets.py`**: `default_trace_width` **0.25 → 0.20**. Three sources
   said 0.2 and none said 0.25 — `temper.kicad_pro`'s `Default` class
   `track_width`, that file's `design_settings.rules.min_track_width`, and
   `core/design_rules.py`'s `default_trace_width`. The emitted width feeds the
   reservation arithmetic, so this is load-bearing, not cosmetic.
3. **`router_v6/clearance_floor.py`** (new): `blocking_clearance_mm()` converts
   a *required gap* into the *rasteriser input* that delivers it, at the
   tightest lattice step. Wired into all three sites in `_astar_reconstruct.py`
   that stamp or unstamp routed copper (mark, tree-mark, ripup-unmark — they
   must agree or ripup leaves a stale footprint).

   The distinction it enforces is the original bug in reverse: the declared
   clearance is a **gap**; the kernel's parameter is a **rasteriser input**;
   they are related by a step function, not equality. Validated against the
   live Rust kernel for every declared netclass:

   | class | w | declared c | input used | pitch before | pitch after | gap achieved |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | Default | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.150 | 0.500 | **0.400** | 0.2000 |
   | FinePitch | 0.127 | 0.10 | 0.187 | 0.300 | 0.400 | 0.2730 |
   | GateDriveHV | 0.40 | 0.25 | 0.350 | 0.600 | 0.700 | 0.3000 |
   | Power | 1.00 | 0.50 | 0.850 | 1.100 | 1.500 | 0.5000 |
   | GND | 1.00 | 0.30 | 0.650 | 0.900 | 1.300 | 0.3000 |
   | HighVoltage | 3.00 | 2.00 | 3.350 | 3.600 | 5.000 | 2.0000 |
   | ACMains | 3.00 | 6.00 | 7.350 | 7.600 | 9.000 | 6.0000 |

   Note **HighVoltage**: it was reserving a 3.600mm pitch for a 3.0mm trace —
   a 0.600mm gap against its own 2.0mm rule. The same defect, 1.4mm wide,
   on the safety-critical class. Passing 0.20 straight through for Default
   (the naive repair) costs a **whole lattice step**, 0.400 → 0.500mm, a 25%
   density loss for margin nobody asked for; picking the step's midpoint avoids
   it. (`0.2/2 + 0.2` is `0.30000000000000004` in IEEE754, so the boundary is
   decided by float error — hence midpoints, not boundaries.)

4. **`scripts/check_router_clearance_floor.py`** (new, 5 properties, wired
   BLOCKING into CI with 10 tests). P4 walks `router_v6/` by **AST** and fails
   any literal assignment to `default_clearance[_mm]` below the DRU floor.
   **Mutation-verified**: run against `origin/main`'s `_pipeline_core.py` it
   fails at line 335 on the 0.15; against this tree it passes.

   `DEFAULT_ROUTING_CLEARANCE_MM` is now a named constant, and the generated
   `.kicad_dru` is **byte-identical** (sha256 `ed81027e…`) — the constant only
   removes a literal, it does not change fabrication output.

## 8. Assessment

**Landable as a router fix: yes. Landable as a board: no, and not closer than
before.**

What this PR is worth:

* the band is **explained**, to four decimal places, with the mechanism probed
  directly against the shipping Rust kernel;
* a real defect is removed — the router no longer optimises against a bar
  0.05mm below the one it is graded by, on **every** netclass including
  `HighVoltage`, which was reserving 0.6mm against its own 2.0mm rule;
* a **mutation-verified** gate makes the reintroduction impossible to land
  silently, which is the actual durable outcome (the defect survived 31 days
  and three separate clearance investigations precisely because it is
  invisible in either file alone);
* the "one congested column band" framing from #1086 §4 is **retired**: it was
  a two-net parallel bus, and the strip-rescope it recommended would not have
  helped.

What it is not:

* it does **not** reduce `clearance`, and §6.4 argues no router change will;
* variant C raises the aggregate 1143 → 1310, driven by `solder_mask_bridge`
  49 → 201 and `shorting_items` 137 → 199, both of which trace to the
  untouched `OTHER_NET_CLEARANCE_MM = 0.05` backbone path;
* it costs completion (86/102 → 78/102 topology; 55/139 → 51/139 pad).

**Recommendation.** Land the router correctness fix and the gate on their own
merits, keep holding #1082, and take the backbone's `OTHER_NET_CLEARANCE_MM`
next — it is now the largest single named mechanism, worth 280 pad–track
violations of which 170 are hard shorts. Do **not** spend another cycle trying
to move `clearance` from the router; six measurements across two lineages now
say it is placement.
