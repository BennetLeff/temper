**Project:** Temper Induction Cooker
**Scope:** Certification-readiness axis of the BOM — component agency approvals, not electrical function
**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Sources:** `elec/src/main.ato`, `elec/src/modules.ato`, `elec/src/components.ato`, `docs/hardware/BOM.md`, manufacturer datasheets fetched directly (see Evidence column; local copies under the analysis scratchpad, not committed)

## 1. What this is, and why

IEC 60335-1 (household appliance safety) does not accept "this part works" as sufficient for components whose failure can cause fire, electric shock, or injury. Those components — fuses, X/Y capacitors, mains-switching relays, isolation transformers/SMPS modules, reinforced-insulation isolators, thermal cutoffs, and the materials the board and enclosure are made of — must be **separately approved**: tested by a recognized body (UL, VDE, CSA, CQC, TÜV, etc.) to the relevant component standard, at the specific voltage/current/temperature rating the part is used at in *this* design.

This document is the first pass at that list for Temper. It has not been done before — every other BOM axis assessed so far (functional values, availability, ripple) asked "does it work / can we buy it," not "can we legally sell it." This axis asks the second question, and the answer is not yet "yes."

**Method:** for every category IEC 60335-1 cares about, the design was searched for the real ref designator and MPN doing that job (not a generic checklist). Each MPN's approval was then checked against a datasheet or agency database fetched directly — not inferred from the part's family, and not invented. Where no approval could be confirmed, the cell says **UNVERIFIED**. An UNVERIFIED cell is a correct, honest answer, not a failure of this document.

## 2. The isolation-barrier finding (read this before the table)

The task brief for this document specifically warned: verify where the isolation barrier actually is before classifying anything as SELV, because getting this wrong is the exact error class that already bit this project once (the OCP-02 common-mode miscalculation). It happened again, here, in a different circuit.

**The physical isolation component exists and is well-approved.** `AuxSupply` (`modules.ato:1075-1139`) uses a Mean Well IRM-10-15 encapsulated AC/DC module (`PS1`) to take ~170VDC off the half-bus and produce an isolated 15V rail. That module is genuinely certified for this role: Class II, IEC 62368-1, IEC/EN 61558-1/-2-16, UL 62368-1, TÜV EN 62368-1 and EN 61558-1/-2-16, EAC TP TC 004, BSMI CNS15598-1, with a 4.2kVAC input-output withstand rating (confirmed directly from Mean Well's own `IRM-10-SPEC.PDF`). Every downstream logic rail (`vcc_15v` → `vcc_3v3` → MCU, safety interlock, RTD ADC) is powered through this module's secondary.

**But the module's isolation is defeated by how its output is grounded.** `main.ato:273`: `aux_supply.power_out.gnd ~ gnd`. `main.ato:299`: `power_return ~ gnd  # Single-star-point ground join near doubler caps`. And `power_return` is not earth — it is the voltage-doubler midpoint (`main.ato:263`: `power_return ~ power_in.dc_bus.gnd_ref`), which is tied directly to AC Neutral through the common-mode choke (`modules.ato:620-621`: `ac_n ~ cmc.W2_1; cmc.W2_2 ~ dc_bus.gnd_ref`). So the "SELV" secondary of the isolated supply is deliberately strapped back to a node that tracks AC Neutral. The transformer isolates the *power path*; it does not isolate the *reference* the entire logic domain is measured against.

**There is also a second, independent conductive path from AC Line to the same node**, with no isolation device in it at all. `PowerInput`'s zero-crossing divider (`modules.ato:683-689`) runs `ac_l ~ r_zcd_top1.p1 → r_zcd_top2 → zcd → r_zcd_bot.p2 ~ dc_bus.gnd_ref`, and `zcd` is wired straight to `mcu.zcd_in` (`main.ato:456`). That is a resistive (not capacitive) 450kΩ path from raw AC_L to the same star-ground node the MCU, safety logic, and RTD ADC all reference — through ordinary Yageo 1206 resistors, not a certified safety-rated impedance-protection component. The module's own source comment already flags this as a TODO ("ZCD signal crosses isolation barrier"), but it has not been fixed.

**Net effect:** the "SELV control domain" (MCU, safety interlock, RTD sensing/ADC, and any USB or I2C connector eventually populated on the `usb_dn`/`usb_dp`/`i2c_sda_ui`/`i2c_scl_ui` nets, which today are bare, unconnectored signals per `main.ato:106-109,459-465` — no physical USB or I2C connector is currently instantiated in `elec/src`) is referenced to AC Neutral in normal operation, and would be referenced to AC **Line** in an L/N-reversed fault (non-polarized plug, miswired receptacle, or a floating/open neutral upstream — conditions IEC 60335-1 requires the appliance to remain safe under). This is most consequential for the RTD probe: `RTDSensing`'s own docstring (`modules.ato:1259-1265`) asserts "the user-touchable RTD food probe is therefore separated from AC mains potential" — that claim is not fully supported by the actual ground topology traced above. No component certificate fixes this; it is a wiring/topology decision, and it is the single highest-consequence finding in this document.

**What is and isn't mains-referenced, concretely:**
- Mains-referenced (by design, via the star point and the CMC/AC_N tie): `dc_bus_plus`, `dc_bus_minus`, `power_return`, and — because of the star join — `gnd`, `vcc_15v`, `vcc_3v3`, and every net hung off them (MCU, safety interlock logic, RTD ADC, CT bias, USB/I2C nets).
- Additionally Line-referenced via the ZCD divider, independent of the star join: the `zcd` net and, through it, one MCU ADC pin.
- Isolated only in the sense of "floats on the half-bus, not on Neutral": `power_15v_ls` (low-side gate-driver supply, explicitly referenced to `dc_bus.hv_minus`, not `gnd` — `modules.ato:380-381`), and the UCC21550's secondary-side (high-side) gate-drive output, which floats on `switch_node`.
- True chassis/earth: `pe`, bonded to `power_return` only through the 2.2nF Y1 capacitor (`modules.ato:645-646`) — by design a high-impedance EMI path, explicitly *not* a DC safety bond (per the module's own comment).

## 4. Gap list, ranked by consequence

1. **Isolation-barrier topology defeats the certified isolation module (§2).** `gnd`/`vcc_15v`/`vcc_3v3` (MCU, safety interlock, RTD sensing/ADC) are tied to `power_return`, which tracks AC Neutral through the CMC, and are additionally bridged resistively to AC Line through the ZCD divider. The RTD probe's own "isolated from mains" claim in its module docstring is not supported by the actual ground topology. No component certificate — including the genuinely well-approved Mean Well module — fixes this; it requires a wiring/topology change (isolate the ZCD signal, e.g. an optocoupler as the module's own TODO already proposes, and reconsider whether the SELV secondary should really be strapped to the mains-referenced star point).
2. **X2 and Y1 safety capacitor MPNs likely do not exist as specified** (`DE2E3KH221MA3B`, `DE1E3KX222MA4BA01`). These are precisely the parts the task brief calls "outright fail" if unapproved — and right now there is no confirmed-real part number to even check the approval of. Needs sourcing correction before any certification submission, not just a paperwork chase.
3. **Discharge relay contacts (G5LE-1 DC12) are being operated above their agency-approved voltage rating** (170VDC vs. 125VDC/30VDC-UL max). The relay itself is well-certified; this specific application is not covered by that certification. Needs either a relay rated for the actual voltage or a documented engineering-judgment deviation accepted by the certification body — an RC snubber alone does not restore rating coverage.
4. **No AC mains inlet connector, cordset, or strain relief exists in the electrical source.** Every plug-in appliance needs an approved answer here; the design currently has none, and the one document that claims otherwise is stale and contradicted by source.
5. **No fuse holder MPN — only the fuse element is specified.** (Being investigated in depth elsewhere; included here for completeness of the critical list.)
6. **CT1 (Coilcraft CST3015-100ED) carries no agency safety approval** despite a 5000Vrms/reinforced-insulation spec being cited in design rationale. If that spec is meant to carry certification weight, it currently can't.
7. **RT1 (Ametherm SL32-10015) shows no agency approval in its own datasheet**, contradicting generic series-level marketing claims.
8. **PCB base-material flammability/CTI is a stated target, not a verified material choice** — fixable at the fab-drawing stage but not yet done.
9. **Enclosure plastics flammability class is entirely unspecified** — worse than #8 because there isn't even a target.
10. **MOV (Littelfuse V150LA10AP) approval reported only by secondary sources**, not independently confirmed from a primary document opened during this analysis.
11. **Common-mode choke (L_EMI) has confirmed flammability (UL94 V-0 base) but no component-level agency file** — lowest-consequence item on this list; likely not required for this category.

## 5. What this document is NOT

- It is **not a certification**. No test house, notified body, or agency has reviewed this design.
- It is **not a substitute for a test house.** Every "Yes" above reflects a datasheet or agency-directory claim read directly by this analysis, not a test performed on this board, and not verification that the actual part received matches the datasheet (counterfeit/gray-market risk is out of scope here).
- It is **not exhaustive of IEC 60335-1.** It covers component-level approvals for the categories the task specified. It does not cover EMC testing, leakage current measurement, dielectric withstand testing, mechanical/stability testing, labeling, or the user-manual warnings already tracked in `docs/REGULATORY_COMPLIANCE.md`.
- Where a cell says a certificate/file number, it is one this analysis actually read in a fetched primary document (Schurter, Omron ×2, Mean Well, TI, Vishay, or the NEC/Schott distributor sheet) — none were invented, and none were carried over from a part's family to a specific unconfirmed part number.
