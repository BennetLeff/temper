# Temper stage21b and R43 DFMA review

The R43 enclosure is a coherent digital candidate with several documented simplifications, but the evidence does not yet establish a repeatable integrated build. Its strongest confirmed DFMA problems are **manufacturing-packet contradictions and mixed evidence revisions**. The most promising physical improvement angles are a simpler blower-power/harness assembly, inspectable and repeatable thermal installation, a feasible process route for the actual shell, and service paths that restore bonds and seals.

The requested **stage21b board** was frozen after its C30/C31 placement update produced a complete routed build and matching hash-bearing geometry receipt. Its SHA-256 is `c015804959add81e948055279cb78946e3a762ac9077c0b21bfbde3c89d0cb56`. The final board, manifest, raw/corrected placements and newly generated fabrication files agree. The producer resolved earlier corrected-CPL/manifest mismatches concurrently before the final capture. Some README/fab-note counts and several process/preview acceptances remain open. R43 remains a separately reviewed enclosure with older integrated board geometry. Its fit to stage21b is unverified.

This is a review and comparison brief. It changes no board, schematic, enclosure, BOM, baseline pin or supplier relationship, and selects no improvement for implementation.

## Coverage and verification

All **72 criteria** have one reconciled decision: **32 score 1, 27 score 2, 13 U**, with no 0/3/NA. These are evidence categories, not a defect count or an average readiness score. A zero count of score 0 does not establish absence of defects. [Scorecard](scorecard.md) and [criterion index](criterion-index.json) retain the individual source reports.

Fresh KiCad 10.0.4 DRC returned **zero electrical errors, zero unconnected items and zero schematic-parity issues in three runs**. The first included warnings: 199-capped silk-overlap, 199-capped silk-over-copper and 22 silk-edge warnings. The two repeat runs requested error severity only; they do not repeat a warning census. Gerber exports subtract soldermask and the producer's final Gerber screen reports no ink-on-land failure, so CAD silk warnings require an output/legibility check rather than an assumption that ink will cover joints. All 139 pinned review inputs retained their hashes after native operations. See [verification receipt](verification.md).

## Improvement angles for a later comparison

These are candidate experiments, not selected fixes. The acceptance rule remains cheaper or meaningfully simpler **and** technically better, while keeping required behavior, and with noncritical trade-offs disclosed. No percentage savings from the literature is applied to Temper.

| Angle | Possible economic/simplicity mechanism and technical benefit | Added burden / failure risk | Minimum discriminating experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finished-board thickness and terminal interface as one specification** | A supplier-compatible stack/termination can avoid rejects and hand fitting while improving joint repeatability. | A thicker stack or alternative stud changes current, plated holes, enclosure/clamp fit and price. | Compare the current THR part's 1.6 mm minimum with measured/accepted board thickness; qualify one consistent stack or termination at tolerance endpoints. |
| **L-outline panelization and targeted manual-process reduction** | Better panel/fixture utilization and fewer difficult handling or solder/trim steps may lower accepted-unit cost. | Rails, tabs, supports, rework, component stress and enclosure mating can erase savings. | Compare realistic populated-panel and SMT/THR/manual travelers; time expensive operations and inspect after depanel/rework. |
| **Blower supply, inlet and harness as one designed subassembly** | Shorter, more repeatable mains/DC routing could reduce custom brackets, late tie operations and abrasion exposure. It may avoid a long cross-board span. | Relocating supply/termination can change fusing, insulation, leakage, EMI, heat and service; work can simply move to another assembly. | Compare exact installed part/operation lists and routing volumes, then cold-fit selected mates and inspect fault/PE/thermal topology. Test electrical effects before adoption. |
| **Captive J13 retention integrated with an existing insulating support** | Potentially remove a disposable tie or separate flag and make required retention visible and repeatable. | An integrated guide may narrow gaps, make release harder, overstrain the plug or need a custom tool. | Compare current flag/tie to rated captive alternatives on actual board/base/plug coupons; measure installation/removal, tilt, retention and hot/aged separation. Do not delete the protective function. |
| **Controlled thermal seating and a simpler clamp/cradle process** | Fewer adjustments, better contact repeatability and easier defect inspection; fixture reuse may outperform a mechanical redesign. | Extra fixture/NRE or changed clamp forces may add cost or damage devices/insulating tiles. | Build a source→TIM→sink coupon with selected packages; measure force/contact/thermal resistance before and after refit, and time assembly. |
| **Hard-feature process simplification for shell and coil former** | Reduce difficult bends, tool approaches, tiny machined teeth, secondary operations and rejected parts. | Split blanks/joins can increase fasteners, liquid paths and cleaning work; larger former radii may change winding geometry. | Form the hardest P3/P4/door feature and machine a former arc/spoke; inspect fit, burrs, lead abrasion and finish. Compare whole-process routes. |
| **Preterminated, keyed harnesses and deliberate routing presentation** | Replace variable hand prep/routing with faster verified mating and fewer wrong builds. | Purchase price, MOQ, lead-time, cable envelope or extra connectors can rise. | Trial a selected harness versus hand process with crimp/continuity/strain/temperature checks, timed touches and matched installed space. |
| **Repeatable rear-service modules with explicit restoration** | Lower repair disturbance and less chance of missing PE hardware, ties or seals; common locating/fastening may save setup. | Added modular joints can raise part count, leak paths and thermal/interface resistance. | Replace a real intended service item through the closed-product route; record tools, disconnections, consumables, time and post-repair bond/seal/thermal checks. |
| **Datum-based tolerances and finish-specific joint control** | Reduce hand fitting and rework without unnecessarily tightening every part. | Better location can overconstrain thermal movement; selective finish adds processing. | Measure assembled cold/hot critical fits, actual coating buildup and bond performance. Preserve intentional expansion compliance. |
| **Inspectable intermediate states and revision-locked manufacturing packet** | Catch buried errors before more value is added and avoid supplier interpretation of obsolete instructions. | More controls/inspection take effort and can become duplicate paperwork. | Map each critical defect to its earliest observable state; reconcile drawings/BOM/quote/test hashes and try deliberate omissions to test coverage. This is a process-improvement angle, not an implementation performed here. |

A PCB-specific process experiment can compare the present required filled/capped via route with selectively avoiding soldered-pad vias. Removing a few vias is not automatically a cheaper process: establish whether the whole fabrication class or priced operations change, and preserve current spreading, Kelvin accuracy, thermal behavior and solder-joint quality. Reflow/THT and hidden-joint inspection routes should be compared with the same discipline. These are future experiments, not edits in this review.

## Resolved during the review

The user confirmed that stage21b was still updating C30/C31 at review start. The completed routed board was frozen and checked with a review-only 139-input manifest. The producer then accepted the same stage21b hash and regenerated the placement audit. Earlier corrected files had C30/C31 at old positions; **the final capture has correct positions in raw and corrected variants**, equivalent rotations after normalization, and the intended 10 audit rotation overrides. This correction was made by the concurrent producer work, not by these reviewers. The issue is retained as evidence that position/rotation/hash checks are needed in addition to designator parity. It is not an active stage21b failure.

## Positive decisions to preserve

R33 reduced UI wiring through a common board/cable; R39 consolidated metal M3/M4 drivers and PEM families while retaining insulating nylon and OLED exceptions. The producer's stage21b scorecard records a modeled OCP-pickup improvement from the C30/C31/Kelvin reroute, with the same 353-component count as stage21; this is source-bound modeled evidence, not a fresh physical result. The closed P1 seat ring has an explicit electromagnetic role; it cannot be split for better nesting without checking that role. R43's barrier/tie responds to a specific loose-lead path rather than arbitrary part proliferation. The prototype retains S8; skived K1 and owned R44 controls are future branches, not parts secretly substituted into this reviewed package.

Simplification should remove unnecessary operations or causes of complexity. It should not discard necessary insulation, bonds, heat paths, retention, EMI loops or service restoration merely because they increase the count.
