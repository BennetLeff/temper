## Evidence boundary and counted inventory

- `CURRENT.json` calls R43 `DESIGN_CANDIDATE_PENDING_INTEGRATION_REVIEW`. `quote-package/QUOTE-REQUEST.md:1–5,51–59` specifies two prototype sets, optional 2/5/10 price breaks, STEP-authoritative geometry and shop questions, and excludes electronics and bought parts from that shop quote. `quote-package/INTERNAL-NOT-FOR-SHOP.md:1–4` says the package was prepared but not sent. No accepted first-article, recurring yield, complete installed cost or supplier price set was found in the frozen package. `design/r43/HANDOFF.md:102` links an external cost dashboard and explicitly calls its figures estimates until quotes; those estimates are not in this frozen input and were not adopted as verified cost.
- `procurement-register.json:8–17` expressly limits scope to mechanical instances and candidate purchased items, with PCB component inventory in the separate electrical candidate. Its summary is **218 BOM rows** and **220 declared top-level quantities**: 44 custom rows, 138 purchased rows, 29 allocation rows and 7 consumable rows. A direct count of `catalog.json` yields **228 CAD entries**: 45 custom, 143 purchased, 31 allocations, 7 consumables, 2 references. The independently frozen stage21b `frozen/resolved-components.json` resolves **353 PCB designators**, and `frozen/default.csv` groups them into **92 BOM rows** with 92 distinct `Comment` strings. These are three different universes, with overlaps and allocations. In particular, the enclosure register's field `physical_quantity_total` is **not** a verified physical piece count: `BOM.csv` carries grouped lines such as a single `enclosure_fence_M3_screws` row for 12 screws and one louver assembly with a sleeve plus three slats; `design/r43/DECISIONS.md:84` also notes the fourth J13 tie is a procurement item on another row. An exploded, issueable parts and operation list is needed before claiming physical-part or feeder/setup reduction.
- The R43 `inputs/native-21/SNAPSHOT.json:17–28` pins a **stage-12** board (`section.kicad_pcb` SHA-256 `7430159671582725070640393982e4fcc1b24bdb721b83224b4419ad9329fa66`). Its `BOM.csv:42` still describes a **stage-13** board STEP with a different SHA prefix. The stage21b PCB is separately and exactly selected, but **R43's assembly was never re-based on that selected board**. Any power-board pose, fit, thermal or assembly clearance from R43 remains a historical R43 result until the stage21b PCB, fitted components, connectors and mounting envelopes are checked in the R43 frame. Do not transfer the R43 J13 2.22/2.20 mm clearance or thermal/retention PASS (`design/r43/DECISIONS.md:18–32,78–95`) to stage21b.
- Stage21b `frozen/default.csv` is usable as an exact **designator/MPN/footprint inventory**, but all **92 `Price` fields are `0.00` placeholders**. They cannot support a parts cost or installed-unit cost. The board's 353 parts should not be added to the R43's 220 top-level quantities without first de-duplicating its one `native21_pcb_assembly` row and reconciling outsourced modules, harness and consumable bundles. The earlier accepted stage21 scorecard (`pcb-stage21b/producer-context/DECISIONS.md:891–915`) and stage19c benchmark are historical comparators; neither supplies a measured stage21b/R43 unit cost or stage21b qualification.
- Positive architecture work already documented: R33 consolidated six UI looms to one board/cable (`README.md:113–130`); R39 moved metal M3/M4 screws to one 2.5 mm key and three PEM families, while **keeping insulating nylon and OLED M2 exceptions** (`design/r39/DECISIONS.md:5–19`); R43 added a separate polymer barrier plus tie for a specific loose-lead path (`design/r43/DECISIONS.md:13–32`). These examples show why row count alone is a poor optimization target.

## Candidate comparison order

1. Use the exact frozen **stage21b** board and `board.step` to produce a differential fit/interface table against the R43 model. This is the prerequisite to any claim about a compatible cooker unit.
2. Reconcile R43 issueable BOM and quote language. The strongest immediate process opportunity is removing ambiguity before purchasing: current exact inserts, protected nylon exceptions, selected barbs/ties and board/component versions. No hardware edit is implied.
3. On that stable boundary, measure actual pieces, build operations, touch time, tool/orientation changes, defect and rework paths, and installed cost. Explore specific reductions in the cover/fastener/harness/carrier architecture only with electrical, thermal, EMI, liquid and service floors unchanged. The continuous P1 seat ring is justified as an electrical loop (`QUOTE-REQUEST.md:39`); nesting it as separate arcs is not a free cost reduction. R39's intentional nylon/OLED exceptions remain function-specific, not gratuitous variation.
4. Keep prototype and production routes separate. The G11 machined former as winding jig and potential molded PPS former (`QUOTE-REQUEST.md:16`) have different tooling, insulation and dimensional evidence. Compare them at the intended volume with supplier feasibility and first-article checks, not one blended price.
