# Five Flute DFMA extraction for Temper

Reviewed 2026-10-13. Scope: the [Five Flute resources index](https://www.fiveflute.com/guides/) and eight publicly readable original engineering articles by William Burke, Five Flute founder and CEO. The index lists five “Ultimate Guide” download landing pages; the landing pages exposed only a short pitch and preview, so I did **not** treat their unseen downloadable PDFs as read. I followed the site's publicly accessible `guide/` articles instead. This is practitioner/vendor guidance and illustrative reasoning, not controlled evidence that any proposed Temper change will improve yield, cost, safety, or assembly time. The report contains original review questions; citations give motivation, not tested acceptance values. No current Temper board or enclosure is scored. Stage19b is contextual; an enclosure revision has not been selected.

## Original review questions proposed for Temper

These are **our proposed audit questions**, not a score, standard or vendor specification. Each names evidence needed and the decision tradeoff. A defensible answer depends on the selected PCB, exact mechanical revision, target build volume, intended fabricator, load/thermal environment and inspection method.

| ID | Original question for a future review | Evidence to require | Tradeoff or failure mechanism | Motivation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FFR01 | Is there a complete build map from incoming PCBs, coil, magnetic/thermal parts, enclosure stock and cables to packout? | Revisioned process flow with subassemblies, fixtures, handling, finish, test, rework and packaging steps. | “Simpler board” may move labor or purchased modules outside PCBA accounting. | FF1 |
| FFR02 | Which assembly operations are serial because a later part blocks access, and can the sequence change safely? | Assembly sequence, CAD sections, tool envelopes, timed prototype trial. | Parallel work may lower elapsed time but increase WIP or interface risk. | FF1, FF2 |
| FFR03 | Does every attached part have a defined locator, retainer, support/load path and installation order? | Assembly drawing, fastener or joint spec, loads, first-build observations. | Apparent CAD contact may conceal an unsupported or unserviceable part. | FF2, FF6 |
| FFR04 | Are harness and connector orientations unmistakable and impossible to cross-mate where cross-mating is hazardous? | Mating part numbers, keyed envelope model, build trial and inspection instructions. | More unique connectors increase sourcing burden; common connectors may invite assembly error. | FF1, FF2 |
| FFR05 | Can line workers handle, present and locate each awkward coil, shield, thermal pad, module, harness and enclosure piece without unsafe manipulation? | Physical mock build, handling trials, fixture/tool inventory and recorded failure modes. | Added guides or fixtures cost parts/tooling but may reduce handling time and damage. | FF1, FF2 |
| FFR06 | Are orientation changes and temporary holds of a partly built assembly necessary, stable and accessible? | Assembly video/time study, fixture concept, fastener reach sections. | One-axis builds can simplify handling but may conflict with thermal contact or cable routing. | FF1, FF2 |
| FFR07 | Are proposed part combinations justified by total installed cost and all functions, including grounding, insulation, heat and repair? | Side-by-side part/operation counts, functional requirements, material/process cost, service steps. | A monolithic enclosure may reduce assembly while raising tooling, scrap or repair cost. | FF1, FF2 |
| FFR08 | Where a fastener count or type changes, has each joint's static/cyclic load, preload retention, thermal expansion and removal cycle been checked? | Joint stack/material spec, torque/preload method, thermal/load tests, service trials. | Fewer fasteners or snap fits can degrade retention or repeat service. | FF1, FF6 |
| FFR09 | Do locating features constrain the right degrees of freedom without binding under forming and coating variation? | Datum plan, fit/tolerance chain, worst-case physical trial, finish-thickness spec. | More positive location may reduce fixture effort while over-constraining assembly. | FF1, FF3, FF4, FF5 |
| FFR10 | Can any sheet-metal enclosure candidate be cut, bent, hardware-installed and finished in the proposed order with available tool access? | Flat pattern, bend sequence/tool envelope, stock/grain/material, insert process, supplier capability. | A folded solid may demand extra setups or special tooling unseen in CAD. | FF3, FF4 |
| FFR11 | Are formed, cut, punched and coated features located and inspected by the functional datum they serve? | Revisioned part drawing, formed-state dimensions, process route, inspection plan and first articles. | Tight blanket tolerances add scrap; loose critical fits can break assembly. | FF3, FF4, FF7 |
| FFR12 | Does any tolerance-stack claim state distribution, drift and correlation assumptions, and identify interfaces needing bounded worst-case proof? | Measured dimension data, stack model, datum chain and assembly pass criteria. | RSS can be economical in stable volume production; unvalidated assumptions hide tail failures. | FF5 |
| FFR13 | Is finishing specified where it changes fit, electrical contact, heat transfer, corrosion or appearance? | Finish/material spec, coated/uncoated areas, thickness and masking callouts, post-finish gauges. | Coating improves life/appearance but changes clearance and conductive interfaces. | FF3, FF4, FF7 |
| FFR14 | Can critical assembly states be inspected with a clear pass/fail observation before they become hidden? | Access images, go/no-go or electrical/thermal test method, calibration and defect escape list. | Added checkpoints cost time but catch irreversible buried defects. | FF1, FF8 |
| FFR15 | Are drawings, PCB/placement outputs, harness diagrams, STEP/DXF, parts list and build instructions mutually revision-locked? | File manifest with hashes, title blocks, mating references and change record. | More documentation overhead reduces mixed-revision builds and supplier interpretation. | FF4, FF7, FF8 |
| FFR16 | Does the chosen enclosure process match prototype and eventual pilot volumes without hiding tooling or NRE? | Volume scenarios, process route, tooling/NRE, scrap/rework basis and assembly-hour estimates. | Prototype flexibility may have high unit labor; high-volume tooling may not amortize at pilot scale. | FF1, FF3, FF4 |
| FFR17 | Have makers separated the part drawing's inspectable requirements from illustrative CAD geometry? | Critical-characteristic list, datum scheme, drawing notes, first-article acceptance. | Under-defined geometry shifts judgment to the maker; excess annotation can cause impossible specs. | FF4, FF7, FF8 |
| FFR18 | Is every service-removable joint still reachable after assembly and repeated thermal cycling? | Tool path sections, disassembly trial, replacement part/fastener plan, cyclic test. | Permanent joining may aid initial assembly but worsen repair and late-stage scrap. | FF2, FF6 |

## What to carry forward and what to check elsewhere

The strongest contribution is an audit of **process sequence and physical interfaces**: map the build, find serial handling/fixture steps, make critical location/connection states observable, and specify manufacturing intent at functional datums. Five Flute does not provide Temper-specific acceptance limits, a tested induction-cooker case, a qualified supplier process window, or a verified enclosure revision. Its articles mix useful first-principles explanations with vendor promotion and some loose universal claims; all dimensions, time savings and cost percentages need corroboration before use. The five “Ultimate Guide” downloads remain unexamined and must not be cited as though read. PCB fabrication and assembly details require the separate JLCPCB/PCB-focused sources; polymer tooling, thermal-interface qualification, mains insulation and product safety require independent primary evidence.
