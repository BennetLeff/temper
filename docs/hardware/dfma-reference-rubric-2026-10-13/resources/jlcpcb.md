# JLCPCB DFMA resource extraction — unassessed Temper review questions

Read 2026-10-13. Source IDs and access depth are in `jlcpcb-sources.json`. This is a process-specific rubric contribution, not an assessment of any Temper board or a statement that JLCPCB will build the product. JLCPCB's published capability, fee, and inspection pages describe its own service; a selected fabrication and assembly route must supply the controlling process limits and quote. Page-derived statements below are brief paraphrases. The review questions and proposed evidence are my engineering synthesis; their acceptance limits require a named producer, intended volume, process, and Temper requirements. No text from the sites is reproduced verbatim.

## Source findings that change the review

- **J01** makes early package, stackup, outline, and assembly data decisions part of DFMA. Layout DRC does not verify land patterns against component drawings; component locations and orientation may be wrong in an otherwise clean board.
- **J02** distinguishes limits by copper weight, layer count, holes, finish, and special drilling. Its capability values are supplier-specific limits, not comfortable targets. The local JLCPCB skill file has older numeric examples; the live capabilities page is the better dated reference.
- **J03** gives a package-pair spacing matrix whose basis includes paste printing, machine placement, inspection, and rework. It is a JLCPCB recommendation, not a universal PCB electrical clearance.
- **J04–J05** define assembly records: each designator maps to a BOM part and a placement row with metric centroid, face, and rotation. JLCPCB states positive rotation as counterclockwise in its CPL format; CAD export conventions require a preview with polarized parts.
- **J06–J08** show that rails, fiducials, separation, and paste apertures belong to the production array and stencil. JLCPCB's panel page says V-cut is unavailable for its Economic Assembly path. Its stencil service may modify apertures by default; deliberately controlled paste geometry needs an explicit agreement.
- **J09–J10** distinguish inspection of paste, visible joints, hidden joints, and actual circuit function. Flying probe and functional testing are described as customized services; having inspection equipment does not establish the tests or acceptance criteria for a specific order. The X-ray page says package detection can miss particular parts.
- **J11** separates setup, stencil, feeder loading, solder joint labor, X-ray, fixture, panel, and handling fees by assembly mode. Its Sep 2026 fee table contradicts the simplified local skill's fixed “Basic free / Extended $3” summary for Standard Assembly; use a dated line-item estimate and live order quote, not either static slogan.

## Particular pitfalls for synthesis with the books and case studies

1. The JLCPCB pages are manufacturer guidance and pricing information, not an independent study of Temper's future yield. Treat any quotation or numerical improvement as a hypothesis until a selected process and build data exist.
2. A vendor's capability ceiling and a review target are different. For example, the capabilities table changes trace limits with copper weight; a future Temper grade must compare against the chosen stackup and measured current/heat/clearance requirements.
3. AOI, X-ray, flying probe and functional test answer different questions. A hidden QFN joint needs an agreed imaging/acceptance plan; a passing X-ray still does not show correct firmware behavior or mains-fault response.
4. JLCPCB's own current price page has a different feeder fee by Economic and Standard mode. This is a useful warning against importing the $3 Extended fee from the older local skill as a whole-product cost model.
5. An assembly service's automatic stencil processing or panelization is a process input that must be made visible in the released manufacturing package. Preserve the returned production data for comparison with the designed package.
