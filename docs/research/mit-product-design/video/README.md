# MIT video lecture corpus: power electronics

Retrieved 2026-10-03 from the official [MIT 6.622 Spring 2023 lecture-video index](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/lecture-videos/). The [manifest](manifest.json) inventories all 38 playable lecture pages (`VIDEO-001`–`VIDEO-038`) and records title, year, source URL, transcript URL, local path, SHA-256, and retrieval status. Eleven official transcript PDFs were downloaded, with extracted text beside them. The video streams themselves were **not** downloaded or watched; the synthesis below is based on those transcripts. The remaining 27 videos are indexed but their transcripts were not inspected. MIT OCW materials carry a [Creative Commons BY-NC-SA license](https://ocw.mit.edu/pages/privacy-and-terms-of-use/); keep local PDFs/text as research sources rather than copying them into the product repository. No timecodes are present in the downloadable PDF transcripts, so citations use PDF page numbers.

## Findings relevant to Temper

- **Switching loss and snubbers.** `VIDEO-015`, *Switching Losses and Snubbers*, derives turn-on and turn-off energy from overlapping switch voltage and current, then relates average switching loss to event energy times switching frequency ([transcript pp. 2–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1mse3G750j0GsIftgTg1Yp3eg9vIurS6A_transcript.pdf)). For Temper, review loss across operating frequency, bus-voltage, load, and device-tolerance extremes. Treat a snubber as an engineered response to measured overshoot/ringing and loss, with its own dissipation budget.
- **Thermal path and transients.** `VIDEO-016`, *Thermal Modeling and Heat Sinking*, models conduction with thermal resistance; the heat path includes device, interface, heat sink, and ambient, while thermal capacitance matters for transient heating ([transcript pp. 2–8](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1BYB3SGJU-Oi1Vk910805d4bCCzBI3NWZ_transcript.pdf)). For Temper, calculate junction and accessible surface temperatures at continuous and short overload conditions, with tolerances in interface thickness, contact pressure, airflow, and ambient. A nominal steady-state heat sink check alone does not cover start-up or fault energy.
- **EMI measurement and real components.** `VIDEO-028`, *EMI Filters, Part 1*, describes conducted EMI measurement with a line impedance stabilization network and shows why capacitor equivalent series inductance limits high-frequency filtering ([transcript pp. 4–7](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1bdfOZG2NZV1IrLoTf2VneLJpQEn728Uv_transcript.pdf)). Use the correct test setup and place/filter components according to their parasitics; do not infer compliance from ideal component values.
- **Filter resonance and damping.** `VIDEO-029`, *EMI Filters, Part 2*, notes that placement and wiring contribute parasitic impedance, and an undamped LC input filter can peak or interact with the converter ([transcript pp. 3–5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1WM2-syMPuAItj988GRl-pBigGbzksqXI_transcript.pdf)). Temper's mains/input filter should be checked for resonance across line, load, and component tolerances, including damping and stability, before a low-noise result is accepted.
- **Common-mode return paths.** `VIDEO-030`, *EMI Filters, Part 3: CM + DM*, traces parasitic capacitive paths to chassis/ground and distinguishes common-mode current from differential-mode current ([transcript pp. 2–6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1hcw4UlUAkt-0-hmuPKO7I89WRQf323bd_transcript.pdf)). For Temper, draw both return circuits through PCB, enclosure, cookware, mains cable, sensors, and user-accessible metal. Then measure them separately where possible; a differential-mode-only model misses important coupling.
- **Soft switching is conditional.** `VIDEO-033` and `VIDEO-034`, *Soft Switching, Parts 1–2*, discuss zero-voltage and zero-current transitions and their dependence on current, capacitance, and switching trajectory ([part 1 pp. 5–6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1B-EdPfWAdhfRVILtl-7GRVeJQGo3UgRP_transcript.pdf), [part 2 pp. 1–3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1RGQ46ECvt84wGz4Mg5J31J3K890YM50B_transcript.pdf)). Verify Temper's switch transition across the actual pan and power envelope; a resonant topology does not establish that every operating point is soft switched.
- **Resonant operating envelope.** `VIDEO-035`–`VIDEO-037` explain frequency-selective resonant tanks, fundamental-harmonic approximations, quality factor, rectifier/load equivalents, and network matching ([part 1 pp. 1–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1YOdoY_n8k6MLa0Htv4sSgzNisLig_d_X_transcript.pdf), [part 2 pp. 1–3, 7](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1YtOhx0q4OF3UYOU7PZczixk6h-Sg-mDi_transcript.pdf), [part 3 pp. 1–2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1hMyfXxXC8MkS__taKirTa-ZT6h9eBWQv_transcript.pdf)). For Temper, map tank voltage/current and loss under pan absence, small/large pans, pan movement, and temperature variation. The harmonic approximation is a model that needs waveform and tolerance validation.
- **Layout as a circuit parameter.** `VIDEO-038`, *Gate Drive, Level Shift, Layout*, starts by identifying the high-frequency commutation loop and switching node, then shows how excess loop inductance causes ringing and internal interference ([transcript pp. 1–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1jBfQFMSIBooBX_iqPSIqrZuAvrbZu6F9_transcript.pdf)). It explains close outgoing/return paths for the power and gate loops, capacitance tradeoffs, and shielding sensitive control from the switching node ([transcript pp. 5–8](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/1jBfQFMSIBooBX_iqPSIqrZuAvrbZu6F9_transcript.pdf)). On Temper's PCB, annotate actual commutation and gate-return paths, then examine parasitics and probe switching waveforms on the built assembly. Layout quality cannot be settled from schematic connectivity alone.

These are design prompts from lecture material, not findings that Temper currently passes or fails any test. The PDFs often rely on drawings and demonstrations; transcript text alone cannot validate equations, circuit geometry, or the observed waveforms. For a decisive design change, inspect the corresponding slide/handwritten notes and video segment, then ground it in Temper's measured board and enclosure.
