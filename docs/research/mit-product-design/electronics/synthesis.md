## Course map and scope

The [Fall 2026 Course 2 catalog](https://student.mit.edu/catalog/m2b.html) lists **2.678 Electronics for Mechanical Systems**, with labs including power supplies, amplifiers, switched-mode DC/DC converters, and DC motor drivers; **2.679 Electronics for Mechanical Systems II** explicitly includes PCB design and system integration (ELEC-019, ELEC-020, catalog entries only). The [2.737 Mechatronics OCW syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-737-mechatronics-fall-2014/pages/syllabus/) covers analog interfacing, power amplifiers, sensing and control (ELEC-021). These establish relevance; a catalog description alone is not evidence for a detailed design rule.

The strongest directly readable power source is [6.622 Power Electronics, Spring 2023](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/lecture-notes/), taught by David Perreault. OCW says this subject was previously 6.334; the [Spring 2007 6.334 calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-334-power-electronics-spring-2007/pages/calendar/) covers rectifiers, device stress, EMI filters, loss, thermal and resonant converters (ELEC-012, ELEC-022). The 2023 [video index](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/lecture-videos/) lists 38 lectures; the video pages expose transcript interfaces, but the videos and transcripts were **not watched or reviewed** (ELEC-013–015). The [6.002 Circuits and Electronics video index](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/video_galleries/video-lectures/) is a foundational follow-up, with OCW noting that its video and note material comes from Fall 2000 despite the Spring 2007 course shell (ELEC-018).

## Principles transferable to Temper

1. **Place and route around commutation loops and their returns.** 6.622 Lecture 38 says large high-frequency current loops need low inductance; loop inductance drives switching transients, ringing and EMI. It derives the effects of interconnect length, width, and return separation, and recommends close drive/return paths. It also warns that large copper area on a rapidly moving voltage node raises parasitic capacitance into sensitive nodes. For Temper, identify the actual switching loop, gate-drive loops, and sensor/reference returns on the *current* design, then inspect their geometry and test ringing. Do not transplant a generic buck example onto the full-bridge topology without remapping its current paths. [ELEC-001, Lecture 38, pp. 1–3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/mit6_622_s23_lec382.pdf).

2. **Evaluate loss and stress together, including transitions.** Lecture 15 distinguishes conduction, switching, gate, and parasitic losses. It notes the tradeoff between faster transitions and overshoot/EMI; a snubber can move heat from a semiconductor to a resistor and may raise total loss. For Temper, estimate worst-case RMS and switching loss over load, supply and temperature corners; check voltage/current waveforms and device safe operating area; then account for the snubber resistor's heat and fault behavior. [ELEC-002, Lecture 15, pp. 1–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/mit6_622_s23_lec152.pdf). Component limits must come from the actual manufacturer's current datasheet.

3. **Use thermal paths and transients, not a headline power rating.** Lecture 16 models junction-to-case, interface and sink/ambient resistances in series; its example shows a nominal device power rating can be impractical when the case is not held at 25 °C. It also introduces thermal capacitance and transient impedance for pulsed power. For Temper, make a loss-to-temperature budget for each power part across specified ambient, airflow and cooking duty cycles, and validate with temperature measurement at likely hot spots. Model a shared heat sink as a coupled system. [ELEC-003, Lecture 16, pp. 1–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/mit6_622_s23_lec162.pdf). The lecture's device/example numbers are pedagogical and are not Temper ratings.

4. **Treat EMI as a system measurement and layout problem.** Lecture 28 describes source impedance dependence and use of a LISN for repeatable conducted-EMI measurement. It emphasizes capacitor ESR, ripple-current ratings and unintended capacitance bypassing an otherwise high-attenuation filter. Lecture 30 adds damping and the negative incremental resistance of some regulated converters, plus common- and differential-mode currents in line-powered systems. For Temper, identify mode-specific return paths, filter parasitics and chassis coupling, then test emissions with an appropriate setup; analysis alone does not establish compliance. [ELEC-004, Lecture 28, pp. 1–3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/mit6_622_s23_lec282.pdf); [ELEC-005, Lecture 30, pp. 1–3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/mit6_622_s23_lec302.pdf). The lecture's examples of agencies, frequency ranges and older regulations are teaching context, not a current compliance prescription.

5. **Design and verify resonant operation across load variation.** Lecture 35 explicitly names high-frequency inverters for induction heating. It derives resonance, quality factor, voltage magnification, and narrowing bandwidth as Q rises; it also notes that applications differ in load range and control needs. For Temper, test expected pan presence/absence, pan materials, coupling and detuning as operating corners, including the resulting capacitor and switch stresses. [ELEC-006, Lecture 35, pp. 1–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/mit6_622_s23_lec352.pdf). The simple series RLC model is a starting model, not a validated cooker model.

6. **Give PCB assembly and bring-up their own design pass.** The 2.996 Lab 3 sheet recommends placing parts before routing, keeping decouplers near consumers, considering pour solderability, and running DRC before fabrication. Its spacing values and 2007 PCB Artist instructions belong to a small lab board, not a mains power PCB. [ELEC-007, Lab 3, p. 1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-996-biomedical-devices-design-laboratory-fall-2007/b44ec7a50730d04d90638927c85465d1_lab_handout3.pdf). Labs 1–2 require available, hand-solderable components and a schematic/footprint library (ELEC-009, pp. 1–2); Labs 4–6 advise assembling and checking the standard block before adding all custom components (ELEC-010, p. 1). A separate [16.682 Prototyping Avionics outline](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-682-prototyping-avionics-spring-2006/pages/lecture-notes/) lists manufacturing output, documentation, staged assembly, critical-net checks, and first-power-up checks, but most PCB lecture rows are descriptions without a linked PDF (ELEC-017, index only). For Temper, review footprint provenance, assembly access, test points, board release files and staged first-power procedure with manufacturer and lab input.

7. **Plan controlled-power edge cases explicitly.** The 2.996 power-supply lecture covers source impedance, load dependence, brownout, startup, sequencing, and regulator layout. For Temper, verify low-voltage rail behavior during AC brownout, power-up, power-down and fault interruption, then prove the control outputs reach their intended safe state. The lecture is from 2007 and has outdated component/USB specifics; use only the general failure-mode questions. [ELEC-008, Lecture 4, pp. 23–30](https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-996-biomedical-devices-design-laboratory-fall-2007/095410522b047bebb91d8395943c60fd_lec4_pwr_suplies.pdf).

8. **Prove control behavior on hardware and at limits.** 2.737 Lab 5 compares a motor model with measured response, builds PI control, observes integral windup and applies anti-windup; it also warns that out-of-range supply conditions can damage the lab amplifier. The useful transfer is a test method: model, measure response, sweep limits, and validate control saturation/recovery. The lab's DC motor and ±15 V bench hardware are not a mains induction cooker. [ELEC-011, Lab 5, pp. 1–3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-737-mechatronics-fall-2014/bf08200e8344f69f5b8a4ac923ea6d53_MIT2_737F14_Lab5.pdf).

## Boundary for skills and product review

The corpus supports review questions and engineering methods, not numerical mains safety distances, certification claims, safe touch-temperature limits, production PCB stackups, or guaranteed cooker reliability. A product skill should instruct the agent to resolve the *current* schematic/board/enclosure baseline first (the repository has multiple design generations), then document source revisions, assumptions, simulated or measured corners, component-specific datasheets, DRC/test evidence and unresolved risks. Current candidate context supplied by the parent agent points to `zapote/power-stage-120v/README.md` as a full-bridge, unfiltered-bus design; do not silently treat legacy `pcb/temper.kicad_pcb` or a PFC revision as the product authority.

## Gaps to fill before release decisions

- Current applicable safety and EMC requirements for the intended markets, with official standards access or qualified compliance review; no spacing or pass/fail numbers are inferred here.
- Current manufacturer datasheets and application notes for selected semiconductors, drivers, capacitors, connectors, insulation materials and thermal interfaces, plus actual board fabrication and assembly capabilities.
- Production-representative evidence: DRC with correct stackup/rules and libraries; electrical stress and transient captures; thermal tests at specified environments; EMI pre-compliance; sensor faults, open/short behavior, no-pan/wrong-pan tests; and staged assembly/bring-up yield.
- MIT 6.622 lectures 29, 33–34 and 36–37 may help on filter damping and resonant switching, but only their index entries were inventoried here. The 2.678/2.679 catalog provides no public lecture files in this corpus.
