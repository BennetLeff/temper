# MIT product design and manufacturing: evidence for Temper

Checked 2026-10-03. This is a selected, primary-source corpus, not a complete crawl or a certification basis. `manifest.json` identifies the 16 PDFs inspected, their SHA-256 hashes, and local research-cache paths. Page numbers below are printed PDF pages or slide numbers, not web search snippets. Each application to Temper is marked **inference**; the linked MIT teaching material supplies the method, not a verdict on this product.

## Product taste as an engineering decision

**Source-derived — 2.70:** The 2023 syllabus defines FREEDPARRC as functional requirements, ergonomics and environment, design parameters, analysis, references, risks, and countermeasures ([MFG-002, pp. 1–2](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/2.70%202023%20Course%20Info%20Materials/2.70%20syllabus%202023.09.14.pdf)). The deterministic design lecture applies those columns from strategy through concept, module, and component, with loads, interfaces, power flow, structural loops, error allocation, and peer review ([MFG-013, slides 6–11](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2001/Topic%201%20Deterministic%20Design%20Process.pdf)). Its short process asks for bench tests, safety and ergonomics/manufacturing review before detail design, then build and test of the most critical module with predicted versus measured performance ([MFG-001, p. 1](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Reading%20Materials/PMD%20Ten%20Step%20Precision%20Process.pdf)).

**Inference for Temper:** A visually clean enclosure or PCB is high quality only when each visible choice traces to a function, user interaction, process, analysis, and known failure mode. For every major feature (pan locating surface, user control, cooling inlet/outlet, service opening, board mount, mains entry), require a one-row FREEDPARRC record and one observable acceptance check. If a feature's main value is visual, still state the cost of that choice in cleaning, thermal path, assembly access, and tooling. Prototype the highest-risk cross-domain module first: for example, a pan/support/coil/thermal sensor stack or a power-entry/heat-sink/fan assembly. Compare measured performance with a predeclared prediction rather than treating a successful demo as validation.

**Source-derived — 2.007 and 2.009:** 2.007's design-process lecture starts with objectives, user requirements, constraints, functions, and specifications before alternatives, modeling, test, refinement, and documentation; it says the design notebook must show alternatives and reasons for choosing among them ([MFG-003, pp. 2, 5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-007-design-and-manufacturing-i-spring-2009/c6d6a35a25a46a42735735e67b7d4002_MIT2_007s09_lec02.pdf)). The 2.009 course overview uses estimation, sketches, mockups, geometric models, and working alpha prototypes to reason about alternatives ([course page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-009-product-engineering-process-fall-2021/)).

**Source-derived — 2.00B:** Its course info makes sketch models, design journal, and prototype milestones explicit and describes play testing with intended users as essential feedback ([course info, “Play Testing” and “Grading”](https://web.mit.edu/2.00b/www/course_info/courseinfo.html)). This is evidence for testing ideas with people; the course does not provide an appliance usability protocol.

**Inference for Temper:** Run observed sessions with representative cooks using full-size control and surface mockups: pan placement, heat-setting interpretation, feedback under noise and glare, spill cleanup, and recovery after an interruption or fault. Record what people actually did, where they hesitated, and what they misread; separate those observations from designer preference.

**Inference for Temper:** Record at least two real alternatives for each expensive enclosure, heat-flow, and interface decision. A rendering cannot close hand reach, wet cleaning, pan stability, assembly, repair, or thermal questions. Use full-scale mockups, physical process samples, and instrumented modules where those questions determine a choice.

## Manufacturing and assembly gates

**Source-derived — 2.008:** Manufacturing choices have four linked attributes: quality, cost, rate, and flexibility ([MFG-004, slide 4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec02.pdf)). The injection molding lecture treats part geometry, shrinkage, reinforcement, ejection, melt delivery, mold balance, and defects as one design problem. Its slides advise keeping nominal wall thickness thin and uniform, placing ribs to gain stiffness, and designing a parting line and draft for removal ([MFG-005, slides 7, 16–18](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec03.pdf)). Its process-window diagram shows that short shots, flash, and degradation bound usable combinations of pressure and temperature ([MFG-005, slide 2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec03.pdf)). The assembly lecture frames part constraints and combinations of forming, machining, fastening, bonding, soldering, and welding as design questions ([MFG-006, slides 3–5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec08.pdf)).

**Inference for Temper:** Compare folded metal, machined metal, molded polymer, and mixed assemblies against the *same* requirement and expected volume. Put prototype and production process choices in separate rows. Before committing to a molded enclosure, get a toolmaker review of wall transitions, draft, parting line, gate, cosmetic surface, ejection, and measured post-mold dimensions on actual resin. Before committing to formed metal, prove bends, corner treatment, finish, fastener insertion, burr control, and distortion on a production-intent process sample. Do not transfer the lecture's example draft angles, wall limits, or process parameters directly to Temper; material, supplier, tool, and use conditions govern.

**Source-derived — 2.875:** Design for assembly is broader than reducing part count. Whitney's lecture asks whether parts need relative motion, disassembly for repair/inspection/upgrade, or distinct function, and highlights feeding, grasping, visibility, access, orientation, lead-ins, and assembly sequence ([MFG-012, slides 12–18](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/319a0f903d70a1a731d869d2a1c70c7c_class16_dfa04.pdf)). It explicitly warns that optimizing a simple DFA metric can carry functional risk ([MFG-012, slide 19](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/319a0f903d70a1a731d869d2a1c70c7c_class16_dfa04.pdf)). Its assembly-sequence lecture includes non-assembly steps such as tests and temporary disassembly in the sequence model ([MFG-011, opening and “Non-Assembly Steps Can Be Included”](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/aa4c12b52f7d971af7028cbed79513a2_cls10_asy_seq_04.pdf)).

**Inference for Temper:** Build and film a physical assembly of the cooker, from bare chassis to sealed unit, with the actual tools and process order. At each step ask: Is the mating feature visible and reachable? Can the part be installed backwards? Does it self-locate without an operator holding two floating pieces? Can wiring be routed and inspected before the next closure? Can a failed board/fan/sensor be replaced without destroying other parts? Identify where electrical, airflow, thermal, and seal tests occur in the sequence; a passing end-of-line test is inadequate if a later step can damage or bypass the tested feature.

## Tolerances, fixtures, and variation

**Source-derived — 2.75 and 2.875:** Slocum separates a tolerance budget (what shape the assembly can take from part geometry) from an error budget (how the operating system performs under geometric and thermal errors). A hole pattern can fit via enlarged holes yet lose unique location; precision is valuable in the sensitive direction ([MFG-009, slides 7–13](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-75-precision-machine-design-fall-2001/6587342c5f6ffe66f4da3e4e634891a2_topic4errorsinprecisionmachines.pdf)). Whitney tracks critical characteristics at assembly level through part and joint variation. Root-sum-square statistical tolerance stacking assumes centered, independent errors; mean shifts can make it optimistic ([MFG-010, slides 4, 10, 13–16](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/290ff5175c9d657c882a3be78ed8453d_cls9_assy_var04b.pdf)).

**Inference for Temper:** Pick the product-level sensitive directions first: pan-to-coil spacing/alignment, coil-to-shield/chassis clearance, sensor-to-pan thermal contact, fan-to-duct seal, and PCB-to-enclosure datum. For each, draw the datum chain and worst-case stack across part tolerances, bends/molding variation, fastener clearances, thermal growth, and assembly sequence. Add statistical prediction only after real process data show centering and correlation. Check assembled, hot, cold, and reassembled conditions. Fixture design should constrain the product from the same functional datums used in the tolerance analysis; a fixture that references a cosmetic outer edge may measure the wrong thing.

**Source-derived — 2.008:** Quality starts with a measurable characteristic, tolerance, and observed variation, including part, assembly, and use-condition variation ([MFG-007, slides 5–11](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec11.pdf)). The SPC lecture distinguishes specification limits from process control limits and gives process capability in terms of tolerance width and observed spread ([MFG-008, slides 4, 22–23](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/mit2_008_s25_lec12.pdf)). Whitney separates mean shift from spread and asks for assembly-level quality beyond simple interchangeability, including durability, reliability, and noise/vibration ([MFG-010, slides 2, 6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/290ff5175c9d657c882a3be78ed8453d_cls9_assy_var04b.pdf)).

**Inference for Temper:** For each critical characteristic, specify a measurement method, sampling plan, calibration artifact, rejection rule, and traceability to part lot or build. Separate design tolerance from process control limits. Measure both mean drift and spread over actual builds, with operators and material lots varied. Include assembly and use conditions in pilot builds. Product-level production tests should exercise power-up, controls, intended load detection, thermal sensing, fan/airflow response, fault indication, and safe shutdown under representative faults; exact electrical and safety limits must come from applicable standards, component data, and independent engineering analysis rather than these mechanical-design lectures.

## Risks and evidence boundaries

The lecture content gives methods and examples, not dimensions, material ratings, compliance limits, or a tested Temper design. In particular, 2.70 joint-design heuristics are for precision machine structures, so they should not be copied as enclosure screw sizes or torque specifications ([MFG-016](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2006/PMD%20Topic%2013%20Joint%20design.pdf)). 2.008's injection molding numbers and 2.875's historic cost/time examples need supplier and process validation. Treat drawings, enclosure renders, and PCB DRC as separate evidence types. A clean DRC or a beautiful prototype cannot establish assembly yield, surface temperature, structural endurance, cleanability, or appliance safety.
