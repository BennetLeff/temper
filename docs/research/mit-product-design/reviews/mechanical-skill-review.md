## Source fidelity

- **Pass — critical citations match their pages.** 2.70 *Deterministic Design Process* PDF pp. 21–26 distinguishes geometric, thermal, load, and process errors and discusses allocation, linear sums and RSS; p. 31 limits RSS to uncorrelated sources. 2.875 *Key Characteristics* pp. 2, 5, 15 says a requirement merits special attention when critical **and** threatened by variation, then flows it into assembly and manufacturing controls. *Datum Flow Chain* pp. 2, 4–6 maps dimensional relationships through parts, fixtures and assembly sequence, and p. 5 explicitly says it precedes tolerance analysis. *Variation Buildup* p. 3 correctly separates tolerance, variation and clearance.
- **Pass — thermal, joints and assembly citations are scoped.** 2.72 *Constraints* pp. 5, 15–17, 20 addresses overconstraint, thermal growth and directional compliance. 2.70 *FUNdaMENTAL Principles* pp. 20 and 37 addresses thermal distortion and constraints fighting under tightening or heating. *Structural Design 1* pp. 6 and 10 separates static from dynamic stiffness. *Joint Design* pp. 3, 14–16 addresses preload, contact stiffness and possible cyclic loss. 2.875 *Design for Assembly* pp. 3, 5, 14 supports stable/self-aligning assembly, visible/reachable steps and the snap-fit/drop-test counterexample. The skill correctly avoids transferring any of their machine-specific numerical values to Temper.
- **Minor precision point.** The source note describing *Joint Design* pp. 14–16 as about “cyclic preload loss” is supported explicitly on p. 16, but its suggested remedies (retightening, adhesive, epoxy) are precision-machine examples; the skill should continue to avoid copying them into an appliance prescription.

## Forward test answer

**Do not release fits or declare reliable hot click or sealing.** If the air gap and stop dimensions share the stated datum and act in the same direction, available switch stroke at the stop is `stop travel − initial air gap = [0.55−0.05−(0.20+0.10), 0.55+0.05−(0.20−0.10)] = [0.20, 0.50] mm`. Nominal is 0.35 mm. The switch trip range is `[0.15, 0.40] mm`. The guaranteed actuation margin is `minimum available − maximum required = 0.20−0.40 = −0.20 mm`; a valid stack can hit the stop without a click. At the other extreme, stroke after earliest trip could be `0.50−0.15 = 0.35 mm`; the allowable switch overtravel or maximum safe mechanical travel is missing, so switch damage cannot be excluded. This arithmetic assumes independent extreme endpoints are physically possible; if dimensions are correlated, prove the correlation from the datum/assembly chain before reducing the worst case.

The rigidly screwed polymer carrier against aluminum sheet and glass ceramic should be treated as a potential thermal overconstraint and preload-change path. A room-temperature CAD collision pass establishes only one geometric state. Temperature fields, material CTEs, span between mount points, fastener/preload and carrier stiffness are needed to estimate differential growth and coil-to-top movement. Trace the cookware load through glass support and enclosure, and separately inspect whether hot deflection changes the knob/switch gap. A seal needs a specified compression range, groove/stack dimensions, material thermal behavior and compression-set/cycle evidence; neither click nor sealing can be inferred from nominal CAD fit.

Minimum next measurements: define the knob stop and switch actuation datum chain; measure the distributions or controlled limits for actual stop, gap, trip and safe overtravel; measure switch force and travel, including hot and cycled states; set pass criteria for trip before stop, overtravel below switch limit, and tactile click across all combinations. For the enclosure, measure hot relative displacement, glass support load/deflection and joint preload/retention after cycles. For the seal, define required compression and leakage/wet ingress test conditions and verify after service reassembly. These criteria must come from Temper requirements or component specifications, not MIT slides.

## Skill usefulness and minimum corrections

The skill would likely catch the failure: it calls for a measurable criterion, worst-case variation, thermal growth, load paths, and cold/hot/reassembly tests, and explicitly says a CAD collision pass is insufficient. Its most consequential gap is that it does not explicitly require a **two-sided travel check** for a switch or other moving interface: guaranteed actuation at minimum available stroke and no overload at maximum available stroke. Add one concise sentence with those two inequalities and a requirement to record missing component overtravel. Also explicitly mention seal compression as a critical stack where sealing is claimed, including retention of compression after hot cycling and reassembly. These are operational prompts, not MIT numerical rules.

No finding here validates Temper's actual enclosure, switch, seal or coil carrier. The forward test was a hypothetical tolerance stack, and no physical product data were supplied.
