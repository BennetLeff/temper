# MIT mechanical design corpus: source-backed transfer to Temper

Retrieved 2026-10-03. This is a **selected** corpus covering MIT 2.70/2.77 precision product design, 2.72 elements of mechanical design, 2.875 assembly, and 2.008 manufacturing. [sources.json](sources.json) records 22 downloaded source files (17 PDFs, 5 HTML pages), their original URLs, hashes, license evidence, and review status. The OCW site shows a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 link, while some PDFs contain their own copyright notices or third-party exclusions, so reuse beyond internal analysis needs checking.

## Source-derived design principles

1. **Start with functions and physical mechanisms.** Slocum's 2.70 process asks for functional requirements, design parameters, ergonomics/environment, analysis, references, risks, and countermeasures; it develops strategies into concepts, modules, and components while repeatedly using first-order estimates and reviews. That supports a traceable function → physical choice → analysis → test thread, rather than aesthetic selection alone. [MECH-003, PDF pp. 6, 10–11](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2001/Topic%201%20Deterministic%20Design%20Process.pdf); [MECH-007, PDF p. 1](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Reading%20Materials/PMD%20Ten%20Step%20Precision%20Process.pdf).

2. **Allocate an error budget before choosing dimensions and interfaces.** 2.70 separates geometric, thermal, load-induced, and process errors, and apportions an allowable total to subsystems. It distinguishes worst-case linear sum from root-sum-square assumptions for uncorrelated errors. Those are methods, not universal numerical tolerances. [MECH-003, PDF pp. 21–26, 31](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2001/Topic%201%20Deterministic%20Design%20Process.pdf).

3. **Design datum and constraint paths for the whole assembly.** Whitney's 2.875 lectures treat assembly as a chain of dimensional relationships. The datum flow chain maps which mating features and fixtures locate each part and then enables tolerance analysis. Key characteristics are requirements critical to performance, safety, or regulation that are at risk from process variation; they are delivered by chains across parts and processes. [MECH-013, PDF pp. 2–6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/d39cbc40396e52ddb6eab3e879f25f2d_cls11_13_dfc_04.pdf); [MECH-011, PDF pp. 2–5, 15](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/ccd7fd17028fa75457996083cf90f8d2_class_kcs_04.pdf).

4. **Avoid hidden fights between constraints.** Culpepper's 2.72 constraint lecture distinguishes under-, exact-, and over-constraint and shows thermal growth causing binding; Slocum's 2.70 principle set similarly warns that assembly, fastener tightening, loads, and thermal expansion can force deformation if locations are redundant. The course examples are machine elements; apply the *reasoning* to enclosure and PCB mounts, not the particular bearing prescription. [MECH-015, PDF pp. 5, 15–20](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-72-elements-of-mechanical-design-spring-2009/22c0cb8444c787d92b49afe5ed97ff7b_MIT2_72s09_lec06.pdf); [MECH-004, PDF p. 37](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2002/Topic%202%20Fundamental%20Principles.pdf).

5. **Treat all joints as compliant and load-bearing.** 2.70 stresses finite stiffness and misalignment, and its joint-design notes show that bolting, preload, contact surfaces, and tightening methods affect joint stiffness, deformation, damping, and cyclic stability. Static stiffness alone may miss dynamic response. [MECH-003, PDF p. 4](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2001/Topic%201%20Deterministic%20Design%20Process.pdf); [MECH-006, PDF pp. 2–3, 14–16](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2006/PMD%20Topic%2013%20Joint%20design.pdf); [MECH-005, PDF pp. 6, 10](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2003/PMD%20Topic%209a%20Structural%20design%201.pdf).

6. **Make temperature a geometry input.** The 2.70 principles cover bi-material thermal warping and thermal isolation. The 2.72 examples show how a heated shaft and cooler housing change constraint loads. [MECH-004, PDF p. 20](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Lecture%20Materials/Documents/Week%2002/Topic%202%20Fundamental%20Principles.pdf); [MECH-015, PDF pp. 15–20](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-72-elements-of-mechanical-design-spring-2009/22c0cb8444c787d92b49afe5ed97ff7b_MIT2_72s09_lec06.pdf). **Scope caution:** bearing examples do not themselves validate a cooker's materials, temperatures, or clearances.

7. **Specify the actual critical fit and measure variation.** 2.875 distinguishes a tolerance (specified acceptance range), variation (what real parts do), and clearance (space between surfaces). Its key-characteristic lecture calls for flowdown from product requirement to assembly and manufacturing controls. [MECH-012, PDF p. 3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/8badde647439f7a92165d8b8135817d8_cls8_part_var04a.pdf); [MECH-011, PDF pp. 5, 15](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/ccd7fd17028fa75457996083cf90f8d2_class_kcs_04.pdf).

8. **Test assembly sequence and service access with real parts.** Whitney's design-for-assembly lecture favors stable, self-aligning assemblies, forgiving tolerances, fewer tools/fixtures, and easy part orientation, while also illustrating that replacing screws with snap fits can fail a drop requirement. 2.72 asks teams to fabricate and characterize a working product and plan fabrication/measurement before cutting parts. [MECH-014, PDF pp. 5–8](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-875-mechanical-assembly-and-its-role-in-product-development-fall-2004/319a0f903d70a1a731d869d2a1c70c7c_class16_dfa04.pdf); [2.72 assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-72-elements-of-mechanical-design-spring-2009/pages/assignments/).

9. **Review ideas independently before group convergence.** The 2.70 PREP paper describes individual thought, written peer review, then discussion/selection to expose missed risks and avoid the first loud proposal dominating. [MECH-010, PDF p. 1](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/Reading%20Materials/JMDEDB%20PREP.pdf).

## Temper transfer hypotheses — engineering inference, not course claims

- Define the few mechanical **key characteristics** for the cooker, such as coil-to-cookware relationship, PCB-to-enclosure clearances, thermal-interface pressure, touch-surface temperature, and seal geometry. Give each an owner, measurement method, and acceptance criterion grounded in Temper's own requirements. The course supports the method; it provides no acceptable values for this product. Sources: MECH-011, MECH-013.
- Draw a datum flow chain from cookware support → top surface → coil carrier → coil, and another from enclosure datum → PCB mounting hardware → power-device thermal path. Show every locater, fastener, spacer, fixture, and tolerance on the chains. Check assembly sequence against these chains. Sources: MECH-013, MECH-015.
- For PCB and enclosure mounting, count which degrees of freedom each feature locates. Model thermal growth of unlike materials and allow compliance where appropriate; verify that neither assembly torque nor expansion bows the PCB or changes coil registration. Sources: MECH-004, MECH-006, MECH-015.
- Build a dimensional and mechanical error budget for the coil, sensors, touch controls, connectors, heat sink, and user-visible seams. Include manufacturing variation, assembly variation, heat, loads, and service reassembly. Check worst-case paths where failure consequences warrant it; measure actual prototype distributions before relying on statistical combination. Sources: MECH-003, MECH-012, MECH-013.
- Map screw preload and load path for heavy or hot components; test after thermal cycles, transport shock, and repeated service assembly. These tests are **proposed for Temper** and need product-specific requirements and qualified test procedures. Sources: MECH-005, MECH-006, MECH-014.
- Have a person unfamiliar with the design assemble and disassemble a prototype. Record orientation errors, tool reach, connector access, cable damage potential, and whether reassembly returns each key characteristic to its measured state. Sources: MECH-014, 2.72 assignments.

## Access and limits

- The 2.70 home page points to a [FUNdaMENTALs42 video channel](https://www.youtube.com/@FUNdaMENTALs42). The channel could not be fetched through the research browser; **no videos were watched and no captions or transcripts were verified**. The [2.70 syllabus](https://web.mit.edu/2.70/2.70%202023%20Course%20Info%20Materials/2.70%20syllabus%202023.09.14.pdf) identifies prerecordings, but this corpus makes no claim about their content.
- The 138 indexed materials include many bearings, drives, optics, and energy items outside Temper's immediate needs. They are discoverable, not reviewed. See [linked-resources.json](linked-resources.json).
- 2.008 manufacturing PDFs are downloaded but image-heavy; extracted text is sparse. Their titles are verified, but this synthesis does not use them as evidence for specific manufacturing rules. Read slides visually before turning them into agent instructions.
- These lectures teach design methods. They do not substitute for appliance safety standards, supplier specifications, insulation system qualification, thermal tests, or EMC measurements.
