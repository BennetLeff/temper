## Precharge ECO PC88

**Rejected under the present precharge admission contract.** The historical qualified coupled solve finds less than178V charged bus at140Vrms,37.23V crest resistor drop and no qualification. The thermal improvement alone does not justify bypass or RUN. The following is the tested candidate, not an adopted ECO; final-sequence receipts are indexed separately.

Candidate: replace RP1/RP2's parallel 11Ω connection with **four HS200 22R F resistors in series (88Ω nominal)**, each with its own thermally coupled cutoff and separate accessible four-wire POST points. Keep bypass across the complete string. One open resistor inhibits charge; one shorted resistor leaves66Ω. Add two physical mounts and TCO channels; do not silently reuse the old two-resistor pod CAD. The reason is fault power, not normal charging energy.

At 140Vrms, all−1% resistance, a downstream short demands1.607Arms and56.244W per resistor. With one resistor short, the remaining three each dissipate99.990W at2.143Arms. The500ms budgets are28.122/49.995J per surviving part. By comparison the old11Ω design demanded about450J per resistor. These values come from `coordination.rs`. The same source at240V is outside admission, but that wrong-source fault would demand165.3W per element healthy and293.8W per survivor after one short: independent input qualification remains essential.

The manufacturer's [exact HS200 22R F page](https://www.ohmite.com/catalog/hs-series/HS200_22R_F) identifies200W and heat-sink mounting. That is **not a guarantee of200W at the proposed installed temperature**. Freeze a mount supporting at least100W per element at the declared worst initial case temperature; document actual derating, thermal interface and TCO attachment. The attempted official datasheet link returned404, so no new mounting/derating or overload curve is invented. A simple77.74µF capacitance upper allocation gives6.84ms RC; the joined nonlinear startup must still meet voltage/current qualification. PC88 reduces the required rating to a continuous-power thermal condition and avoids relying on an unverified500J pulse curve. It does not replace the independent hardware timeout or prove a shorted whole-string bypass safe.

## Explicit capacitor admission

Keep commissioning current permission **zero**. `coordination.rs` implements a fail-closed **sinusoidal RMS screen** requiring simultaneous application-qualified AC peak voltage, RMS branch currents, dV/dt, frequency and temperature limits. Its bound is the minimum of branch-current/tolerance sharing,2πfCminVac,peak/√2 andCmin(dV/dt)max/√2. Switching waveforms still require direct per-capacitor RMS, peak and slew comparisons; this formula does not admit an arbitrary waveform. Missing data, frequency mismatch or excess temperature yields no permission. With independent capacitance tolerances the limiting share of either220nF part is242/(242+198+90)=0.456604; do not use nominal40.74% sharing. The code's positive test fixture is synthetic; its600V AC peak is not a CDE rating.

The selected942C table's1200V DC rating and430Vrms60Hz label do not release a50kHz operating point. Its10.3/9.2A100kHz70°C row cannot be used as an unqualified50kHz limit. [Existing exact-part record](../../../../../docs/research/mit-product-design/readiness/power/capacitor-limits.md) retains the actual source. Reachable-state simulations can show which limits are demanded; they cannot manufacture an unavailable AC application limit.

## Lower-resistance replacement PC12.5

A separate tested candidate uses **two ARCOL/Ohmite HS400 25R J in parallel**,12.5Ω nominal,11.875–13.125Ω with ±5% tolerance. It preserves a low resistance compatible with the filter's reactive current while changing to a family with an explicit single-pulse overload specification. Nominal140V joined startup passes the existing crest checks; see the retained `pc125` run, not the rejected PC88 run.

The manufacturer-authored [HS400–600 sheet, pp1–2, distributor-hosted copy](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/303/acl_hs400_600-3082597.pdf) identifies25Ω as a standard value,400W with heatsink and a10×power/5s single-pulse characteristic. It also has a resistance-dependent adiabatic-pulse graph. Text was inspected; graph pixels could not be retrieved, so that graph is **not verified**. The series'400W rating cannot be transferred to the old HS200.

At 140Vrms and23.75Ω per resistor, each sees825.263W RMS,1650.526W instantaneous peak and412.632J over500ms. A deliberately cold≤25°C initial-mount admission is the candidate first-prototype condition; the old60°C condition is not carried over. Do not interpret the10×/5s label alone as mounted hot/repeated-pulse qualification. The new182mm-long bodies need new mounting/guard drawings, a suitable heat spreader, independently coupled TCOs and renewed25Ω±5% POST. This proposal remains unadopted until those exact application conditions and the graph are verified. The source circuit still owns the original two22Ω HS200 parts.

## Concrete integration changes and unresolved coordination

1. Retain zero nonzero-power admission until the exact50kHz/hot capacitor limits and measured or independently bounded pan envelope exist. Do not import a45A or85A study threshold as permission.
2. Add correlated bus/catch charge proof before RUN and monitor it in READY/RUN. The supervisor/firmware peers adopted a separate `catch_charge_proven` predicate; its ADC algorithm, response delay and error budget still need implementation and proof. An open catcher cannot be detected by VCATCH<250V alone.
3. Do not install PC88 under the existing admission contract. PC12.5 is a concrete alternative component selection requiring new182mm mounts, cold-start condition, individual TCOs and exact overload/mount validation. The original11Ω downstream-short screen remains about900J in500ms; the historical joined470.458ms corner dissipated792J, not a protective pass. PC12.5's minimum-resistance joined corner dissipated735J total; neither value establishes a hot resistor rating.
4. Require a documented DC interruption/let-through envelope for FWP-10A14F plus holder, actual loopL/R and initial diode temperature. Match the prospective fault to the vendor's specified test waveform before claiming hot-diode or bridge survival. Source fault impedance is a diagnostic assumption here, not the installation maximum.
5. Add independent containment for a shorted catch capacitor's local stored energy. At250V and51.7µF maximum capacitance that energy is1.616J; the capacitor-to-local-short loop can avoid the upstream fuse. Bus-fed fault energy is additional.
6. Specify source-contactor making/breaking coordination and resistor-bypass weld/rail sequencing against the completed supervisor. The historical shorted precharge resistor probe reached337.85V bus and338.98V catch before delayed isolation despite zero tank RUN; initial admission alone cannot suppress that transient. MOV/TVS, real contact arcs and saturation are absent, so these are conditional diagnostics requiring improved device modeling.

Remaining supplier inputs are the exact capacitor application envelope, AUX inrush waveform/tolerance, hot mounted resistor overload curve, DC fuse/holder coordination and contactor making/breaking data. Remaining **digital work** is the nonlinear device/catcher fault model with numerical convergence, executable closed-loop feedback and analog supervisor proof, waveform-specific capacitor checks, conductor/termination energy limits and protection-chain timing. These are distinct from physical coil/pan characterization and prototype correlation.
