# Native19 / R4 cooling interface revision

**Cold engineering study; not a machining or powered-build release.** This revision preserves the R4 shell outline and front controls and imports all 142 references from the actual native19 STEP. It replaces the old disconnected cooling allocation with a contact-bearing sink, a chassis-mounted carrier and enclosed push/pull air passages. The former fixed 0.685 mm bridge boss is removed.

## Decisions implemented

- Each power device gets an individually measured, single-piece copper shim on the live side of a fully supported ceramic tile. The four MOSFETs need not share a rear plane, and BR1 need not match them. Install and gauge the unsoldered devices before soldering; do not use the clamps to pull soldered leads into position.
- Sink base front is local Y−0.100; the nominal 1 mm ceramic front is local Y0.900. Correct drawing bounds put the MOS rear at 1.170–1.825 and BR1 at 1.300–1.800. Available shim-plus-film thickness is therefore 0.270–0.925 and 0.400–0.900 mm respectively. These are geometric intervals, not procurement thicknesses. The STEP shows midpoint placeholders, explicitly named as such.
- The MOS contact allocation is moved up into the drawing-bounded rear metal and relieved around the mounting hole. At the proposed 5 mm body seating height, the guaranteed vertical metal interval from D/D1/D2 limits is Z44.910–56.350. A 12×10 mm contact from Z45.3 has a 5.5×5.5 relief, leaving 89.75 mm² nominal. Metal centring, the complete mounting-hole tolerance, seating and actual pressure witness must be checked. BR1's 24×14 rectangle remains provisional. These areas cannot be substituted into a qualified thermal resistance.
- Boyd Max03NG is the MOS clip candidate: the manufacturer's packet shows 15 mm width, 0.5 mm strip, 14.7 mm anchor/contact span and 45 N nominal; the family is described as 30–50 N. Independent adjustable anchor fixtures are required. The free profile and force-versus-installed-stack curve are missing, so no fabricated surrogate clip is presented as the selected part. BR1 needs its own mount; its drawing hole is 3.10–3.40 mm despite a textual #6 recommendation. An M3 low-head screw fits that diameter nominally, but its washer/bushing/torque process is not released.
- Two DC0602512W2B-BT0 fans surround the 200 mm custom sink, with closed side ducts and isolated sleeves around the four R4 legs. One fan pushes and one pulls through the same sink. Nine new rear-right intake slots separate intake from the retained rear-left outlet. The geometry is no longer an open rear sink disconnected from the devices.
- Four insulating edge shoes retain the actual board with one left X datum, one front Y datum, and 1 mm nominal positive-X/Y float. The final native19 saved-fill screen supports all four contacts and both corner strips at zero extra copper margin. This is not an insulation margin. Two rails attach to the floor with four M3 screws; a separate sink cradle uses four M4 floor screws and four horizontal M3 sink screws. Thread engagement, preload, shock and thermal growth still need mechanical qualification.
- D22's filter body plan is packed within its 110×80×50 allocation; carrier and cover are represented separately from the component bodies. The LPSC0001Z holder sits outside this volume on a TS35/7.5 rail allocation. The Schurter 4435.0002 rear cutout is 34.1×26.1 nominal, within its 34(+0.2/0)×26(+0.2/0) drawing. Its conservative external actuator envelope projects 7.2 mm beyond the rear shell, to world Y447.2; this is an explicit new rear hardware projection. Its body is 33.8 mm wide; the drawing's 21.3 mm dimension is terminal spacing, not body width.
- Dedicated main and sink-cradle PE studs and a routed bond allocation are included. Removable duct/carrier fasteners are not the sole PE connection. The long bond route invalidates any inherited 100 nH coupling assumption.

## Contact assembly and acceptance

Use sink land A as the depth datum, carrier lips B as board-height datum and the left/front stops C/D for X/Y. Measure each actual ceramic front and each freely seated device rear independently. Let `g_i` be the measured gap, `t_i` the lapped shim thickness and `f_i` the total installed film thickness across the added interfaces. Require `g_i − t_i − f_i` to lie in the interface supplier's permitted compressed range, including gauge uncertainty, flatness, ceramic/shim tolerance, hot differential growth and reassembly error. **That permitted range and film process are not yet supplied.** A negative worst-case gap is interference, not beneficial preload.

The 5 mm seating proposal keeps the widened MOS lead section (L1 up to 4.50 mm) and GBJ shoulder (D up to 4.20 mm) above the board nominally. The exact holes, lead straightness and lead insertion must be gauged. Infineon requires lead support between a bend and the body, bending beyond the widened region (otherwise at least 2.5 mm from the body) and at least 0.5 mm bend radius. There is no new lead bend in the current STEP. Do not force a lead-form correction using the PCB as the forming die.

Fit each shim to a numbered device/position, witness the contact, gauge clamp force, then solder while the fixture preserves alignment. Remove flux and inspect the lead exit. Recheck force/contact after one removal/reinstallation and thermal cycling. Keep live shims captive and away from the PE sink except through the qualified dielectric stack. Nominal adjacent MOS shim separation is 6 mm and minimum lateral tile overhang is 4 mm; these are reported dimensions, **not approved clearance/creepage limits**. The actual insulation design must set required distances and edge barriers.

The imported native19 package poses remain unchanged. Its generic MOS rear is local Y1.515 and provisional BR1 rear is Y2.200; the latter falls outside the corrected drawing interval. Visible midpoint-shim gaps in that model are not contact validation. The corrected drawing bounds and measurable assembly procedure take precedence over those generic surfaces.

## Assembly, service and electrical boundaries

Build the carrier, sink cradle, ceramic/shim/device fixture and PCB as a removable subassembly. Install the horizontal sink screws before the fan/duct assemblies. With power removed, discharge verified, harnesses disconnected and the top/coil assembly removed, support the PCB/sink subassembly before undoing its underside floor screws. Floor screw heads remain above the foot contact plane. Do not pull the sink from soldered devices; semiconductor replacement requires the controlled unsolder/fixture sequence. Fit fans/ducts afterward, then install the coil carrier/top. The fuse door's conservative open projection reaches Z97.5 and requires top/coil-carrier removal; this is not front-user service. The rail clip datum and complete door sweep still need the manufacturer model or a physical sample.

The modified STEP reports every omitted historical chamber and every changed R4 part. Removing the old PCB chamber does not establish an electrical enclosure: a new PCB barrier/cover, connector exits and inspection access remain required. The filter cover is an insulating-material allocation, not a specified recognized material. No wire bends, selected FASTON crimps, cord gland, mains transition or precharge resistor/relay are modeled. The original cord opening is blank-covered. The electrical team's new precharge requirement is explicitly **not integrated**; its selected hardware must return through the packaging check.

The filter's stated 8 W excludes fuse, rocker and precharge losses. Keep choke ambient at or below its qualified limit (current allocation 60°C), away from sink exhaust. The rocker has a 60°C ambient limit; its typical contact resistance is not a worst-case heat allowance. Neither the common 125 V cord nor unapproved fine-stranded cord terminations may be assumed suitable for a 140 V laboratory corner test.

## Thermal and airflow gate

The required allocations remain sink ≤0.15°C/W, MOS case-to-sink interface ≤1°C/W, conditional bridge interface 0.45°C/W, 50°C **at the fin inlet**, and ≥20 CFM delivered. The custom 200 mm sink has no obtained supplier thermal/pressure curve. A stock 305 mm datasheet does not qualify this cut geometry. Each fan has 55.5 CFM free-air and 1.17 inH₂O shutoff endpoints; neither endpoint establishes a 20 CFM operating point. Series operation adds pressure at common flow, not free-air CFM. The catalog gives 11.4 W per fan, but the exact manufacturer product page gives 6.48 W and an operating ambient range of −10..70°C. Keep 22.8 W total as a conservative steady supply allocation pending supplier revision confirmation; it is not a reconciled nominal rating. Use a dedicated supply with checked startup/stall/derating limits. At 20 CFM, a 50°C fin inlet and assumed air density1.092 kg/m³ / heat capacity1005 J/(kg·K), an ideal bulk-air rise to the pull fan’s 70°C limit corresponds to about207 W of sink heat. This is a necessary screening calculation with no hot-spot or recirculation margin, not a qualified allowable dissipation; the current loss budget must be reconciled before retaining that exhaust fan.

The narrow leg bypasses, bends, slots, seals and sink must be measured as an assembled pressure/flow system. Seal the rear mates and fan flanges; the CAD mate gaps are not leak tests. Exact fan mounting-hole drawings, flange fasteners, duct attachments and gasket compression remain unreleased; the STEP fan bodies and custom duct shells are packaging allocations. The sampled leg-bypass areas are 1015.005 mm² left and 899.005 mm² right. At the requested 20 CFM they imply 9.30 and 10.50 m/s, respectively; at assumed 50°C air density 1.092 kg/m³ their dynamic pressures are 47.22 and 60.19 Pa, before loss coefficients, sink resistance or fan operating curves. These are conditional flow calculations, not delivered-flow evidence. The push fan's own heat raises inlet temperature, so measure fin inlet downstream of it. Blocked inlet, stalled fan, one failed fan, leakage, recirculation and fan supply loss must enter protection testing. Existing board-only conductor simulations do not qualify the new coil/sink/chassis capacitive or magnetic coupling.

## Evidence and next release decision

`interface.json` is the integration handoff, pinning the exact native19 board/STEP, saved-fill screen and corrected package bounds. `build_revision.py` preserves the imported part identities and checks nominal positive-volume intersections, air obstacles and connected sink solids. Output `checks.json` distinguishes new/native, new/R4, native/R4 and new/new results; intended thread intersections are reviewed explicitly. All dimensions remain nominal until a tolerance/process drawing is released.

This work supports a cold fixture and engineering review. Before powered assembly: obtain the exact clip profile/force curve and samples; choose interface film and dielectric/edge-retention process; gauge the five rear planes and lead seating; qualify sink/duct/fans against the thermal allocation; finish PCB barriers, D22 precharge and inlet wiring; then rerun the integrated STEP and insulation review. Build and inspect one instrumented unit before building its siblings.

## Reproduce the receipts

Run `build_revision.py` with the existing CadQuery environment, then `check_revision.py` with Python 3 and `plot_revision.py` with Matplotlib. No new dependency installation is required. The checker rejects source/STEP drift and any unexpected intersection; its 16 allowed pairs are individual modeled thread engagements with analytically checked volumes. `verification.json` pins the reviewed source files; generated STEP files are excluded from publication by the output directory’s `.gitignore`. `sources.json` records primary-source URLs and downloaded PDF hashes; the raw PDFs are not included.
