# Cooling correction for physically installable native19 orientation

This is a cold engineering candidate, not fabrication or energized-operation approval. The populated native19 STEP is rotated 180° about Z and translated by (129.5,162,30.387) mm. It is never reflected. Historical reflected assemblies remain preserved and superseded for installation geometry.

The existing custom 200×68×60 mm sink moves +36 mm in X to −76…124. This retains its fin section and length, covers all five heat sources and moves both existing 60×60×25 mm fan envelopes to X−101…−76 and124…149. Both front duct transitions change; rear openings and leg sleeves stay. This custom sink still has no qualified thermal or pressure curve. The previous ≤0.15°C/W sink, ≤1°C/W MOS interface, ≥20 CFM delivered and 50°C fin-inlet conditions remain unverified requirements. Keeping the old section is not thermal equivalence of the installed air path.

Newly manufactured symmetric ceramic tiles, PEEK shoes and rails receive Xnew=19−Xold. This is a machining definition for custom parts, not a proposed reflection of a real populated board. Labels L/R retain their *native board* identity. Native L20/L140 now sit on world right; native R20/R80 sit on world left. The original checked native copper strips are retained exactly. A fresh `check_native_contacts.py` uses KiCad effective pad shapes, track bounding boxes and whole saved fill polygons on both outside copper layers; it writes the native and rigid-world rectangles. It checks zero added clearance and does not qualify insulation. The right carrier's outboard edge is trimmed from X141.5 to140.5, leaving0.5 mm to the existing right wall. X datum is now the right edge;1 mm nominal X growth allowance points left, and Y float remains toward the rear. Accept only if differential thermal growth, manufacturing tolerance and service deflection fit inside the measured available float; vertical warp/thickness remain independent.

The earlier STEP had no installed MOS clamps. This candidate adds six individually adjustable C frames machined from6061: four for MOS packages and two for BR1. Each top anchor screws vertically into the sink base; a horizontal M3 pressure screw pushes a captive PEEK pad onto the package front. A1.5×8 mm dowel provides positive frame antirotation; the PEEK pad has two side arms and a rear retaining bridge around the pressure screw, allowing the pad to remain captured when unloaded. The pad, package, live copper shim, ceramic and sink form the compression path. These are custom engineering fixtures, not simulated Boyd Max03NG clips. No safe screw torque, clamp force, PEEK grade/creep limit, vibration lock or ceramic stress limit has been established. Both BR1 screws must be adjusted together while checking flat seating. Do not use clamp force to pull already-soldered leads into alignment.

The MOS source-model plastic rear is at worldY163.515 but its large rear metal plane isY163.530, so the explicit CAD contact proxy is0.630 mm thick beyond the ceramic faceY162.9. The BR1 source model needs1.3 mm. **Neither is a shim purchase dimension. The MOS proxy uses a7.4 mm square relief centered on the source ring atZ51.1; its model-only metal contact is65.24 mm². The old smaller relief contacted plastic while leaving a15 µm gap to this model’s metal, so bounding-box contact was insufficient.** Manufacturer drawing intervals remain MOS rearY163.170…163.825 and BR1 rearY163.300…163.800 for the earlier straight-lead assumptions, giving shim-plus-film ranges0.270…0.925 and0.400…0.900 mm. In particular, the generic bridge model does not meet that seating interval. Gauge actual unsoldered parts and lead seating; use single measured shims and recheck the whole clamp stack. Drawing-bound rear-metal coverage, lead form, film thickness, flatness, ceramic edge creepage and dielectric withstand remain physical/design holds. A nominal zero CAD gap does not close those holds.

Fan frames are captured by two rear top caps per fan and four separately floor-mounted posts. This avoids inventing manufacturer mounting-hole coordinates. The cap touches only the top outer-frame region in the60 mm body envelope. Exact frame rib geometry, allowed frame compression, axial/lateral retention under fan vibration, wire exit and gasket compression need actual fan drawing/sample confirmation. Fans are still vendor body envelopes, not fully modeled impellers or connector exits. Dedicated fan power and stall/startup protections remain electrical requirements.

`build_correction.py` exports a named replacement STEP and an exact removal list. The packaging owner must remove every listed old shape before inserting the replacements, start from a NEW floor blank, discard the eight obsolete cooling holes and combine the supplied drilling list with other owners' holes, and cut fresh sense-wire ports into the revised barrier/duct shapes. The left barrier starts atY156; the front barrier steps around the changed duct and has explicit0.2 mm fitting reliefs around sink and anchor penetrations. These reliefs need a retained rated sealing/insulation detail; the CAD does not claim a finished IP or electrical enclosure. The right wall stays in place. The shifted rail holes overlap old hole positions by1 mm; this is a new floor fabrication, not an instruction to elongate holes in an existing floor.

Assembly: remove the top glass/coil support and chamber roof for vertical clamp-anchor access. Set the devices and shims against the sink before soldering. Straight pressure-screw access is blocked by C5/PS1/PS2 in the populated board; establish and inspect the preload before those obstructing parts are fitted. A later service operation needs the sink/board subassembly withdrawn and, for BR1, the obstructing component or a separately qualified short tool removed/selected. No claim of in-place retorque access is made. After any disturbance, repeat contact witness, lead-strain, isolation and force checks before energization.

Checks distinguish source model contact, material collisions, intentional thread overlaps and access collisions. Positive tool intersections are retained as assembly-sequence evidence. Thermal, pressure/flow, contact-force, electrical insulation, PE continuity and complete assembly tolerance tests have not happened; no hardware exists yet. Packaging integration must recheck the latest catch and sense wiring after inserting this correction.

The new dowel and captive-pad geometry establish positive retention but do not qualify their shear/bearing strength, fatigue or hot creep. Fan frame captures need axial/lateral vibration retention qualification; exact fan frame geometry is still missing.

## Dedicated sink protective-earth addendum

The pre-PE frozen revision is preserved in `checkpoints/pre-pe` in the source and output directories. It remains historical evidence, not the current integration input. Shape creation now reads the immutable protection STEP, while collision validation reads the current packaging assembly separately; regenerating after packaging updates cannot apply a second reflection or sink translation.

A dedicated M4 terminal at sink(X−66,Y157.9,Z72) connects to the existing cradle PE stud(X−128,Y160). Mask a10×8 mm bare-metal contact pad from anodizing/paint, deburr and clean it, and mark the earth symbol and `PE` beside it. The stack is external-tooth washer, fused copper end pad, flat washer, spring washer and an independent M4×12 screw tapped into the sink. It has no cooling-mount function. Washer shapes are maximum envelopes; exact tooth/spring preload and torque are still procurement/qualification inputs.

The custom jumper specification is fine-strand tinned copper braid, minimum2.5 mm² effective copper, maximum4×1.5 mm free section, and fused10×7×1 mm copper end pads with4.3 mm M4 holes. This cross-section is a **candidate dimensional requirement**, not an established ampacity or protective-conductor sizing result. The routing STEP uses a conservative4.4 mm swept section,3 mm centerline bends, and1 mm flattened fused tails at the terminal pads. Supplier bend/twist capability, strand construction, pad-fusion quality and actual fit must be confirmed. No fault-current or clearing-time withstand is claimed.

At the cradle, a conductive spacer supports the original braid’s modeled16 mm contact plane. A second spacer supports the new jumper above it, followed by a flat washer, external-tooth locking washer and M4 nut. The existing dedicated stud needs a qualified permanent welded attachment to the cradle; its cylindrical CAD model does not qualify the weld. Bare-pad corrosion compatibility and contact preservation through thermal cycling remain open.

Two floor-fixed PEEK guide halves permit assembly around the jumper after its end pads are fused. Their lower guide captures two swaged copper stop collars, providing an explicit axial load path to the cradle instead of placing cable pulls directly on the electrical terminals. Swage pull strength, guide hot creep, thin-wall strength and screw retention remain unqualified. Added floor holes belong in the new floor drawing. A small insulating wall recess accommodates the cradle terminal stack; the final material/sealing process is still unreleased.

The sink terminal uses vertical Allen access; the cradle nut uses the recorded thin-wall socket allocation. Remove the chamber roof and top assembly for the vertical tools. The cradle socket additionally requires removal of the detachable left and front chamber panels; torque and inspect the PE stack before those panels are installed. The split wall bushing allows panel removal around the existing braid. Raw tool intersections remain in the receipt; no in-place torque access through installed panels is claimed. Final acceptance still requires measured low-resistance PE continuity, the applicable fault-current withstand test, verified permanent-stud attachment, torque/locking process, corrosion and temperature cycling, and adequate separation from live circuitry. None of these has been tested on hardware.
