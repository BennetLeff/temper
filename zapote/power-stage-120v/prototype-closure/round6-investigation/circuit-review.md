# Round 6 joined-circuit and protection investigation

**Disposition: engineering-prototype investigation only; no fabrication or powered-test release.** This review uses the round 5 central board identified by SHA-256 `f307cdd9c75840c6b90c42dadd8eeb334c827fefedf520cd4b79076bdc8c9221`, its eight local sensor boards, `PC125-CATCH-R5-2`, and the unchanged native19 power board. The checkpoint records 728 electrical components on nine auxiliary boards and three clean DRC samples per board. Those checks establish routed connectivity against the generated pin tables, not that the electrical functions, insulation rules, contact interruption, or fault energy are correct. No assembled hardware exists. FPGA work is outside the product architecture. The old 45 A assumption is not an operating permission.

I traced the source-derived pin tables at the joined controller, ADC/sensor, supervisor, contactor and proof interfaces, and compared the fault assumptions with the round 5 protection/model receipts. This is a focused review of the consequential paths; the generated central schematic is split into typed-pin blocks and still needs the readable system drawing described below. Previously corrected R5I-001 through R5I-020 findings are not reopened here.

## Circuit path checks and limits of the present evidence

* **Start/reset/stop and source:** J1/J2/J3 feed 3.3 V through a switch into biased `POD_START`, `POD_RESET`, `POD_STOP`; `POD_STOP` enters the hardware health chain at U28 pin 1 (`pins.tsv:509`) and STM32 PC2 (`:1300`). The watchdog output reaches STM32 NRST (`:1295`) and U28 pin 2 (`:510`). The R5I reset/STOP_DONE and one-token fixes have executable regression receipts. Remaining unknowns are rail ramp/backfeed, input bounce, latch setup/recovery and the total detection-to-current-extinction interval, especially when a contact welds or a semiconductor shorts. Logic low at a gate is not current extinction.
* **Controller boundary:** J9 is the ten-wire isolated interface; U98 passes `SUP_RUN_OK` toward the controller, U100/U101 return PWM request and heartbeat, U102 returns active-high controller fault, and U107 passes `NATIVE_PERMIT` to J10. The `ISO7710F` channels default low on input-side supply/signal loss; the deliberately non-F U102 defaults high for active-high fault, matching the observed polarity. This verifies the selected suffix intent only, not installed power sequencing, common-mode behavior or signal propagation. [TI ISO7710 data](https://www.ti.com/product/ISO7710).
* **Sensors and ADC:** each local voltage card uses an eight-by-249 kΩ / 2.49 kΩ 801:1 divider, AMC3330 input pins 6/7 and low-side differential output pins 11/10; its J2 pins 1–6 carry 3.3 V, return, P, N, active-low diagnostic and return (`boards/bus-pins.tsv:1–62`, corresponding VPRE card). Central J18–J21 preserve that order, use 10 kΩ/10 kΩ per-leg input attenuation and send paired channels to ADS131M08 (`central/generated/pins.tsv:87–105, 424–433, 1379–1402`). The VPRE 801:1 change is already the R5I-016 correction. AMC diagnostics indicate device supply state, not a correct divider or live measured value; open top resistors, frozen in-range channels, cable capacitance/settling and calibration remain credible faults. The target must reject missing/stale/discordant measurements using an actual injected fault matrix.
* **Physical pin semantics:** the six SN74LVC1G74 DCU mappings were corrected in round 5. The source-derived pin table, XML/netlist parity and 27 native DRC passes do not test manufacturer pin behavior or whether an active-low signal becomes safe during supply loss. The controller rail-direction discrepancy above is a direct example of that limitation. The remaining readable review should compare every safety-critical part's physical pin number/function and off-rail behavior against its manufacturer document, then link the result to a revision-locked joined schematic.

## Smallest review package that can close the digital circuit review

1. One readable, revision-locked joined schematic/netlist package for native19, all nine auxiliary boards and external contactor/fuse/capacitor assemblies. Mark live, AUX, CTRL, PE and accessible boundaries; number every harness contact from board and mating faces; show actual K1/K2/KB/KPA/KPB/KT pole and coil paths. Include a pin-number/function/rail audit for safety-critical devices and resolve JCTRL semantics.
2. A one-page state/path matrix for OFF, cold POST, START, precharge, both loaded proofs, RUN, zero demand, STOP, brownout and fault. For each, record source contacts, isolation contacts, bypass, gate permission, stored-energy path, sensing evidence and the condition that really extinguishes current. Run explicit single-fault variants for welded/open contacts, lost mirror excitation, open/frozen sensor, stuck gate and capacitor-local short.
3. A bounded coordination appendix that imports exact fuse/contact/resistor/diode/capacitor supplier limits and extracted installed L/R/coupling, with assumptions and units visible. Keep simulation and supplier data separate from physical measurements. The applicable product insulation basis and first-unit measurement plan are separate required release artifacts.

The next executable digital task is to generate a joined component-pin/net comparison and manually annotate the above state/path matrix from the native artifacts, then review it against manufacturer pin tables. In parallel, prepare the exact-corner data packets for Eaton, Schneider, Ohmite and Sensience; the questions above are drafts, and no supplier has been contacted. After supplier data and geometry extraction, rerun the joined model with bounded DC interruption and pulse histories, preserving its frozen historical receipts. Physical closure then requires a staged first-unit inspection and controlled captures of rail ramp, mirror/contact timing, four gate pins, resistor temperature, proof pulses, stop/fault current and insulation. Successful digital checks alone do not authorize making several units.
