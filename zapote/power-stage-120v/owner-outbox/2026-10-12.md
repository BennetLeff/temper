> Hello,
>
> We use the CSS2H-2512R-1L00F (1 mΩ) as an overcurrent sense shunt in a 120 V induction-heating power stage. It dissipates 0.78 W continuous (0.88 W worst case), and our thermal model puts the terminal pads at about 135 °C. We have three questions:

> 3. **Thermal resistance.** What is the element-to-terminal thermal resistance (K/W), or the hot-spot temperature rise at rated power with the terminals held at a fixed temperature?
>
> Thank you.

## 2. TDK: B32654A1104K000 (resonant bank)

**To:** TDK Electronics film capacitors, product support (tdk-electronics.tdk.com, "Contact" / product inquiry)
**Subject:** B32654A1104K000: single-event over-voltage capability above 1600 V DC

> Hello,
>
> We use five B32654A1104K000 (0.1 µF, 1600 V DC / 500 V AC) in parallel as the resonant capacitor of a 25–60 kHz series-resonant induction-heating tank. In normal service each capacitor sees 484 V peak.
>
> In a multiple-fault study, the capacitors can see up to 1702 V peak, which is 1.064 × V_R DC. The waveform is a near-sinusoidal 24.7 kHz voltage with dV/dt at or below 345 V/µs. In the worst case it can last for seconds, until a thermal protection stops the bridge, because the current sits just under the overcurrent trip.
>
> 1. What over-voltage capability does this part have above V_R DC, as a peak voltage and duration at about 25 kHz, for a rare fault event?
> 2. Does the 1.6 × V_R, 2 s factory test (datasheet p.35) imply any non-destructive margin we may rely on for a few such events over the product's life?
>
> Thank you.

Evidence: `validation-results/02-protection-timing/bank-ov-double-fault/` (results.json), DECISIONS 13. This doesn't block anything: the design already holds the case below 1600 V with the 35 kHz floor. The 1702 V case needs a third, independent fault.
