# Round 5 review (Claude, 2026-09-28)

Review of the round-5 handoff at `51a48c26b`. Recommendations here are not
owner approvals.

## Port contract (answers the coordinator's question)

ROUND-5.md's "six ports" was an error in the spec: each leg has a
high-side and a low-side gate circuit. ROUND-5.md now specifies **eight
ports per leg**: four power ports (each local capacitor pair tied as one
port, with the solved current split reported) plus a drive and a return
port for each of the two gate circuits. The owner can still choose
otherwise.

## Corrections accepted

- "A narrower conductor errs high" was overstated: it tends to raise an
  isolated self inductance but doesn't bound the coupled matrix or the
  waveform. ROUND-5.md is corrected.
- The related-board rebuild claim (O11) was wrong; the `FlashLayer` census
  shows no missing outer lands on the RTD, thermal-sense or current-sense
  candidates. The native-17 README was corrected by the coordinator.
- "Vias tented both sides" in the fab note is now "353 of 354 via sides".

## D1: recommendation

Two rounds of FastHenry raster meshing have stopped at the same place:
hundreds to thousands of candidate edges that leave the copper, before any
board solve. The next step proposed (a custom native-conforming mesher) is a
tool-building project with no guarantee of convergence either.

**Recommend taking D1 off the critical path.** The switching questions end
on the bench (the D1 README's own item 3), and the bench procedure now
extracts the commutation-loop inductance directly from ringing at two
snubber values (BENCH-SWITCHING.md, review addition 3). The staged
50/100/170 V procedure, with its 520 V and 3.0 V stops, is what keeps the
first board safe, not a pre-computed matrix. D2 can then be run against the
measured loop inductance as a calibration, not a prediction.

If a pre-build number is still wanted, time-box one attempt with a tool
built for arbitrary PCB geometry instead of a hand-built mesh: openEMS via
Antmicro's gerber2ems (Gerbers plus stackup to an FDTD model with ports),
reading the loop inductance from the port impedance at 1–30 MHz. Stop at the
time box and don't block the board on it.

## Bench procedure review

The procedure is sound and appropriately conservative (no mains,
independent inhibit, isolated probes, staged voltage, hard stops,
discharge verification). Three additions are in BENCH-SWITCHING.md:
1. T1 is in the fixture's current path: keep each shot at or below 320 V·µs
   of CT secondary (half the 638 V·µs rating), use a 100–250 µH fixture
   inductor, and allow at least 10 ms between shots.
2. The shunt OCP's worst-case band (38.4–85.6 A) overlaps the ≤37 A
   campaign: capture BUS_FAULT and U6's output, and record any trip current
   as a measurement of the real threshold (evidence for held item B).
3. Extract the commutation-loop inductance from the ringing frequency at
   two snubber values.

## What the owner decides next

To get from here to a measured answer, the path is a **prototype build
for bench bring-up only**: bare boards from the native-17 candidate package,
leg A and its driver hand-assembled, a DC bench supply at no more than
170 V, no mains. That needs:
- **O4:** the JLCPCB CTI answer (for the eventual mains board; also
  settles which laminate these prototypes use);
- a DECISIONS.md entry scoping the release to bench prototypes, since its
  full fabrication gates (thermal and insulation qualification) aren't
  closed and shouldn't be for this purpose;
- **O1:** a temporary heatsink and insulating pad for the bench (few pulses,
  so little heat, but the PE-bonded heatsink insulation still matters);
- the fixture parts: a 100–250 µH non-saturating inductor, isolated probes,
  the auxiliary 15 V and SELV harness, and the independent inhibit.

The 50 sourcing candidates for 57 BOM lines matter for a full assembly;
a leg-A bench build needs far fewer, so qualify those first.
