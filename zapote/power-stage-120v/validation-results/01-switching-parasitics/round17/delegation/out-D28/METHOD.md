# D-28 method and frozen acceptance rules

Base revision: `6717013f7ffcd7f880dc750a0579ef89059c480f`. Only this output directory is edited. `physics.rs` is a standalone Rust calculation compiled directly with `rustc`; no workspace or native bridge is built. Python transports files and invokes the existing D-22 numerical qualification functions. The Infineon library and its download archive stay in ignored `vendor/`; `fetch_models.sh` checks the same full archive/library SHA-256 values as the repository fetcher. See `fetch.txt`.

## Current and phase convention

A_H rises at phase zero; B_H rises at positive separation δ (0–180 degrees). Positive tank current flows from `swa` through R, L and C toward `swb`. At A rising/falling transitions, the desired current sign is negative/positive; at B rising/falling it is positive/negative. The current which discharges the incoming device's capacitance is reported as positive `current_inductive_A`. A enters the active interval from freewheeling; B ends the active interval. This identifies the vulnerable freewheel-to-active commutation without relying on ambiguous leading/lagging names. Source circuit: [D-19 periodic deck](../out-D19/periodic-sweep.cir#L19).

The ideal calculation uses 70 µH and 0.54 µF, with both D-22 source resistances (2 Ω and 100 Ω). Frequency is 50 kHz, the prototype's inhibited bench configuration at the base revision, `prototype-closure/round5/firmware/esp32/mcpwm_target.c:88`. It is not a validated heating setting. Only the frequency is taken from that initializer; the controller-gap requirement is 200 ns ([DECISIONS.md:68](../../../../../DECISIONS.md#L68)), while this inherited switching deck uses a nominal 443 ns ideal source-command gap to represent the driver dead time. The prototype cleanup/gap conformance change is handled separately by D-29. Bus values are 170/198 V as requested; each ideal curve has a 1-degree grid from 0 to 180. This freezes the tank and excludes line-bus ripple, pan variation, semiconductor losses, device capacitance and dead-time distortion.

For state x=[i,vC], A=[[-R/L,-1/L],[1/C,0]], and constant bridge voltage u, the exact segment map is `x(t)=exp(A*t)*(x(0)-[0,u])+[0,u]`. Solve the positive-voltage segment of length δ/(360*f), followed by a zero-voltage segment to half-period, with `x(T/2)=-x(0)`. `physics.rs` handles underdamped and overdamped RLC poles. Load power is the average of R*i² on a 10000-interval half-cycle trapezoid; the other half follows antisymmetry. Reported CT zero delays refer to the next current zero appropriate to each leg, not the same zero assigned to both legs.

The independent ngspice ideal oracle uses voltage sources and the same physical RLC values, without calling the Rust state map. Its first recording missed the exact start-boundary `ia` measurement; `ideal-oracle/` and `ideal-oracle.json` retain that incomplete attempt. `ideal-oracle-v2/` starts recording one period earlier and measures both transition currents and RMS current inside the captured interval. No failed measurement is treated as an agreement.

## Periodic switching campaign

Start with [D-19's periodic deck](../out-D19/periodic-sweep.cir), [D-22's runner](../out-D22/periodic.py), and `.options itl4=100000`. Use the native-19 best **leg-A matrix on both legs**, as requested: [matrix](../../d2/legA-h0-best-n19.matrix.txt). No leg-B extraction is supplied. The legacy-matrix 170 V / 35 kHz / 2 Ω / 0.5 ns / 24-cycle anchor reproduces the matching D-22 periodic reference; it is a separate reproduction, not a claim that different matrices or frequencies are matching conditions.

The first source adaptation gave both legs the same initial command state. It aborted during startup; those cases and interrupted attempts are retained under the `v...` labels and `campaign.json`. The main `complementary.json` adaptation retains D-22's initial A-low/B-high commands. For phases below 180 degrees, B's DIR=1 pulse source is delayed by (δ/360+1/2)/f; its settled rise is δ after A modulo one period. At exactly 180, its delay is zero, matching the original startup. This changes control-waveform timing only. It does not add imposed initial conditions, physical shunts, modified recovery parameters or a supply ramp. `run-startup-attempt.py` preserves the first adaptation's runner.

The main grid includes 0/30/60/90/120/150/180 degrees at both buses with R=2 Ω; R=100 Ω adds 30/90/180 degrees. Nominal maximum step is 1 ns, 40 cycles at 50 kHz. The declared 0.5 ns refinement covers 60/90/120/150/180 degrees at R=2 Ω and 90 degrees at R=100 Ω, both buses. Every attempt has a 1800-second subprocess/monotonic timeout; calendar elapsed time can be longer across host suspension. Failure, timeout, incomplete capture or missing measurement is indeterminate. Retain exact parameters, deck, simulator log, status and any raw capture, even for aborts. The model is the pinned CFD7 L1 MOSFET with D-22's 5 Ω/0.55 Ω driver-output approximation, RG=3.9 Ω, 15 V drive, 1 nF snubbers, 1.06 nH capacitor ESL and 443 ns ideal command gap. This is not a validated UCC21550 gate-threshold timing model; see [D-26 limitations](../out-D26/README.md).

D-22 qualification requires the completed endpoint and less than 0.001 relative RMS change between the last two cycles in both switch nodes and bus current. Then run its original receiver scenarios through `numeric_compare.compare`: significant spectral lines must differ by at most 0.2 dB, and weak complex-line differences must be no more than `10^(0.2/20)-1` times the declared floor. Check both cycle-to-cycle and step refinement. The 28 receiver scenarios produce 56 terminal checks for each comparison. Redirect only output roots to this folder; do not change thresholds. A complete transient alone is labeled `unqualified_complete`, not passing. [D-22 numeric rules](../out-D22/NUMERICS.md), [implementation](../out-D22/numeric_compare.py#L16).

## Switching measurements and limits

Measurements use the penultimate complete period so all four commutation windows fit inside the saved two-period capture. For each device/transition: incoming die VDS at the incoming ideal command (outgoing off+443 ns); outgoing die gate peak over its full off interval (outgoing-off+443 ns to its next on command half a period later), including other-leg disturbances; die VDS over a full period; and sum of both devices' positive terminal-power integral over outgoing-off through incoming-on+0.75 µs. The original narrow commutation-window gate/VDS peaks are retained separately for comparison. Numerical source nodes and 74.4 µΩ internal drain-resistor current inference follow [D-15 waveform replay](../out-D15/analyze.py#L47). The first saved point may follow the capture boundary; only turn-off current can use that first sample within 2 ns. Incoming-VDS samples are strictly inside the capture.

Screens are incoming VDS <=5% of bus (ZVS), outgoing gate <3.0 V and separately <1.9 V, and die VDS <520 V, as used in [D-26](../out-D26/README.md#L54) and the native-19 D2 carryover. The 1.9 V criterion is a hot-threshold **screen on the 27 °C model**, not a hot simulation. The positive-terminal-power overlap integral is an energy proxy, not dissipated switching heat or efficiency. It includes capacitive and conduction effects; do not add it to an unadjusted conduction or diode-loss budget. [D-15 definition](../out-D15/README.md), [D-18 warning](../out-D18/README.md#L16).

The final envelope requires both numerical qualification and all four transitions' switching screens. Sparse passing points cannot prove an unsampled interval or device/tank/temperature tolerances. A CT timing proposal is not a hardware release: CT polarity, comparator/filter delay, hysteresis, timer-capture uncertainty and PWM-to-node timing must be bounded on the real assembly. The [2026-10-03 update policy](../../../../../DECISIONS.md#L68) still requires timer-zero updates, inductive commutation and both legs off for burst entry/exit.

## Bounded follow-up and replay

`boundary.json` adds 100/110 degrees at both buses (0.5 ns), and `upper-boundary.json` adds 125/130 degrees at 170 V. `quarter-step.json` declares 0.25 ns comparisons at 170 V, 90/120 degrees; `candidate-refinement.json` applies that same refinement to the 125-degree candidate. All use the same 40-cycle window and physical parameters. These are finite follow-ups to locate the low-power ZVS boundary, not a license to interpolate an approved interval. No abort is removed. `candidate-refinement.txt` records an initial launcher path error before any solver started; `candidate-refinement-run.txt` is the corrected invocation.

`replay.py` verifies each complete capture's decompressed SHA-256, repeats D-22's measurement, and forwards the saved die waveforms to the final `physics.rs` analyzer. `runs/*/replay/analysis.json` pins that analyzer's source and binary hashes and is the final source for `events.csv`, `captures.csv` and `TABLES.md`. The initial `result.json` remains the original run record. A low incoming voltage alone does not establish inductive commutation: the final ZVS flag also requires positive `current_inductive_A`. The ideal CSV's historical `leading_A`/`lagging_A` column names mean leg A/leg B respectively, as identified by their transitions above.

The last two cases already declared in `refinement.json` were dispatched through `refinement-tail.json` to use idle solver capacity. The original runner sees their immutable per-case cache records and does not launch duplicate solvers. Final campaign indexes are refreshed only after both dispatches finish. The four interrupted startup captures lacked an in-run raw hash; `verification.json` pins their retained bytes at closure and distinguishes those hashes from hashes recorded by the original runner. They remain indeterminate.
