# D8 — measure recovery and actual gate dead time

**Use a native-17 leg-A fixture for S4 and gate timing, and a separate recovery coupon for the 400 V/58.2 A datasheet comparison; neither simulation nor this unexecuted procedure qualifies the board.**

This is a document-only supplement to [BENCH-SWITCHING.md](../../../../../validation-plan/BENCH-SWITCHING.md). Its preparation, isolation, auxiliary/back-feed review, independent inhibit, probe-null checks, full discharge and raw-record requirements remain mandatory. The native-board current ceiling stays **37 A**. The coupon does not authorize raising that ceiling or bypassing board protection. The extension to 198 V is an explicit D8 S4 test condition, reached only after the lower-voltage rows pass; 280 V/fault-current tests remain outside this procedure.

Source baseline: `1f09223516cc37d00f73b5e5e6d33a0197d7a30d`. Before use, record actual assembly identifier, photographs, MOSFET lots, board/netlist/BOM hashes and probe attachment drawing. A different assembly is a new test identity. Resolve missing hardware prerequisites at the hold points below before charging anything. No bench shot or assembled-fixture inspection has been performed.

## Fixture and energy

**Native fixture:** retain fitted 3.9 Ω R10/R12, 10 kΩ hold-offs and 1 nF C12/C13 baseline. Connect an insulated **100 µH inductor in series with a 1 Ω noninductive pulse resistor** from J2 to J10 for Q2 pulsing/Q3 diode recovery. Positive current flows SW_A → T1 → J2 → load → HV_RET; map this to the deck's `IL=-20 A, DIR=0`, rather than guessing from the scope polarity. For the mirror, after discharge move the far load end to J8: Q3 pulses and Q2 diode freewheels. J2 always includes T1. J5 remains externally open; retain the board's resonant bank and disabled leg B, and inspect that path as BENCH-SWITCHING requires. Measure leg-B gates during commissioning; any unexpected switching stops the campaign.

Use an air-core or characterized gapped inductor whose measured incremental L stays within the recorded fixture tolerance through **40 A** (native) or **80 A** (coupon). Construction requirements are design selections: insulated winding/terminals rated at least 1 kV, mechanically restrained winding, guarded leads, no exposed conductive core, verified pulse thermal capability. Select the load resistor for ≥10 J per shot, ≥80 A pulse and ≥1 kV terminal insulation using its manufacturer's pulse-duration curve, not average wattage alone. Record actual L versus current, total series R including winding, leads and resistor, and resistor/inductor temperatures. If no suitably qualified assembly exists, the fixture hold point is open; a nominal component label is insufficient.

Add **100 µF, ≥630 V** pulse-rated fixture reservoir at J8/J10 with measured capacitance, ESR, connection inductance and suitably rated guarded terminals. Keep C38/C39 in place. This is an explicit fixture change: fit its measured impedance in any simulation comparison; it cannot validate the original deck's ideal source merely by matching nominal voltage. The board's nominal bus total is 5.8 µF, so nominal total becomes 105.8 µF. Include all other connected capacitance in the recorded inventory. Charge from a floating isolated DC source through a fused, interlocked charging path, current limit **10 mA**, with a DC-rated contactor isolating the source before a shot. Monitor local voltage until stable after disconnection. The capacitor, not the supply current limit, feeds a switching fault.

The separate coupon uses two identical IPW65R018CFD7 devices in a half bridge, a compact measured commutation loop and **470 µF ≥630 V** reservoir. Put a separate **1 mH/1 Ω** load from switch node to negative rail; pulse the upper device and measure the lower diode. No native PCB, T1, R5 or board protection is involved. Give the coupon its own fast independent gate inhibit, load overcurrent trip **70 A**, supply isolation, discharge and enclosure. Use isolated gate supplies with a 13 V command and equal external gate resistors, initially **730 Ω**, matching D3's starting fixture; 13 V and 730 Ω are D3 choices, not datasheet mandated numbers. The lower device is held off through the equal resistor to source. Tune both resistors together at lower energy to obtain **100 A/µs measured** at current zero; 730 Ω is not a guarantee. Approve device SOA and actual switching energy for its deliberately slow transition before escalating. A failed/unsafe low-energy calibration is a hold, not permission to force the target.

[fixture_arithmetic.py](fixture_arithmetic.py) and [its output](fixture-arithmetic.json) provide transparent RL estimates, not a SPICE model or production engineering rule. With 5 µs freewheel and a nominal 1 µs second pulse, target 20 A immediately before commutation: the first pulse reaches about 21.03 A. Constant-bus first widths are **13.20 µs at 170 V /11.23 µs at 198 V**; estimated droops are **1.54/1.33 V**. The same estimate with only the board capacitors gives **28.03 V** at 170 V and is unsuitable as a constant-bus assumption. At 50 V/20 A even the added reservoir gives about 6.10 V droop: use the low-current commissioning row, not that illustrative high-current setting. Adjust charge and pulse width using measured local voltage/current; target actual pre-edge bus within ±1% and current within ±2%, recording actual values without relabeling them exact.

For the coupon the separate 1 mH/470 µF calculation gives a 158.09 µs ideal ramp, 64.97 A after a 20 µs fully-on second pulse and about 12.72 V droop over the event. Slow gate action changes these estimates. Calibrate to 400 V at the recovery edge, not at initial charge; initial charge must remain below 420 V, whose nominal capacitor energy is 41.45 J (½·470 µF·420²). If the edge target cannot be reached within that charge cap, hold and revise the fixture. The discharge inventory/rating uses the actual initial voltage.

The formula is `t 1=−(L/R)ln(1−I1 R/V)`, with `I1=Iedge exp(R·toff/L)` before allowing for diode drop. Start below the estimate and increase only after reviewing a valid capture. Use a hardware load-current comparator set to **25 A** for the 20 A native campaign, plus a separately enforced maximum pulse width derived from the validated low-energy trace. Both are additional controls; keep native protection live. Never reprogram it to dodge a trip. Limit actual second-pulse current below 25 A; shorten the pulse if necessary. The separate 37 A board ceiling remains an absolute stop, not a target.

The independent comparator trips do not stop current instantly. Before high voltage, measure total detection-to-effective-gate-off latency with the actual resistor/driver, including faults and tolerances. Required fixture limits are≤1 µs native and≤10 µs coupon; verify Lmin≥90 µH native/900 µH coupon and impose charge caps 210 V/420 V respectively. The conservative load-current increment is `ΔI≤Vmax·tresponse/Lmin`: with the 25 A/70 A trips this gives 27.34 A/74.67 A rounded upward. Verify these peaks against inductor ratings and device pulse SOA before charging. If latency or Lmin is unverified or fails, hold; the 730 Ω coupon path may require a different reviewed shutdown design. This estimate applies only to load current through L. It does not bound shoot-through or capacitor fault current: verify separate fast inhibit/fault containment and commutation-loop/device SOA, with enclosure and energy-rated fusing. The gate inhibit cannot undo the first destructive edge.

Nominal reservoir energies are **1.529 J at 170 V, 2.074 J at 198 V**, and **37.600 J at 400 V coupon**. Example post-second-pulse inductor energies are **0.0237 J native 198 V /2.110 J coupon (20 µs command estimate)**. Inventory and rate for measured worst-case capacitance/voltage, not just these nominal values. Use a normally connected on inhibit, DC-rated **2 kΩ discharge network**, rated ≥150 W initial pulse and ≥100 J energy, with a switching element rated ≥1 kV; verify its low-voltage discharge curve before use. Ideal time below 1 V is about 1.12 s native 198 V/5.63 s coupon 400 V, but wait at least **10 s** then meter-check: below 1 V at bus and separately across resonant bank, wait 60 s and recheck, proving meter before/after. The resistor calculation is not proof the contactor closed or the bank is discharged. Isolate/discharge auxiliary rails before touching gates.

The 1 Ω series load makes the **entire final freewheel tail** decay, approximately L/R=100 µs. Integrate T1's secondary voltage throughout ramp, freewheel, second pulse and tail, not only the switching window. The estimate uses `Vsec≈I·3 Ω/100` from BENCH-SWITCHING's T1 source and gives about **72.6 V·µs at 198 V**, below its **320 V·µs** budget. Recalculate using actual burden and measured integral; stop if approaching that budget or if baseline fails to reset. Allow ≥10 ms CT reset and, for this procedure, ≥10 s between manually armed shots with thermal recovery. Never disconnect the load while current remains. The coupon's arithmetic includes a hypothetical CT value only for comparison; it has no T1 requirement. Its 1 mH load requires a longer tail capture: use≥10 ms memory after the second pulse and confirm current zero before re-arm.

## Instruments, channels and calibration

Use one timebase with **at least eight simultaneous analog channels**, ≥1 GHz scope bandwidth, ≥5 GS/s per enabled fast channel, enough memory for the complete pulse plus ≥1 ms decay, and isolated digital channels on the same trigger. Primary capture: Q2/Q3 package VDS, Q2/Q3 package VGS, diode terminal ID, load current, local bus and T1 secondary voltage (coupon uses spare channel). Timing repeats require four gate-related analog channels for U1 inputs/outputs plus both package VGS, while retaining current and local bus. Repeats are separately identified shots; do not splice non-simultaneous events into an overlap claim. Record PERMIT, DIS, BUS_FAULT and actual controller PWM with appropriately isolated channels; logic inputs must not create a HOT-to-SELV connection.

Minimum probe capabilities are **specified requirements**, not claims that any unnamed instrument meets them:

- Two VDS differential probes: ≥200 MHz, ≥1 kV differential and common-mode/terminal-to-earth working capability with the exact accessories, adequate transient rating; insulated tip loops ≤10 mm at each TO-247 drain/source pair. Verify rating at operating frequency and environment.
- Two isolated VGS probes: ≥500 MHz, ≥1 kV common-mode working rating, ≥±30 V differential range, noise plus common-mode residual ≤0.10 V for this screen. Solder-in shielded tips with exposed gate/source loop ≤5 mm; measure source at the device lead, not distant ground. Additional isolated probes for U1 OUTA–VSSA and OUTB–VSSB and input-to-input-ground timing. Never float the scope or attach an earth ground clip to a switch node.
- A calibrated **≥100 MHz terminal-current channel**, range at least ±150 A, no saturation at the measured recovery peak, and a separate load-current channel for the comparator/slow ramp. A Rogowski around one accessible drain conductor avoids a series shunt but has droop/integration/position errors and cannot read DC. A calibrated coaxial CVR can measure terminal recovery accurately but changes loop R/L and needs an isolated differential readout and qualified mechanical installation. Record the added impedance and repeat with a second placement or fixture calibration. If neither can be fitted without unknown parasitics, terminal recovery is **unavailable**. R5's 1 mΩ Kelvin reading is useful for the protection/bus-current record, but is shared return current with uncharacterized high-frequency transfer and cannot substitute for a diode drain-current measurement. An external load sensor likewise cannot resolve the diode's fast reverse current.
- Rated DMM, isolated current-limited supplies, programmable two-pulse generator/controller, independent inhibit/comparator, guarded enclosure/interlock, emergency stop, pulse reservoir/discharger, thermal sensors, calibrated L/R meter, low-energy deskew fixture and rigid probe attachments. Instrument examples and manufacturer documents are in BENCH-SWITCHING's source dossier; validate the actual serial-number/accessory configuration.

Deskew at the selected ranges using a low-energy common edge and known current through the reference shunt. Save raw and corrected traces, gain/offset, delay and uncertainty; require residual channel skew ≤1 ns for the timing comparison. Repeat after any tip/range/channel change. At zero bus verify gate polarity and current zero. Repeat the established source-to-source VGS null test at staged bus conditions; retain null excursions and independently positioned tip captures. Any gate noise above 0.10 V, clipping, uncertain high-frequency CMRR or placement-dependent rebound makes the row indeterminate. Do not average away peaks. Give current integration uncertainty, voltage uncertainty and deskew contribution separately.

## Sequences and extraction

1. Complete BENCH-SWITCHING preparation: no mains cable, remove/secure J7–J8/J9–J10 straps, independently inspect auxiliary PS1/PS2 back-feed, keep R5 in the return, leg B disabled. Prove independent inhibit at 0 V on loss of each supply, heartbeat/PERMIT and fault input. Review T1 burden/reset, reservoir/discharger and insulation before first charge. For Q2 shots first charge bootstrap by a low-side on interval at zero load current, then verify U1.16–U1.14 and its droop through the full Q2 pulse. If the chosen fixture cannot charge it reliably, hold; no improvised supply connection.
2. **Recovery:** command only the incoming switch. First pulse builds current; turn it off for 5 µs so the opposite diode conducts; second pulse initially 1 µs on the native board commutates that diode; turn both off and capture full decay. Diode conduction and measured pre-edge current must be verified. For the slow-gate coupon, start at 1 µs and lengthen only enough to include the descending 10% recovery endpoint, with a hard 20 µs command cap, 70 A load trip, and recorded SOA/pulse-energy review. If no valid capture fits, hold and redesign the fixture; do not extend the pulse cap. D3's ideal-current fixture has no finite ramp; compare the measured commutation window, not equal pulse widths. Raise voltage/current through the run sheet, saving/reviewing every shot before manual re-arm.
3. **Timing and matched S4 carrier history:** retain that load and run a synchronous variant. After building current, turn the outgoing device off, wait the actual controller's gap, turn its partner on briefly to carry the freewheel current, then turn that partner off, wait the gap and turn the original device on. This yields both transition directions with nonzero measured current. For the S4 comparison specifically, let Q3 carry the reverse load current through its channel, turn Q3 off, then turn Q2 on after the measured gap (target 348 ns, plus 307 ns only if supported); mirror for the other direction. This reproduces the outgoing-gate/short-diode-history condition more closely. The separate 5 µs diode-conditioning recovery shot is not an exact D2 S4 replica: recovery charge depends on conduction history. Compare that shot only to a simulation rerun with the same 5 µs conditioning and actual measured fixture. Record both histories separately. Use actual firmware image, SDK build identifier, source hash, configuration/API return codes and pin mapping. Start at requested 500 ns and low energy. Record any intended 307/348 ns request only if actually supported and measured; the hardware DT setting can extend it. Keep the historical 307 ns simulation cases until real bounds are justified.
4. For each edge, record controller-pin gap, U1-pin input gap, driver-output gap and package-gate gap separately. Driver-output dead time uses outgoing 90% falling to incoming 10% rising relative to each output's measured rail levels. TI SLUSE89C §5.9 p 10 specifies propagation input VIH→output 10% and input VIL→output 90%; use these thresholds for those delays, not a blanket 50%-to-50% interpretation. Report package VGS 90%-to-10% and fixed 3 V crossing intervals separately, including every re-crossing/ringing excursion. A3 V crossing is a timing convention, not a guaranteed hot channel threshold. Negative gaps indicate overlap of those voltage definitions; correlate with current rather than equating a voltage crossing with shoot-through.
5. Recovery current polarity: diode forward current is negative drain-to-source; use interpolated terminal-current zero as t 0, positive peak as Irrm, first subsequent descending 10% of that peak as tend. Integrate **positive terminal current t 0→tend** trapezoidally for Qrr; trr=tend−t 0. Also publish unfiltered tail and later ringing separately, `ta=tpeak−t 0`, `tb 10=tend−tpeak`, `tb 10/ta`, zero-crossing slew fit over±20 ns, actual pre-edge current/voltage and temperature. This matches D3's analysis convention. Terminal charge contains displacement current; it is not an isolated measurement of minority-carrier charge. Do not subtract an assumed Coss curve and call the remainder measured diode charge.
6. For the coupon establish 25 °C equilibrium on an insulated controlled-temperature mount, case sensor target 25 ±1 °C, sparse shots, and a documented junction-rise estimate from measured losses and Infineon transient thermal impedance. A case sensor alone does not establish Tj 25 °C. Target 400 V±1%, 58.2 A±2%, 100 A/µs±5% as acquisition bins; compare simulations at actual values. A bin match does not prove a datasheet compliance test. Record actual 13 V gate command, equal resistors and off-gate rebound. Report mismatch instead of normalizing Qrr to a different test point.
7. Retain both native files and lossless CSV, complete scope setup, raw/corrected traces, all hashes and the BENCH-SWITCHING shot metadata. Add flashed-image/SDK hash, pulse controller settings, threshold conventions, full T1 integral, current-sensor transfer function, capacitor inventory/discharge record, uncertainty budget, actual temperature, recovery endpoints and abort cause. First repeat each accepted target three times as separately armed shots; disagreement beyond uncertainty is investigated, never selected away. No unattended repeat train.

## Acceptance and what changes next

**Stop/inhibit on any native load current above 25 A, or any earlier native protection trip; the 37 A absolute ceiling must never be intentionally reached here.** Coupon load-current stop is 70 A. Recovery current may exceed load current; its sensor must remain within calibrated range, and device SOA/diode slew limits remain applicable. Inhibit prevents subsequent gate commands; it does not extinguish inductor current, so the diode/resistor decay path remains connected. Preserve the waveform, isolate supplies, discharge and meter-check before investigation. Also stop for VDS upper uncertainty bound exceeding 520 V, off-gate upper bound reaching 3 V, abs(VGS) approaching 20 V, unexpected opposite-leg activity, CT saturation, rising/saturating load slope, unexplained bus rise/droop, rail/bootstrap failure, clipping, hot/arcing hardware or failed discharge.

Infineon Rev 2.0 Table 2 p 3 specifies static±20 V and dynamic±30 V with AC f>1 Hz. This sparse single-shot procedure conservatively stops at 20 V; it records the historical D2 ±30 V comparison but does not treat that as permission to exceed 20 V. Off-gate 3 V remains a historical screen, **not** D6 hot qualification. A≤0 V engineering goal would be stricter, but is not a datasheet guarantee either. Any correlated off-channel current requires diagnosis even below 3 V.

The 520 V normal switching target is from [01-switching-parasitics.md](../../../../../validation-plan/01-switching-parasitics.md) and the existing bench plan. Package-lead voltage is not die voltage: passing this screen does not prove D2's die criterion. Compare a model with the measured fixture/probes to **terminal** waveforms first, then state any die inference and uncertainty. An uncertain or failed waveform ends escalation; do not bypass the baseline failure to reach a higher row.

If measured recovery has a different tail/slew than D3 beyond the fixture/temperature/probe uncertainty, obtain a vendor-supported recovery model or fit a documented empirical model to multiple independent points and validate against held-out shots. Qrr agreement alone does not validate snap-off. If the terminal S4 peak fails, the physical problem exists regardless of a favorable integrated Qrr. If it passes but the model fails, correct source/fixture/driver/parasitic and recovery assumptions before using that model for design. A measured timing gap above 348 ns at one condition cannot erase 307 ns cases across unknown controller configurations or temperatures.

D6's best tested passive candidate keeps 3.9 Ω and adds a diode+1 Ω parallel discharge branch and 2.2 nF gate-source capacitor on **both** devices. Before any optional A/B test, select and review real diode/capacitor voltage/current/pulse/thermal ratings, orientation, package and connection parasitics; record the new assembly identity. The branch diode conducts from gate toward driver during discharge. No such parts are selected or fitted by this document. Repeat baseline and candidate under matched measured conditions and compare actual switching overlap/driver loss as well as rebound. A negative rail or powered Miller clamp needs a different reviewed design; neither is available merely by changing a UCC21550 setting.
