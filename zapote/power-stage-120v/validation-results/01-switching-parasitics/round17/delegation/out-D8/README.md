**Measure native-17 S4 recovery and both gate-timing edges with a guarded, current-limited double-pulse fixture; compare D-3 at 400 V on a separate device coupon, and leave hardware release blocked until timing, hot off-gate margin and fixture qualification are established.**

## Fixture and energy budget

Use leg A only. Inherited connections are Q2 drain BUS_P/source SW_A, Q3 drain SW_A/source LEG_RET, R5 between LEG_RET and HV_RET. Bus source positive goes to **J8**, negative to **J10**, never to LEG_RET. Remove and secure J7–J8 and J9–J10 rectifier straps; J1 has no mains cable. J5 remains externally disconnected. The J2 path includes T1; J5 is not a direct SW_B terminal. Hold both leg-B PWMs low, keep U2 disabled and observe its gates in the zero-bus check. Confirm the retained resonant branch into leg B has no unintended current path before charging.

Proposed load: **100 µH air-core pulse inductor**, mechanically restrained winding on an insulating former, insulated terminals, pulse-rated winding/fixtures for at least **100 A**, and insulation rated for the coupon's **1 kV** fixture envelope. These are procurement/acceptance requirements, not a claim that a particular winding meets them. Air core avoids core saturation but does not remove heating, force, self-capacitance or insulation concerns. Measure L, DCR, self-resonance and current-ramp linearity; accept L within **±10%** of nominal across the planned pulse current, record actual L, and reject a changing slope not explained by bus voltage. Locate its magnetic field away from gate loops and current sensors; secure the geometry and photograph it. Do not improvise an uncharacterized hand-wound coil at the energized bench.

For DIR=0 connect the load **J2→J10**; positive load current leaves SW_A. Q2 charges it, and Q3's body diode carries reverse channel current during freewheel. For DIR=1 connect **J8→J2**; positive load current enters SW_A. Q3 charges it and Q2's diode freewheels. This matches the switch-node current polarity of D2 `IL=-20` and its `DIR` sign transform; a passive inductor always carries +20 A in the stated physical direction. For DIR=0, D2 returns its ideal negative source current from SW_A to BUS_P, while this passive fixture returns through HV_RET. These bus-current boundary conditions differ even when switch-node current agrees. Include the actual load return, reservoir, series resistor and lead impedance in the comparison model before attributing a waveform difference to diode physics. Reverse fixture leads only after discharge. Record T1 polarity, and capture protection activity rather than bypassing it. Put a **0.33 Ω ±5%, noninductive, ≥50 W, documented ≥1 J pulse-rated resistor** in series with the external native-board inductor. This proposed fixture resistor makes the final current decay short enough for T1; it is not a gate or board resistor change. Include its impedance and measured loss in the fixture model, and correct pulse widths from measured current.

Keep fitted C5/C6 and C38–C41. Their nominal combined bus capacitance is **5.8 µF** in the inherited procedure. A proposed **100 µF, ≥630 V** external reservoir at the supply side supports the pulse; include its actual C, ESR, lead inductance and supply-output C in the fixture model. Connect through a guarded, rated charge/discharge assembly, not loose clips. Record the branch geometry: it changes the bus boundary condition and must not be called an unchanged native-17 simulation. Do not add a capacitor directly across the local commutation pads as an unrecorded “stiffening” fix. If measured edge bus droop or ringing differs, compare simulation at the measured waveform/fixture, not the source front-panel setting.

[calculations.sh](calculations.sh) and [its output](calculations.txt) give rerunnable planning arithmetic, with `t1=L I/V`, `E_L=L I²/2`, `E_C=C V²/2`, reservoir-only droop `V−sqrt(V²−L I²/C)`, and discharge `RC ln(V/1 V)`. Ideal estimates exclude losses, supply delivery, tolerances and extra capacitance:

| Case | Ideal first-pulse time | Load energy | Planned reservoir + board energy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Native, 170 V / 20 A | 11.765 µs | 0.020 J | 1.529 J |
| Native, 198 V / 20 A | 10.101 µs | 0.020 J | 2.074 J |
| Separate coupon, 400 V / 58.2 A | 14.550 µs | 0.169 J | 8.000 J (100 µF; coupon parasitic C additional) |

The original board-only energy estimate does **not** cover this reservoir. Use an isolated source with an initial **50 mA recharge limit** and independent output disconnect; it cannot limit current from charged capacitors. Proposed independent dump: **10 kΩ, ≥25 W, ≥1 kV, documented ≥10 J pulse capability**, guarded and normally connected on inhibit. Recalculate for actual total energy and resistor pulse derating. The ideal maximum initial dump power is 16 W at 400 V; the estimated discharge time to 1 V is approximately 6 s, but **timing is never permission to touch**. Meter-check bus and resonant bank below 1 V, wait 60 s and recheck, proving the meter before and after, per the inherited procedure.

Native J2 current exercises T1. Retain the inherited **320 V·µs** shot budget and **≥10 ms** reset interval; calculate the full current-time integral including ramp, freewheel, second pulse **and final decay**, using measured burden/secondary response. The calculator's ramp-plus-10-µs values are partial planning checks, not full-shot certification. With the proposed series resistor, L≤110 µH, R≥0.3135 Ω and I≤22 A, the passive final-decay current-time integral is at most L·I/R = 0.007720 A·s if the diode is replaced by a zero-drop short; the nominal 3 Ω/100 CT conversion gives 231.6 V·µs. This conditional circuit estimate leaves room for the earlier pulse segments but is not a measured CT guarantee. Without this resistor (or another qualified energy dump), a long diode freewheel tail can exceed the CT budget even at 20 A; do not omit it. Wait for load current to return to zero before re-arm. The 400 V/58.2 A comparison cannot run on native-17: it exceeds this board campaign's voltage/current/protection envelope. Do not defeat OVP/OCP or the CT trip. Use the isolated coupon below.

## Equipment and measurement acceptance

The detailed grounding, rating and common-mode null procedures in BENCH-SWITCHING remain mandatory. Proposed minimum equipment:

| Equipment | Minimum acceptance requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Acquisition system | Common synchronized timebase; ≥8 simultaneous analog channels for Q2/Q3 VDS, Q2/Q3 VGS, diode ID, local bus and OUTA/OUTB; separate synchronized load-current channel or recorder, plus logic channels for INA/INB, PERMIT, DIS and BUS_FAULT. ≥500 MHz scope, ≥2.5 GS/s **on every active analog channel**, full-event memory and lossless export. Input logic thresholds must be known. |
| VDS / bus probes | Three HV differential probes, ≥200 MHz for VDS, ≥1 kV differential and input-to-earth working capability with manufacturer frequency/transient derating checked. The inherited THDP0200 example's 500× range is suitable in principle; verify its exact accessories. |
| VGS / OUT probes | Four isolated probes, ≥500 MHz for gate channels, differential range at least ±50 V, verified common-mode rating and rejection for the switching node; short solder-in differential adapters. OUT is referenced to the relevant VSS, not earth. |
| Current instruments | Device-terminal isolated Rogowski or engineered coaxial CVR with ≥100 MHz usable bandwidth and ≥150 A pulse range; separate calibrated DC-capable load sensor. Verify integration/droop, pulse area and isolation for the entire event. |
| Pulse/control source | Synchronized complementary pattern channels with independently configurable edges, ≤5 ns nominal time resolution and measured jitter/skew; hardware single-shot latch/inhibit independent of the controller. Actual controller binary is also required for the firmware claim. |
| Power/fixture | Isolated bus source, guarded reservoir/charge/dump assembly, isolated auxiliary rails, qualified bootstrap/precharge or isolated high-side supply, emergency disconnect and enclosure interlock; no back-feed into PS1/PS2. |
| Other | Calibrated meter, low-energy deskew fixture, LCR/impedance measurement, isolated temperature measurement/heater, rated barriers and PE-bonded enclosure. No instrument availability is assumed. |

Use **≤10 mm exposed tip separation/loop length where practicable**, with rigid insulated adapters directly at the device pins; this is a layout target, not a rating or guaranteed error. No long ground clips. Never float the scope or defeat PE. Even the low-side return may float; the default is isolated measurements.

R5 is useful for observing board OCP and return current without adding a component. It is **not** a recovering-device current oracle: common return/bypass currents and its own parasitics prevent separating diode recovery on both directions. A coaxial CVR resolves DC and fast ID but adds impedance; document its transfer function and loop change. A Rogowski avoids series insertion but needs an accessible single drain conductor, calibrated integrator, adequate low-frequency droop correction and controlled coupling. A sensor on the load lead measures load current, not Irrm. If direct device ID requires reworking native copper or lifting a lead, stop the native-current claim and use an owner-approved instrumented assembly/coupon; do not quietly modify the board. Voltage/timing-only rows may proceed after their own fixture qualification, with Qrr **unavailable**.

Deskew before any powered shot: apply the same low-energy edge to all relevant voltage tips; for current, use the calibrated deskew fixture through the actual sensor. Keep operational ranges, cables and filters. Correct fixed delay; retain raw and corrected records. Repeat after a range/lead change. Proposed residual delay acceptance is **≤2 ns**, with relative gate-voltage uncertainty **≤0.10 V** and peak VDS uncertainty **≤5 V** as procurement targets; if the demonstrated uncertainty is larger, report it and withhold any near-limit pass. Include probe gain, offset, common-mode null, noise, interpolation and placement repeatability. Verify the high-side gate null at a common source point at low bus before escalation; never move a live tip.

## Sequence A — native S4 recovery and rebound

1. Complete inherited assembly, no-mains, auxiliary, back-feed, bootstrap, independent-inhibit and meter checks. Verify that loss of any rail/enable/heartbeat asserts inhibit and disconnects charging at zero bus. Protection remains enabled. Capture BUS_FAULT and the HOT OCP output on isolated channels. A protective trip invalidates the requested matched row and is a finding, not a reason to bypass protection.
2. Start at **50 V / 2 A**, then 50 V / 5, 10 and 20 A, followed by the same current ladder at **100, 170 and 198 V**. These intermediate rows are fixture qualification choices; only the final 170/198 V, 20 A rows correspond to the requested S4 case. Initially use a **500 ns requested input gap**, then 391, 348 and 307 ns only after each preceding shot passes the abort checks. The driver may prevent the shorter request from becoming the same output gap: record that outcome; never defeat its DT interlock to force a target.
3. For each direction, start with the incoming device's charging pulse. Begin at half `L I/V` for a new target, inspect the current, and increase by no more than 10% of the final ideal time per shot. Match current at the **final recovery edge**, not merely at the charging pulse's end. Then turn the incoming device off. After a verified **1 µs** non-overlap, command the outgoing device on for **1 µs** in reverse-channel conduction. Turn it off, wait the requested interval, and turn the incoming device on. This last transition is the D2 S4 analogue: outgoing off → diode freewheel → partner hard turn-on. Capture both transitions and prove the outgoing gate actually rose/fell. A simpler pair with the outgoing gate permanently off is useful diode characterization but does not reproduce the D2 outgoing-channel history.
4. Initially limit the final incoming command to **1 µs** and verify recovery fits before its turn-off. At 198 V the ideal load-current increment is 1.98 A; impose a native load-current ceiling of **22 A** for this campaign, independent of the board's much higher nominal OCP. If the recovery window is incomplete, mark it truncated and review a revised pulse/current budget before any longer shot. Retain the inherited absolute **37 A** campaign ceiling; it is not permission to exceed the tighter 22 A choice here.
5. End all gate commands low, allow the freewheel/load current to decay while the interlocked auxiliary supply remains valid, then disconnect charging, discharge and meter-check. The chosen fixture must provide a qualified path for the remaining inductor energy during emergency auxiliary loss; if that behavior is unproved, no shot is allowed. Re-arm manually only after waveform review. Proposed repetition is **one shot per second or slower**, also subject to full current decay, CT reset and temperature recovery; no automatic repeat train.
6. Capture at least three accepted single shots per final condition to report observed variation, not a production bound. A failed shot stops that condition and all harsher conditions; retain the trace. Repeat both physical directions after discharged fixture change. Keep gate resistors, snubbers and supply voltages at the recorded native baseline.

## Sequence B — D-3 matched device coupon, not native-17

Use two IPW65R018CFD7 devices from recorded lots, a dedicated guarded 400 V half-bridge coupon and the same measurement discipline. Diagram the commutation loop and measure its parasitics. Use the 100 µH/100 µF proposed fixture with no T1, no native board and no native protection bypass. The diode DUT is low side; the incoming device is high side; load connects switch node to return, matching [D-3 recovery.cir](../out-D3/recovery.cir). A qualified isolated high-side gate supply avoids relying on an unverified bootstrap dwell.

At low voltage/current qualify the coupon, then approach the **400 V, 58.2 A diode current, 25 °C, 100 A/µs** matched point in separately reviewed increments. Do not call a different point matched. Suggested comparison tolerances are ±2% for measured bus/current, ±5% for zero-crossing slew and ±2 °C initial case temperature; retain actual values and uncertainty. Establish near-isothermal junction before the shot and use measured dissipated energy plus a justified transient thermal model to estimate its rise; case temperature alone does not certify Tj=25 °C.

Use **13 V** gate command and **equal external resistors**, starting at D-3's **730 Ω** on both devices. This is a model-fixture starting value, not the native 3.9 Ω drive. Charge the inductor with the incoming device, turn it off, and allow a proposed **2 µs** diode dwell before the second pulse; DUT gate stays returned to source through its equal resistor. D-3's recorded command-to-recovery-end time is **5.013225 µs** (command at 2 µs; zero at 6.803267 µs; 10% endpoint at 7.013225 µs, `out-D3/results/recovery-25C-0.2n/result.json`). A 5 µs command cap would therefore truncate even that reference. Start second-command width at **2 µs for incomplete low-energy qualification only**, extending only after a complete low-energy waveform review to at most **6 µs**, with an independently set **85 A load-current ceiling** and **100 A device-terminal current stop** as proposed fixture limits. Tune both resistors together after discharge until the **measured** commutation slew is 100 A/µs; do not tune Qrr toward the expected answer. Verify pulse duration against the datasheet SOA and actual thermal estimate before the final current step. The matched shot requires measured recovery completion plus a **0.75 µs** observation guard before incoming turn-off; the proposed 6 µs cap must be qualified against actual command latency. The ideal worst-case load increase over the whole 6 µs command is 24 A at 400 V/100 µH (actual conduction starts later). If the full recovery window and guard do not fit within those constraints, stop and redesign the coupon budget rather than lengthening pulses ad hoc.

Measure positive drain current in the recovering device after its forward current crosses zero. Interpolate zero and the first descending **10% Irrm** crossing, integrate between those endpoints for terminal Qrr, and compute `trr=t10−tzero`, `ta=tpeak−tzero`, `tb10=t10−tpeak`, `softness=tb10/ta`. Fit di/dt over **±20 ns** about zero to match D-3, also reporting wider-window sensitivity. These definitions intentionally match D-3 and Infineon Table 8; a scope's tangent-to-zero default is a different measurement. Save the full later tail and ringing separately; do not absorb negative ringing into Qrr. Terminal current includes displacement charge, so do not label its entire integral minority-carrier charge.

At the matched point compare with Infineon Table 7: Qrr **2.30 µC typical / 4.60 µC max**, trr **236 ns typical / 354 ns max**, Irrm **15 A typical with no max**. D-3's model comparison is **1.738815 µC, 209.958 ns, 12.302259 A** at its measured 100.564 A/µs; use its full waveform and fixture differences, not only those scalars. Typical values define comparison, not a guaranteed target or automatic model-accuracy tolerance. A real tail differing beyond combined measurement/model-condition uncertainty calls for a recovery-model update and rerun of both corrected matrices; agreement at this single point does not validate high-slew S4 or hot silicon.

## Sequence C — dead time and acceptance readings

First use the actual controller at zero bus with its binary hash, SDK commit, pin assignment, requested timer settings, startup/stop/restart and fault-recovery sequence recorded. Test **both edges on both legs** at the intended frequency/duty/phase settings; leg-A energized tests remain the scope of this procedure, while leg-B zero-bus timing is a separate required capture. Repeat low/high operating rail and intended temperature endpoints only within qualified apparatus ratings. A laboratory pattern generator characterizes the driver and fixture; it cannot close the firmware claim.

For every edge report these separate intervals:

- INA/INB fall-to-opposite-rise at documented thresholds, with input slopes and channel skew. Save analog inputs in a dedicated timing acquisition if digital threshold accuracy cannot support the budget.
- UCC output **90% falling to opposite 10% rising**, referenced to each channel's supply/return, per TI Figure 6-4. This is DTS. Do not add firmware gap and RDT-programmed gap, or subtract mismatch again from measured DTS.
- Package gate fall below **3.0 V** to opposite rise above **3.0 V** at room temperature, and again at **1.9 V** as a provisional exploratory screen pending D-6. Report all crossings/re-crossings and any negative interval. The interval below a selected voltage is not proof of zero drain current or a guaranteed hot threshold.
- Off-device VGS maximum/minimum from the partner's INA/INB on command through at least **0.75 µs** afterwards; also retain an output-aligned window extending **0.75 µs** after effective driver-output turn-on, and a longer capture for later rebound. D2's command drives its output-resistance model directly, so it does not contain the UCC input propagation/programmed-DT latency. Correlate that model command to the measured output transition before comparing peaks. Report VGS at the partner command separately from the later maximum. Record load and both terminal stress traces at the same time.

Pass only a **specified measurement screen** when its worst-direction uncertainty stays inside it. VDS peak + uncertainty must be ≤520 V; off-gate peak + uncertainty must be <3.0 V for the room-temperature project screen. D-6's typical-model-derived 1.9 V comparison is exploratory, not hot qualification. Infineon specifies static ±20 V and dynamic ±30 V with `AC (f>1 Hz)`; this sparse single-shot protocol conservatively stops at **|VGS| + uncertainty ≥20 V** unless the vendor confirms a wider allowance for this exact event. Also report D2's ±30 V simulated criterion, without silently applying its dynamic rating to every bench pulse.

These are **package-lead** measurements. D2 reports internal die nodes; package inductance can change either sign of the difference. A package pass does not establish a die pass. Preserve a measured terminal-model comparison and explicitly validated package de-embedding, with uncertainty, before transferring a verdict. Likewise a low rebound does not establish absence of shoot-through: inspect current and determine its path.

Abort/invalidate on overrange, failed null/deskew, missing diode ID when recovery is claimed, unexpected leg activity, rail/bootstrap droop, BUS_FAULT, current-limit crossing, unexplained slope change, temperature excursion, any stress screen failure or failed discharge. Stop later shots; the oscilloscope/software is not fast protection for the first nanosecond event. Hardware inhibit, bounded pulse widths, limited stored energy and enclosure must be qualified independently. Do not retry until the failure is assigned to circuit, fixture or instrument with retained evidence.
