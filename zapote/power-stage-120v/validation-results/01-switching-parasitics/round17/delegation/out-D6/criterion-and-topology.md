# D6 criterion and proposed circuit interpretation

The historical 3.0 V cutoff is not a guaranteed hot-junction limit.

The [Infineon IPW65R018CFD7 datasheet](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/24/49/infineon-ipw65r018cfd7-datasheet-en.pdf), Rev. 2.0, 2021-04-19, printed p3 Table 2 specifies maximum operating Tj=150°C, static VGS ±20 V and dynamic VGS ±30 V. Printed p5 Table 4 gives VGS(th)=3.5/4.0/4.5 V at ID=2.91mA, VDS=VGS, under the section's 25°C default. No hot minimum or threshold temperature coefficient is specified. Printed p9 Diagram 9 is a **typical** transfer curve at 25/150°C; it cannot establish the minimum threshold across production at temperature, nor a guaranteed subtraction from the 25°C minimum. Consequently “3.5−0.5=3.0 V” is an optimistic claim of guaranteed hot margin, although whether 3.0 V causes material current on a particular unit remains unknown. A threshold measured at milliamperes also does not identify a safe transient power/current limit.

Recommended acceptance has two levels: retain <3.0 V solely for comparison with the historical grid; require a temperature- and lot-qualified criterion for hardware signoff. Report signed gate peaks. A ≤0 V off-gate screen is an engineering target here, **not a replacement vendor limit**. Hot bench tests must resolve gate-source voltage at the device terminals, probe/common-source error and channel/cross-conduction current, including startup and loss of bias. Typical model behavior cannot close that requirement.

The [TI UCC21550 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ucc21550.pdf), SLUSE89C, August 2024, p3–4 Table 4-1 has no Miller-clamp output; p22 §7.3.1 describes an unbiased/UVLO output clamp, not an independently activated powered Miller clamp. It is therefore skipped as required by the brief. P34–36 §8.2.2.9 permits negative bias using split supplies or Zener arrangements. Independent positive/negative rails require two supplies or a dual-output supply per isolated domain; the single-supply gate-path alternative depends on duty cycle. P5 operating conditions allow a 25 V output-supply span; +15/−2 and +15/−4 imply 17 and 19 V spans.

The frozen netlist ties U1.14 (VSSA) directly to Q2.3 on `sw_a`, U1.9 (VSSB) to Q3.3 on `leg_ret`, U1.11 to `v15_ls`, and U1.16 to `leg_a-boot` via D1/C10/C11. It has no independent negative rail. The negative-bias simulations retain +15 V on and use an ideal fixed −2 or −4 V off. They propose changing the supply arrangement: a 15 V supply cannot simultaneously produce +15 and −4 V across a 19 V span. The bootstrap, startup, recharge, regulation, UVLO sequencing and negative-rail impedance are not simulated. No board/netlist modification is implied.

For split gate resistors, retain the existing 3.9 Ω path and add a diode plus 1 or 2 Ω discharge branch **in parallel**, with diode anode toward gate and cathode toward driver. The diode is a declared generic finite model (`Is=1n`, `N=1`, `Rs=0.1`, `Cjo=10p`, `Tt=5n`), not an ideal switch or selected component. Thus 1/2 Ω names denote branch resistors, not exact total effective Rg_off: the original resistor also conducts, the diode has forward drop and dynamic impedance, and the MOSFET retains 2.7 Ω internal Rg. A part selection, pulse-current rating, reverse-recovery/capacitance check, component/trace inductance and actual layout remain required. This study isolates the proposed change and preserves the original deck's omissions rather than silently updating its driver model.

External Cgs is connected to package gate/source terminals, not internal die nodes; capacitor ESL/ESR are idealized. Larger gate capacitance can delay turn-off enough to reduce the available commutation interval, so S1 incoming VDS at the partner command is retained, particularly at 348 ns.
