Bound: Ik <= 784 A, so the fuse has a 1.91x margin on 1.5 kA at R = 0.15 ohm. Continuous loss is 0.30 W at 1.42 A. The holder with cover is rated 6.3 A / 2.5 W at 250 V.

**Main finding.** The series R bounds the fuse's interrupting duty, but the resistor's survival depends on the fuse's arcing I2t, which Littelfuse does not publish. Survival can be settled only by a bench short-circuit test at J11 with the resistor fitted (measure i(t) and integrate), or by Littelfuse let-through data at about 800 A and 140 VAC. Margins:
- AC05 0.15 ohm: 1.86x if the fuse is current-limiting (arc voltage at or above the 197.6 V source peak); 0.96x at my 3x-melting design point.
- AC07: 3.3x and 1.7x on the same two cases.
- No PCB resistor survives the non-current-limiting bound (774 J).

If the resistor does not survive, it heats until its wire opens. The coating is inorganic and passes the needle-flame test (no burning after 30 s). The opening only lowers the current, so the fuse never sees more than Ik. The resulting "fails open, no fire" behaviour is my inference for a cemented wirewound part; Vishay does not state it, so it remains a certifier item (P2 SAFETY (c)).

littelfuse.com (product page and assetdocs PDF) returned 403 again, so I could not read the Littelfuse page directly. The datasheet copies above are Littelfuse's own PDFs, hosted by distributors.

**Clearing I2t at 784 A.**
- *Published:* melting I2t only, 43.3 A2s measured at 10 In (31.5 A, about 44 ms). At 784 A (250 In) melting is adiabatic and its I2t is at most this figure, so 43.3 A2s is an upper bound on pre-arc I2t. No clearing or arcing I2t, and no I2t-t curve, is published for the 215.
- *Method (`parts_calc.py`):* the loop is 139.7 V rms in series with R + 28.25 mOhm and a 2 or 10 uH source.
  - The fault starts at any point on the wave (2 deg steps).
  - Pre-arc ends at 43.255 A2s.
  - A constant arc voltage Va then burns until the current reaches zero.
  - The table keeps the worst case.
- *Results at R = 0.15 ohm:*

| Va (V) | I2t_clear (A2s) | x melting | E in R (J) | conduction (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (not current-limiting, burns to the natural zero) | 5,158 | 119 | 774 | 9.3 |
| 100 | 916 | 21 | 137 | 5.3 |
| 150 | 207 | 4.8 | 31 | 3.3 |
| 200 (at or above the 197.6 V source peak: current-limiting) | 67 | 1.56 | 10.1 | 0.34 |
| 300 | 56 | 1.29 | 8.4 | 0.16 |

- *Design multiplier: 3x melting = 130 A2s (19.5 J at 0.15 ohm).*
  - Justification: it is about 2x the current-limiting model value (1.3-1.6x). It is equivalent to a mean arc voltage of about 160 V, i.e. it allows a fuse that is only partly current-limiting.
  - The repo's own Class CC data point (KLDR025: 1,710 / 226 = 7.6x) is at that fuse's full rated duty (600 V, 200 kA). Ratios above 3 do occur at rated duty. Here the fuse runs at 56 % of rated voltage and 52 % of rated breaking current, so a lower ratio is expected, but that is not proven.
  - **3x is an estimate, not a bound.** The only true bound is the Va = 0 row.

## 3. Series limiter candidates

Pulse capability is from the Vishay AC.. Series datasheet, doc 28730, Rev 07-Dec-2020, p.6 (https://wmsc.lcsc.com/wmsc/upload/file/pdf/v2/lcsc/2304140030_Vishay-Intertech-AC05000001507JAC00_C1382388.pdf). The curves are the same in Rev 13-Jan-14 (https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/2099789.pdf).
- The datasheet text: "Choose the R value on X axis and look for the corresponding E (Ws/Ohm) value on Y axis. Multiplying ... will be resultant total pulse Energy ... for adiabatic conditions."
- So the Y axis is directly an I2t capacity in A2s, and it can be compared one-to-one with the fuse I2t.
- I read the values by eye, +-25 %. On 2026-10-13 I re-read them by pixel position against the decade gridlines (300 dpi render):
  - AC05: 153 / 124 / 76 A2s at 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.22 ohm.
  - AC07: 266 / 213 / 118 A2s.
  - AC03: 27 / 18 at 0.15 / 0.22 ohm.
  - These agree with the table below within the stated tolerance.
- `parts_calc.py` prints one capacity line per R. The 75 and 120 A2s lines are the **0.22 ohm** values for AC05 and AC07. They do not conflict with 125 and 220 A2s at 0.15 ohm.
- The same datasheet gives short-time overload of 10x P40 for 5 s, and says "All materials used are non-flammable and inorganic". Needle flame: no burning after 30 s.

Lead pitch is my choice for axial hand-forming: body length plus at least 2 x 3 mm before the bend. Vishay also offers formed EK/DK variants (datasheet p.3).

Rejected:
- **AC03 0.15 ohm (C1365803)**: about 30 A2s, below the 43 A2s pre-arc alone.
- **Yageo KNP300 0.15 ohm (C3477288, out of stock) and other stocked KNP/NKNP/MOF parts (e.g. TyoHM C5249364, 734 stock)**: no single-pulse energy data.
  - The KNP V.5 sheet (https://www.tti.com/content/dam/tti-commons/supplier/yageo/ecom/yageo-knp-wirewound-resistors-datasheet-specifications-ap.pdf, p.7) gives only repetitive-pulse curves.
  - On those curves the KNP300 0R1 line is flat at about 4 kW, while this circuit puts 92 kW (784² x 0.15) into the resistor.
- **SMD**: Vishay CRCW2512-HP single-pulse capability is about 0.25 J at 1 ms, and its range starts at 1 ohm (https://www.vishay.com/docs/20043/crcwhpe3.pdf, Rev 17-Mar-2026, p.5). The pre-arc energy alone is 6.5 J, so SMD thick film is short by about 25x.

## 4. Bound and energies (`parts_calc.py`; formula checked in `f2_variants.py`)

`Ik = V / (R_fuse_cold + R)`, with V = 139.7 V and **0.02825 ohm**.
- The 0.0283 ohm is the fuse's datasheet cold resistance, not a source impedance.
- `f2_variants.py` sets the source impedance to 0 ("conservative, because |Z_src + R| >= R").
- The values match `result.json`.

| R (ohm) | Ik rms (pk) | Margin on 1.5 kA | Loss at 1.42 A | E, pre-arc only | E, current-limiting (Va 200) | E, 3x design | E, non-current-limiting bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 1,089 A (1,540) | 1.38x | 0.20 W | 4.3 J | 8.6 J | 13.0 J | 992 J |
| 0.15 | 784 A (1,108) | 1.91x | 0.30 W | 6.5 J | 10.1 J | 19.5 J | 774 J |
| 0.22 | 563 A (796) | 2.67x | 0.44 W | 9.5 J | 12.1 J | 28.6 J | 589 J |

Raising R lowers the fuse duty, but the AC05's pulse capacity falls faster (thinner wire), so 0.15 ohm is the balance point for the AC05.

## 5. Not verified

- **Fuse clearing I2t**: not published by Littelfuse. Everything about resistor survival rests on the arc-voltage model above. The settling test is a bench short-circuit at J11 with the resistor fitted, on a stiff source, recording i(t). Alternatively ask Littelfuse for let-through I2t at about 800 A and 140 VAC.
- **The 1.5 kA on the agency certificates**: the datasheet footnote says the rating may differ by agency. Certificates not read.
- **Resistor failure mode** ("fails open, no flame"): inferred from Vishay's non-flammable, inorganic construction and the needle-flame test. Vishay does not state it. A turn-to-turn weld (which would fail low) is not excluded by any document.
- **AC chart readings**: by eye, +-25 %. The chart has no pulse-duration axis, only "adiabatic". The fault pulses here last 0.2-3 ms.
- **Holders**:
  - 646/648: assembled height and UL/CSA certificates not seen.
  - BLX-A: no approvals.
  - Würth: height not verified (PDF 403).
  - LCSC and JLC stock differ for C206887 (1,452 vs 2,260).
- **Stock and prices**: from LCSC product pages and the JLC parts API (`jlcpcb.com/api/overseas-pcb-order/v1/shoppingCart/smtGood/selectSmtComponentList`) on 2026-10-13. 500-board quantities exceed current LCSC stock for AC05 0.15 (245) and for 64600001223 (67).
- **Side note, not a substitute**: Schurter SPT 5x20 3.15 A (0001.2509) carries UL 10 kA @ 125 VAC and 1500 A @ 250 VAC (https://www.schurter.com/en/datasheet/typ_SPT_5x20.pdf, 21.07.2026, p.3-4). 125 V is below this study's 127 V nominal / 139.7 V maximum, so it does not apply unless the certifier accepts a 120 V-nominal rating basis.
