 PCBA Testing & Quality Control
SPI (Solder Paste Inspection)
Uses 2D/3D SPI to detect solder paste volume, height, offset, and bridging—addressing up to 80% of SMT defects at the printing stage.
AOI (Automated Optical Inspection)
Applies 2D/3D AOI to identify component misalignment, polarity errors, missing parts, and visible solder defects.
X-Ray Inspection
Performs X-ray inspection for hidden joints in BGA, QFN, and LGA packages, analyzing voids, bridging, and internal defects.
Flying Probe Test
Verifies PCBA connectivity and electrical integrity via probing, enabling open/short detection without dedicated fixtures—ideal for prototypes and low-volume runs.
Functional Testing
Conducts power-on and functional testing based on customer requirements to ensure proper operation before shipment.
Manual Visual Inspection
Uses manual visual inspection with high magnification to check solder joint quality, component orientation, and workmanship details.
IC Programming
Flash firmware to microcontrollers and memory devices using customer-specified tools, methods, and interfaces.

 FAQs About PCB Assembly Services
 How much does PCB assembly cost?
Typically, the total cost of PCB assembly includes setup fees, stencil, assembly labor, and components, with real-time pricing available through the online quote system.
For a detailed cost breakdown and pricing examples, check our full guide: PCB Assembly Price Details
 How do I prepare a BOM file for PCB assembly?
Make sure your BOM includes: component values, reference designators, package types, and manufacturer part numbers. Standard formats like .csv, .xls, or .xlsx are recommended.
View detailed instructions and a sample BOM file here: Complete BOM guide
 How can panelization reduce PCB assembly costs?
Panelization groups multiple PCBs into one panel so they can be assembled simultaneously, improving efficiency and lowering cost per unit.
For better efficiency and pricing, panelization is strongly recommended for quantities over 50 pcs.
See detailed PCB panel guidelines.
 How long does PCB assembly take?
PCB assembly begins immediately after PCB fabrication is completed. Since all necessary materials—such as components, engineering data, and solder paste stencils—are prepared in advance, there is no delay between stages.
About 90% of PCB assembly orders are completed within 24 hours, and most within 48 hours at most.
This makes it ideal for rapid prototyping and urgent production needs.
Learn more about lead times and production details here: SMT Service Lead Time Guide
 What if I can’t find a component in the JLCPCB parts library?
Components for PCB assembly can be sourced in multiple ways, depending on your needs:
In-stock components from JLCPCB’s parts library (fastest option)
Global sourcing via trusted distributors (lead time: 9–15 days)
Pre-order parts added to your private library
Consigned parts supplied by you
New part requests if a component is not yet listed
This flexible sourcing system ensures you can always find or request the components required for your project.
See all components sourcing methods and requirements here: PCB Assembly Parts Sourcing Guide
