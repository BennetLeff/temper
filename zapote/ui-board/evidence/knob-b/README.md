# Knob option (b): magnetic detents above the panel, nonmagnetic retention, and their effect on the TMAG5170 angle

2026-10-12, temper-bc (this session, not the Codex mechanical agent). Everything below is generated by `detent_study.py` (deterministic; two full runs give identical `results.json` apart from wall-clock times) and is in `results.json` (`headline` first), `plots/` and `raw/`. A physics design study of rigid permanent magnets: not a measurement and not released CAD. Inputs read: `../knob-ring/` (kernels, sensor model, calibration and validation method, controls), `tools/ui_calcs.py`, DECISIONS U19 to U32, the owned-controls SPEC (read-only).

## 1. Question

Option (b) of `KNOB-SIMPLIFICATION.md`: the knob is held by nonmagnetic mechanics on a blind formed feature of the solid 3 mm 5052 fascia (no hole), and **captive magnet pairs make the 24 detents**. Fixed: knob axis (110, 53), visible Ø56 clear-anodised knob (r <= 28), Ø10 x 5 diametral Sm2Co17 sensing magnet at w +1..+6, TMAG5170 Hall centre (110, 53, w -4), 5 mm face to die, no stationary steel inside r 25. Which pole arrangement gives 24 detents of the SPEC torque, what does it do to the sensor, and what must R44 provide?

Method: rigid uniformly magnetised SmCo blocks, exact cuboid field kernel (copied from the ring study, checked against magpylib to 1e-14 here); torque = -dU/dtheta of the rotor-stator interaction energy U = -int M.B dV (Gauss quadrature, 5 points per axis), checked by the direct force on the magnets (equivalent surface charges, route 2). Calibration and validation exactly as the ring study: one cold calibration (offset + gain + skew `affine`, or 4 error harmonics `harm4`, or `harm4_24` with the 24th and 48th) fitted on 35 angles 0.5 + 10.25 k deg (none on a detent centre), validated on the other 1405 angles of the 0.25 deg full-turn run and on seven other conditions (push 0.5, gap +-0.2, 176 C, combinations), ideal coaxial unit (A) and a worst-tolerance as-built unit (B: knob offset (0.1, 0.1), tilt 1 deg, carrier offset 0.1, sensor tilt 1 deg), and 30 Monte Carlo draws of piece scatter on unit B. D = the largest difference between the calibrated residual of the same unit with and without the detent assembly. The hot sensor gain residual (0.057 deg, TEMP_RESULT corrected, ui_calcs) is common to both and cancels in D.

## 3. Bottom line

**Recommended: B1, a radial-gap rotor/stator pair entirely above the panel: 24 rotor blocks in the knob skirt, 4 stator blocks in a carrier on the formed feature, all radially magnetised Sm2Co17, attracting.** The 24 detents come from the cogging relation (section 4): the 24 rotor blocks at 15 deg pitch each pass the stators; the 4 stators at 90 deg are a multiple of 15 deg, so all align together. Retention (pull-off and lateral locator) is **mechanical and nonmagnetic**; the detent pairs add about 1 N magnetic restoring force but cannot carry the 12 to 20 N.

## 4. Architectures compared (`results.json` `architectures`, `plots/architectures.png`)

**Cogging relation.** Two rings of N_r and N_s magnets couple only through harmonics that both patterns contain: same-pole rings contain multiples of N, alternating-pole rings only odd multiples of N/2. Detents per turn = the lowest common harmonic: same-pole 24 and 4 (or 24, or 3) give lcm = 24; alternating 24 and 24 give **12**; alternating 48 and 48 give 24; a same-pole ring against an alternating one has no common harmonic when they are coprime. The script counts the equilibria of the full-turn energy: every row below matches the relation (12 for the alternating 24/24 rows).

Each architecture is sized (axial length, or the radial width for B2) to a mean torque of 24.3 mN m at nominal, within its envelope; "not reached" means the best the envelope allows is shown.

Why across the panel fails: the 24-fold part of the field varies with wavelength lambda = 2 pi r / 24 = 6.3 mm and decays as exp(-2 pi g / lambda). With the 4.1 mm gap (rotor bottom at w +1, stator top at w -3.1) only a few percent of it survives; the best the envelope allows (4 x 3.5 mm faces, 5 mm rotors, 4 mm stators) is 9.7 mN m, 2.5 times short, while the axial attraction (4 to 11 N) is gap-coupled to the push. The alternating 24 + 24 across the panel reaches the torque only because it has **12** detents (lambda 12.6 mm). The same argument chooses the radial gap above the panel (0.67 mm, exp(-0.7) instead of exp(-4.1)), and it also decouples the torque from the push: the rotor moves axially inside the stator's axial extent, so 0.65 mm of push changes the torque by -6 % (the axial gap changes it by +116 %). Nothing is added behind the fascia.

Why 24 + 4 and not 4 + 24: the torque is identical (the energy is symmetric). With the 4 blocks stationary the leakage into the sensor from piece scatter is smaller (9.7 and 9.4 uT per axis at 3 % strength scatter against 12.5 and 13.3 uT) and the stationary Bz is 0.43 mT against 1.3 mT; a rotating error field co-rotates with the sensing magnet and cannot produce an angle error. Axial against radial magnetisation: the axial arrangement (B3) cannot reach 2 mN m in the envelope; radial attracting is axially stable (restoring, + 1.6 N) and laterally mildly unstable (0.76 N/mm), the repelling one (B1x) is the reverse.

## 5. Recommended design in detail

### 5.1 Torque (`plots/torque_vs_angle.png`, `raw/nominal_torque.csv`)

Nominal trace at 0.1 deg: mean 24.18, peak 37.39 mN m. The corner table of section 3 uses 0.25 deg steps (nominal 24.1 / 37.3). Corners at 20 C, recommended tolerances (mean / peak): Br +3 %: 25.6 / 39.6; Br -3 %: 22.7 / 35.1; gap -0.07: 26.1 / 40.3; gap +0.07: 22.3 / 34.6; axial +-0.30: 23.8 / 36.9; lateral 0.30 at 0 or 45 deg: 24.3 / 37.6; tilt 1 deg: 23.9 / 37.0; pushed 0.35 / 0.65: 23.7 / 36.7 and 22.7 / 35.2; STRONG (Br +, gap -): 27.7 / 42.7; WEAK (Br -, gap +, axial -0.3): 20.7 / 32.2; WEAK pushed 0.65: 19.8 / 30.7. At -40 C: 25.1 / 38.9 nominal, 28.9 / 44.5 strong. The catalogue set is in `results.json` (`corners_catalogue_tolerance`): the gap tolerance is the sensitive input (+-12 % torque per 0.1 mm, exp(-2 pi g / lambda)), then Br (torque ~ Br^2). Hence the tolerance requirement of section 8.

Torque by two routes (control 2): 0.07 % worst over concentric, 0.3 mm eccentric, 0.5 mm pushed and 1 deg tilted at three angles (energy derivative against the force on the magnets). The sensing magnet's coupling to the stators is nil (energy variation 1.6e-10 nJ over a turn: a C4 stator pattern cannot couple to the dipole's odd azimuthal orders).

### 5.2 Forces (`plots/forces.png`)

Axial restoring force on the knob over the lift: 0.21 N at 0.2 mm, 0.55 at 0.5, 1.13 at 1.0, 1.54 at 1.5, **1.59 at 2.0**, 1.30 at 2.5, 0.82 at 3, 0.20 at 4, about 0 at 5 mm (negative = restoring, toward the seat); between detents about a fifth of that. It adds to the pull-off and to the push return; the flexure designer must include 0.55 N at 0.5 mm push (+-10 % over the lots) as part of the 7.5 to 11.5 N. Lateral: 0.054 N at 0.1 mm, 0.18 N at 0.3 mm, 0.38 N at 0.5 mm: a destabilising 0.76 N/mm at most, 2 % of the locator's 40 N/mm. Net force of the rotor ring on the stationary carrier: zero in the ideal case; each of the four stator blocks is pulled 0.74 N radially (a 0.5 mm wall of PEEK holds that; no adhesive credit).

### 5.3 Irreversible loss (T9)

At 176 C (M 895 kA/m, HcJ >= 1043 kA/m from 1710 x (1 - 0.0025 x 156) using the RECOMA 35E table: HcJ min 1710 kA/m, alpha(HcJ) -0.25 %/C, read 2026-10-12): opposing field along M inside the blocks, volume mean 329 kA/m, 95th percentile 473 kA/m (assembled), 469 (rotor alone, knob removed), 515 (stator alone), maximum node 545; margin 2.03 against HcJ. The datasheet gives the demagnetisation curves only as a plot and no knee (Hk), so this is necessary, not sufficient: the supplier's worst-lot curve at 176 C with the 2.0 mm magnetisation length is a gate (section 9). A longer magnetisation length (2.5 or 3 mm radial) raises the permeance coefficient at the cost of the gap-sensitive torque; it was not optimised.

### 5.4 Steel and permeability

No steel anywhere (section 8 lists the materials). The only ferromagnetic material is the SmCo (recoil permeability 1.05 to 1.10 per supplier tables found on the web, 2026-10-12, not read from the RECOMA sheet): with chi = 0.10 and no self-demagnetisation (an upper bound) the induced moments of all 32 blocks add at most 0.85 uT at the die and a peak angle error below 1e-12 deg (C4/C24 symmetry again). The rigid-magnet torque ignores the same effect, a few percent on U for B of 0.3 T over the gap (first-order estimate, not computed): inside the +-0.07 mm and Br tolerance bands.

## 6. Effect on the TMAG5170 angle

### 6.1 Field at the die (`plots/field_at_die.png`, `raw/assembly_field_at_die_A_cold_uT.csv`)

Ideal coaxial unit, cold: the whole assembly at the die is **(Bx, By, Bz) = (0, 0, +1.279 mT) for every knob angle** (stators +0.425 mT, rotor ring +0.854 mT; Bx, By below 1e-12 uT). By symmetry: a pattern with 24-fold (rotor) or 4-fold (stator) symmetry produces no field component that rotates at the knob angle except through harmonic orders 24 and 4, and the vector field on the axis contains only azimuthal order 1. The Fourier amplitudes of Bx + i By at orders 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 12, 24, 48 are all <= 3e-12 uT, and of Bz at orders 1 to 48 <= 6e-11 uT; **the sin 24 theta term the request worried about is zero (1e-11 uT) for the ideal unit and 1.3e-11 uT at worst over the 30 scatter draws**: it is not a field at the die at all, because the die sits on the axis. What the assembly does change: a constant Bz (1.28 mT) and, in the as-built unit, a constant X/Y bias: stators -1.96 uT in x (carrier offset 0.1 mm), the sensor-tilt coupling of Bz (1 deg x 1.28 mT = 22 uT: mean (-6.3, -23.1) uT in unit B), and up to 26.6 uT of stationary bias with piece scatter (worst draw). A constant vector is a first harmonic of the angle error and is removed by the offset/harmonic terms of the cold calibration; only its change with temperature, gap and push survives.

## 8. Retention approach and R44 geometry requirements

### 8.1 Retention (nonmagnetic, mechanical)

The detent pairs give only 1.6 N of axial pull. The 12 to 20 N must come from mechanics. A magnetic pull-off from the detent pairs was rejected: an axial-gap pair (B2) gives 5.7 N at a 0.5 mm push and doubles the detent torque there; a pull-off made of dedicated axial magnets would need about 130 mm^2 of pole face at 16 N (Maxwell stress B^2 / 2 mu0 of about 120 kPa at 0.55 T, a hand estimate), several times the magnet volume used here, with a force that rises steeply as the push closes the gap. **Approach:** 3 ball plungers in the knob skirt (Si3N4 balls, BeCu or Elgiloy springs) engage a 35 deg flank groove in the carrier; the groove is the ball footprint + 0.8 mm wide so that the 0.65 mm push never rides the flank. Estimate: F = n P (tan beta + mu) / (1 - mu tan beta) + F_magnetic(0.8 mm = 0.90 N) with n = 3, P = 5.4 N +-10 %: **12.9 to 21.4 N for mu 0.08 to 0.25**; for 12 to 20 N the friction must stay in 0.05 to 0.21 over dry and wet, which is the open question (a ball rolling in a lubricated seat would be better than the 0.08 floor assumed; a dry seat of 0.25 breaks the 20 N limit). It is an estimate on assumptions and the first fixture measurement. Lateral locator: two lands 6 mm apart with 0.10 mm diametral clearance and a 10 mm lever give about 0.20 mm rim displacement at 10 N against the 0.25 mm SPEC (assumptions in `results.json`): the lands must sit above the magnets so that tilt does not move the rotor ring. Life (5 k removals) of the ball seat is unproved.

## 9. Limitations

- **Rigid magnets**: superposition of fixed magnetisations; the recoil permeability (1.05 to 1.10) changes the pair energy by a few percent (first-order estimate, not computed) and the induced-field bound on the sensing side is 0.85 uT. The demagnetisation knee at 176 C is not in the datasheet text: T9 is against HcJ only. A supplier curve for the worst lot, with the 2.0 mm magnetisation length and the 0.1 mm cover, is a gate.
- **Mechanics are not designed**: ball plunger friction (0.08 to 0.25 is an assumed band), spring rate, the lateral locator and the life of 5 000 removals / 50 000 turns are estimates; PEEK, Si3N4, BeCu behaviour in oil, vinegar, detergent and degreaser at 176 C is unverified; no friction torque of seals and bearings is modelled (the non-detent drag is only the magnetic/eddy part: eddy bound 0.15 mN m).
- **Scatter inputs** (1.5 % strength, 1.5 deg, 0.05 mm) and the thermal expansion coefficients are assumed typical values; the Monte Carlo has 40 (torque) and 30 (sensor) draws, not a tolerance chain.
- **Thermal**: the radial gap change from differential expansion is computed (section 3) but the temperature of the knob and the carrier is taken as 176 C uniformly; the knob is hotter near the fascia than at the rim in reality.
- **Coil**: the 0.173 deg allowance is the owner's number without these magnets; eddy heating of the SmCo blocks (resistivity 90 uOhm cm, skin depth about 2 mm at 60 kHz) was estimated by hand at microwatts, not computed; the AC force on the magnets is negligible by the same hand estimate.
- **Angle**: sensor offset drift, the TMAG5170's own +-0.4 deg and noise are not simulated (as in the ring study); the tilt pivot at the sensing magnet's near face and the carrier offset of 0.1 mm are assumptions.
- **Cost**: list prices of a different block size, an assumed custom premium and an assumed exchange rate; no quote.
- **Safety**: 32 exposed-able SmCo blocks (2.4 g) are mechanically captured but a knob removed for cleaning exposes 24 and the carrier four small magnets to steel debris and swallowing: a compliance review is needed before a child-safety statement (the SPEC already says the removable knob is not a child lock).
