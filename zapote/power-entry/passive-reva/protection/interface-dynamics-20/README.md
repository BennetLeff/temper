# Revision 20: exploratory vendor-model dynamics

Date: 2026-09-22. This is an isolated simulation of the Revision 19 clamp graph, not a compiled power-entry revision or a product qualification. The modeled circuit is the 14-component LT4363-1 / FDB33N25 candidate, with external source, load, and fault stimuli. Revision 11 remains the latest full compiled candidate (136 components).

## Model and circuit identity

- Simulator: official ADI LTspice 26.0.2 for macOS, downloaded from `https://ltspice.analog.com/software/LTspice_26.pkg`. Package SHA-256: `f3bf528b1df1b32d2fecf8e20b92bc17fe6f1723f42b527543c6c309c9739ff2`. The package and extracted app passed macOS signature verification outside the tool sandbox. The bundled LT4363-1 example ran successfully; its schematic, generated netlist, log, and compressed traces are retained in `vendor-example-smoke/`.
- LT4363-1 subcircuit: installed ADI `lib/sub/LT4363-1.sub`, SHA-256 `1348def925e80126ed0e1b78aae0370ff1e7cafbc729030de1990acd97db2bce`. The example netlist establishes pin order `FB OUT SNS GATE Vcc _SHDN UV GND _FLT ENOUT TMR`; the `XU1` line in every deck follows it.
- FDB33N25: exact named `VDMOS` entry in ADI's bundled `standard.mos`, SHA-256 of the complete library `f39c5119538391e01130560f9e303c260a8fd05e882a0518b261ad00431d0d5c`. This is the LTspice bundled Fairchild-marked model, not a separately validated current onsemi model. It has nonlinear capacitances but does not establish hot safe operating area or thermal behavior. The onsemi product page identifies the physical FDB33N25; no separate downloadable SPICE file was used.
- D1 uses the bundled `1N4148` diode model as a surrogate. The proposed part is `1N4148W`; its exact dynamics are not established by this fixture.
- Revision 19 native source: `../interface-dynamics-19/clamp.net`, SHA-256 `9cf36611b0d0539c714af83846bc26a41574b8d0c7ca63741972850cede8125a`. The deck's R1–R7, C1–C4, D1, M1 and U1 graph follows the native pin/net review, including R7/D1 on the controller side of R6. The FET body ties to SNS as in the ADI example. The separate external load and source do not appear in that native graph.

The common test source is 15 V through 0.5 Ω, and the load is a 130 Ω resistor (about 115 mA at nominal output). Those are declared investigative choices, **not** measured source impedance, the real converter startup load, or guaranteed application limits. C3 is 1.5 µF and C4 is 330 µF unless a deck states otherwise. The startup corner deck steps C3 through 1.3/1.7 µF and C4 through 198/495 µF, with an additional ideal 18 µF downstream; these are allocation endpoints, not measured biased ceramic capacitance. All runs are at the simulator's 27 °C nominal temperature.

## Results

All 20 transient decks ended normally under a 30 s host-process watchdog. Each deck, LTspice log, standard output, simulator database, and losslessly compressed transient and operating-point traces is retained here. Values below are readings from the `.log` files, rounded for review; the logs hold full precision. The simulator's internal elapsed-time field is not the host watchdog clock.

The surge runs at 100 and 20 µs maximum step agree on the 16.3507 V output peak. Integrated FET electrical energy differs by ~0.7%; other results are as printed in the logs. That numerical check does not validate the component model or the chosen application stimulus.

## Decision and remaining evidence

This work resolves the earlier **model/runtime availability** gap and exercises startup, surge, shutdown, UV, short and reset in an isolated vendor-model fixture. It does not qualify the clamp, approve FDB33N25 hot SOA, or establish a driver-pin voltage limit for the real PFC assembly. The modeled 35 V / 50 ms event specifically demonstrates an output outage and LT4363-1 latch-off; whether that event is required and whether output continuity is required remain application decisions.

The [input provenance audit](input-provenance.md) traces these conditions to their sources. The latest compiled candidate leaves AUX production external; direct IRM-10-15 and older IRM-10-24→LDO records are different proposals. The manufacturer supply specifications do not turn the fixture's 0.5 Ω, 1 ms and 50 ms choices into guaranteed fault limits.

Before a design choice or wider campaign, bind the source impedance and transient envelope (including slew and duration), the real converter startup/operating load, output continuity requirement, temperature and FET thermal/SOA conditions, biased downstream capacitance, and physical reset/discharge wiring. Then rerun those declared cases and measure the prototype. The 1N4148W substitution also needs a justified model or bench check. F1/F2 coordination, upstream control producers and complete-converter behavior are outside this fixture.

Manufacturer references: [LT4363 product/model page](https://www.analog.com/en/products/lt4363.html), [LTspice download page](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/design-tools-and-calculators/ltspice-simulator.html), [LT4363 Rev C datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/4363fb.pdf), [FDB33N25 product page](https://www.onsemi.com/products/discrete-power-modules/mosfets/low-medium-voltage-mosfets/fdb33n25).

## Reproduce and audit

With the same official LTspice version and bundled libraries installed, run `<LTspice.app>/Contents/SharedSupport/ltspice/LTspice/run_ltspice -b <deck>.cir` from this directory. For these runs, `<LTspice.app>` was `/tmp/LTspice_26-expanded/Package.pkg/Payload/Applications/LTspice.app`; install or re-extract the identified official package if that temporary copy is gone. The `.cir` files declare the stimulus, element graph, transient maximum step and `.meas` statements. The absolute Wine library path in `.lib` lines is this macOS installation's LTspice bottle; adjust that path for another installation without changing the model bytes. Review each `.stdout.log` and `.log` for completion and loaded libraries. A case completing is a solver result, not circuit acceptance.

The original 42 raw trace SHA-256 values are in `raw-uncompressed.sha256`; corresponding `*.raw.zst` files decompress byte-for-byte to those hashes (all 42 verified). Use `zstd -dc <name>.raw.zst > <name>.raw` to restore a trace. `artifact-hashes.sha256` inventories the retained local files. Temporary installer and installed library files are identified by digest above; the simulator package itself is not copied into this evidence directory.
