# Rev38 DWW barrier feasibility — native placements 03 and 04

**Review input, 2026-09-24. No insulation construction or release rule is accepted.**
This examines only the two source/HOT digital-isolator crossings in the
unrouted, slot-free `placement-review/03/native-diagnostic/section.kicad_pcb`
(SHA-256 `c277e9cae08213557f8bed43253253966229c695a9e754474d229babcf786ff5`).
The native extraction is `placement-review/03/native-diagnostic/native-export.json`
(SHA-256 `3c539845b5e6e7b841d2450732d9ac6bc25b8b64d0abe9d24637ce9fd651b56e`).
The later `placement-review/04` moves four SELV-owned parts away from HOT
receiver logic but retains both DWW positions and pad geometry. Its board
SHA-256 is `03dfa0f74a62666215315c880f49bc2b9ce7e7b2a9b964607a58c3f104cabb79`;
KiCad's native export was independently regenerated byte-for-byte. The
provisional 16 mm pad screen falls from 663 findings on `/03` to 104 on
`/04`, all within the two DWW footprints (52 per isolator). The slot
discussion therefore applies to the latest placement too.

The existing [insulation basis](INSULATION-BASIS.md) uses **16.0 mm** only as
a provisional PD3, Group IIIa/IIIb FR-4 *board creepage* screen for its
>400–500 V row. The selected edition, full voltage envelope, air clearance,
solid insulation, and appliance interpretation remain open.

## What the saved board actually places

| Crossing | Exact MPN and position | Native side assignment | Closest opposed copper lands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U1 `receiver.iso_protocol` | TI `ISO7741FQDWWRQ1`, `(252, 65, 180°)` mm | pins 1–8 SELV on the right; pins 9–16 HOT on the left | pad 1 `selv3v3` / pad 16 `hot_logic5`; also opposed rows 2/15 through 8/9 |
| U44 `receiver.iso_feedback` | TI `ISO6742FQDWWRQ1`, `(252, 105, 180°)` mm | same sides | pad 1 `selv3v3` / pad 16 `hot_logic5`; other opposed rows have the same geometry |

Both review-only footprints use the DWW0016A **optimized option 03** geometry
shown in the two TI package drawings: 16.725 mm between opposed pad centers,
1.525 × 0.6 mm rounded lands, 1.27 mm pitch, and 15.2 mm nominal copper-edge
gap. On U1, the example pad centers are `(260.3625, 69.445)` and
`(243.6375, 69.445)` mm; their inner edges are `x=259.6000` and
`x=244.4000`. U44 repeats these x coordinates at y `109.445` mm.
The 15.2 mm result is a **projected bare-copper gap**, neither an installed
air-clearance measurement nor a measured creepage path. The nominal gap is
0.8 mm short of that provisional 16 mm board-surface screen. Solder fillets,
misregistration, paste spread, mask openings, copper tolerances, and future
routing can reduce the assembled gap. The current native board has no tracks,
vias, zones, or slot to evaluate.

**Feasibility finding:** the selected DWW package and a 16 mm *board-surface*
schedule are geometrically compatible in principle if a manufactured slot
diverts the FR-4 surface path; the unmodified lands do not meet that screen.
This is not yet a safety-qualified combination. The slot cannot enlarge the
air path or the molded package path, and the two parts' PD2 component
certification has no established acceptance argument for the proposed PD3
appliance. Without that package/environment decision and the final air
requirement, this geometry cannot be promoted to a routed native release.

## Distinct paths through this crossing

| Path | Evidence now | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct air between exposed terminals and solder | TI specifies **>14.5 mm** terminal-to-terminal external *package* clearance for each DWW part; the saved bare lands project 15.2 mm apart. | A board slot does not increase direct air distance. Derive the required air clearance and measure the assembled, three-dimensional path at worst tolerances. If the accepted air requirement exceeds the actual package/assembly path, a slot alone cannot solve it. |
| FR-4 board surface between land/solder edges | Straight nominal path is 15.2 mm on the slot-free candidate. | This fails the project's provisional 16.0 mm Group IIIa/IIIb screen. A through-slot, qualified higher-CTI laminate, or another reviewed construction could change this path. Solder mask alone receives no insulation credit. |
| Across molded package surface | Both TI insulation tables specify **>14.5 mm** external package creepage and **CTI >600 V, material Group I**. | This is a separate material path; do not apply the FR-4 Group IIIa value to it or transfer the package's CTI to the PCB. The project currently screens a separately qualified Group I path at 12.6 mm, subject to product-safety review. |
| Inside the package | TI lists reinforced-component insulation and its own internal dielectric, impulse, and working-voltage ratings. | Component recognition and internal dielectric figures do not qualify the board lands, solder, slot, enclosure, or complete appliance. |
| Environment of component and appliance | Both TI insulation tables list **pollution degree 2** for their component certification. The project plans a vented **PD3** appliance environment. | A PD2 component certificate is not a PD3 appliance acceptance. Review whether an actual sealed compartment can earn a different microenvironment, whether these packages are suitable in PD3, or whether the crossing needs another component/construction. |

TI explicitly cautions that board mounting pads can reduce the package's
creepage and clearance and says equipment-specific isolation requirements
govern. The package's >14.5 mm dimension is therefore not a claim that the
assembled board has >14.5 mm on every path. The datasheets' land-pattern
option 03 yields the native 15.2 mm gap; shrinking lands further would need
manufacturer and assembly review and still cannot lengthen the molded
package's own external path.

## Through-slot geometry worth prototyping

A **measurement specimen**, not a footprint/board edit or qualified slot,
would mill an unplated, through-board, round-ended opening centered at
`x=252` mm, nominal width **1.0 mm**, over `y=55–75` mm at U1 and
`y=95–115` mm at U44. The two openings would be separate, each nominally
20 mm long. This strip lies between inner land edges `x=244.4` and
`x=259.6`; on the saved unrouted extraction it intersects no electrical
pad. The slot ends extend 5.255 mm beyond the outer edges of the end-row
lands (`y=60.255` and `69.745` for U1; +40 mm for U44). For a nominal
1 mm round-ended slot, the straight side-to-end legs can be illustrated
with `sqrt(7.1² + 4.755²)` mm each, or **17.09 mm before the path around
the rounded end**. The 4.755 mm uses the tangent of a 0.5 mm end radius;
even this margin over 16 mm is only about 1.09 mm before fabrication and
assembly tolerances. This only demonstrates useful geometric room
for a physical trial; it is not a creepage value. Actual end radii, tool and
registration tolerances, solder, component shadow, slot walls, back-side
surfaces, any contamination path, and all added copper must be measured in
the manufactured assembly. The direct air and molded-package paths remain.

The [replayable Edge.Cuts specimen](placement-review/04/slot-specimen.patch)
adds those two slots to a temporary copy of the frozen `/04` board. Its
[compact receipt](placement-review/04/slot-specimen-receipt.json) pins input,
patch and output hashes. KiCad 10.0.4 loads the patched board and reports
two DWW silkscreen-to-slot warnings, zero schematic-parity issues and 499
capped unconnected items. This only establishes that the opening can be
represented as a native outline and exposes silkscreen changes needed for
the specimen. The saved `/04` candidate remains slot-free and source-bound;
the patched board has no updated source manifest or insulation-rule file.

Before promoting even this specimen to a candidate board, obtain the
fabricator's controlled slot-width and end-radius capability, slot-to-copper
and copper-registration tolerances, finished thickness and CTI evidence,
and the assembler's decision on reflow and mechanical support with a slot
under the 10.2–10.4 mm molded body. Include solder geometry and mask/paste
limits; route a copper keepout on **all six copper layers** around the
opening; inspect the finished cut edge and debris process. The native
KiCad board must then contain the exact slot and keepout, be re-extracted,
pass explicit pair coverage and applicable DRC, and receive independent
3D creepage/clearance measurement. The current default KiCad DRC pass
does not establish these results.

Alternative review paths are a laminate with a documented Group I CTI
and an approved board-surface schedule; an enclosure that actually earns
the assumed component environment; or a different isolator architecture
if the final air/package/PD3 requirements exceed these DWW parts. No path
is selected here. The nearby Y1 capacitors, power crossing, connector,
mounting hardware, and other SELV/HOT pairs require their own shortest-path
reviews.
