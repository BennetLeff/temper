# Temper power-entry/PFC simulation assessment

The nominal authored model meets the unchanged normal-operation screens at all nine declared line/load points. The fault campaign exposes protection limits: the diode-short detector responds too late, a failed switch continues conducting despite gate-off, and the bypassed detector permits a local VD overvoltage. One switch-short recording ends prematurely and remains indeterminate. These are conditional simulation findings, not a qualified hardware design.

All declared cases have been assessed from their retained runs and completed archive analyses, including the incomplete SW-SHORT run. The companion [evidence index](reproducibility-index-140.json) identifies retained inputs, commands, tool identities, reports, and canonical raw recordings. Earlier failed attempts and strict-checker rejections remain preserved.

## Scope and operating target

The campaign evaluates the authored Temper PFC surrogate over nine normal points and prepared fault graphs. The Breville Control Freak comparison is an approximately **120 V AC, 60 Hz, 1800 W mains-input** benchmark ([power target](power-target-105.md)); it is not an 1800 W DC-output or pan-heating requirement. No PMP10948 efficiency is inherited. The existing 15 A modeled input screen remains unchanged; at 108 V it permits at most 1,620 W at ideal PF, and at 120 V at most 1,800 W at ideal PF.

Normal results are discrete authored-model screens, not interpolation, thermal, SOA, magnetic, fuse, or hardware evidence. The accepted cold 120 V / 190 Ω baseline is [accepted-baseline-11](../accepted-baseline-11/acceptance.json); its 650 ms run uses a three-cycle settled window. It reports 773.454 W resistive load power, 7.321738 A RMS input, a 383.378 V mean bus (381.305–385.386 V), and 0.273086% cycle drift. Ideal auxiliary rails exclude real fan, bias, and inverter consumption from a whole-appliance power claim. The `event-aware-normal-v1` path accepts source-bound complete samples while preserving the legacy strict checker’s repeated-timestamp rejection; this is a measurement-contract distinction, not a claim that duplicate timestamps are physical behavior. The nine-point acceptance and minimum margins are recorded in [checkpoint 53](operating-envelope-checkpoint-53.md) and [margin review 89](normal-screen-margins-89.md).

## Protection graph and functional interpretation

The frozen graph keeps F2 as the VD-to-VB path and models F2 opening as an ideal scripted state change. With F2 closed, the diode-short/both-short graph permits stored-bank energy through `Cbank → F2 → VD → shorted diode terminal → MOS/channel path → node 0`; the VD local capacitor is a separate reservoir. Source-fed `Lboost → switch node` current is distinct from this bank-discharge path. Voltage detectors observe VD/VB conditions and do not directly allocate current among F2, diode, body, MOS, or local-capacitor paths. Source-return ISENSE is therefore not a complete stored-energy measurement.

Protection functions that remain requirements rather than proven implementation are: a gate-independent and F2-independent interrupting path for a failed MOS channel; defined clearing time and interrupted energy; safe failure and restart interlock; F2 isolation across startup, charged, discharged, and residual-energy states; detector coverage of each fault mode; qualification of the assumed negative ISENSE clamp; and requalification of the graph if a reference D2 inrush bypass is adopted. The reference D2 path can bypass the boost inductor and MOS-controlled conversion path, so existing fault evidence cannot transfer to that changed graph.

The checker’s VD 500 V, VB 450 V, switch-node 650 V, gate 25 V, `|Lboost|` 100 A, gate-off, channel-off, and passive-report values are frozen **model screens**, not component ratings or hardware qualification. The diode diagnostic’s sampled -326.5 kA branch extreme and BOTH-SHORT’s 22.147 kA first off-state branch are idealized model observations with unproven mechanisms and no physical die/SOA meaning.

The [feedback topology review](bypass-feedback-topology-133.md) identifies a specific revision requirement: `Rfb1` senses VB on the bank side of F2; opening F2 separates that feedback node from VD and its local capacitor. Local VD must remain protected when those nodes separate. Static connectivity supports this concern but does not uniquely establish the dynamic cause of the 651 V excursion. The bypass validator reports the VD violation before reaching its bypass-flag rule; the independent waveform diagnostic supplies the negative-control evidence.

A failed-MOS source-current path must be interrupted independently of its gate command and F2 bank isolation. Both local VD energy and VB bank energy require explicit handling; opening F2 alone cannot be credited with clearing every path. Detector coverage, clearing/energy limits, actuator failure behavior, and restart interlocks remain hardware design requirements in [116](protection-requirements-116.md). No component or protection threshold was changed to obtain a pass.

## Controller, clamp, magnetic, and reference bounds

The selected clamp remains an assumed model with unresolved low-current forward-voltage, leakage, temperature, and lot bounds. The clamp audit records the TI worst-case PCL threshold of -0.438 V and recommended ISENSE lower limit of -1.1 V, which constrain the required clamp behavior and the selected BAV23C’s missing guaranteed low-current VF-versus-temperature/lot envelope; its fixture limits are engineering screens, not vendor limits ([clamp evidence gap](clamp-evidence-gap-review-49.md)). The campaign inductor is a fixed 180 µH / 20 mΩ model. The retained Würth package is a separate linear RLC vendor asset, not adopted into the campaign, and omits nonlinear DC-bias, saturation, temperature, and thermal behavior ([vendor-model review](inductor-manufacturer-audit-72/vendor-model-review-73.md)); the datasheet’s 180 µH ±20%, 20 mΩ maximum DCR, 24.5 A thermal rating, and 43 A typical saturation criterion are not encoded as guarantees.

The assumed clamp's temperature fixtures pass the selected 1 µA / 2 mV loading screens at -20 and 25 °C and fail at 85 and 125 °C ([retained results](../clamp/host-rerun/checks.txt)). These are engineering screens on an assumed model, not vendor guarantees. The [saved F2-CREST ISENSE scan](../faults/saved-pin-observability-50/full-F2-CREST38/parent-observability-review.md) ranges from -0.306538 V to +0.000116321 V: it does not pass the strict recommended upper bound of 0 V, although no sampled voltage exceeds the cited absolute voltage limits. PCL request and hold remain zero, so this case does not exercise PCL or a large negative clamp event. Diode current and silicon input-current compliance are not established.

The selected Vishay low-current VF versus temperature/lot envelope remains **unbounded by guaranteed data**. This campaign explicitly limits its claims to the retained authored diode law; it does not substitute simulation sensitivity for that missing part characteristic. Primary TI/Vishay documents and the exact unresolved measurements are retained in the [datasheet audit](../clamp/datasheet-audit/report.md). Closing the part-level gap requires vendor characterization or hardware measurement.

MOS/diode models do not establish switching loss, SOA, physical short behavior, or die-current sharing. F2 is an ideal scripted switch with no fuse arc or clearing law. Capacitors lack a validated ESR/ESL/ripple/temperature envelope, and auxiliary rails are ideal. Large fault-current transients therefore diagnose the modeled topology without predicting credible physical peak currents or survival.

The local UCC28180 surrogate and protection path are behavioral. TI’s encrypted TINA transient and PSpice average assets are available but not open ngspice transient models; no vendor correlation has been performed ([vendor-model availability](ucc28180-vendor-model-availability.md)). [Reference deviations 107](ucc28180-reference-deviations-107.md) records EVM and TIDA-00779 Rev D differences in current sensing, feedback/follower, compensation, frequency, bias, gate driver, magnetics, and D2 inrush connectivity. Those references are comparison evidence, not automatic component qualification or an adoption decision.

The six small controller fixtures use the accepted controller include, but their integrated protection closure differs; they are not whole-converter or TI-silicon validation ([binding and coverage](accepted-controller-test-binding-54.md)).

For a reference-anchored revision, UCC28180EVM-573 is the same-controller 360 W / 390 V wiring reference; TIDA-00779 is the same-controller high-power reference with a stated 190–270 VAC full-power range. PMP10948 is a useful 120 VAC benchmark but uses a different UCC28063 interleaved transition-mode architecture. Its efficiency and compensation cannot be inherited by this design. The [visually checked deviation inventory](ucc28180-reference-deviations-107.md) records actual schematic differences, including TIDA Rev D bias, driver, voltage follower, and the D2 inrush path absent from the current model. Neither topology adoption nor hardware scaling is completed by this comparison.

## Schematic and hardware follow-up

Later hardware evidence must measure synchronized line/bridge/inductor/shunt current, VD/VB, VDS/VGS, gate-enable, ARM/PERMIT/fault, controller state, rails, temperatures, probe settings, calibration, and raw traces. It must separately establish source-current interruption, stored-energy handling, clearing time, device survival, restart behavior, clamp behavior, magnetic loss/saturation, capacitor ripple, and any D2 inrush path. The hardware document is an observation/comparison specification, not an energization authorization.

The next design revision should first allocate the 1800 W nominal mains-input budget across PFC, auxiliary, inverter, and coupling losses. At a retained 15 A input constraint, low-line power must be derated; the 108 V ideal-PF upper bound is 1620 W before those downstream losses. Then anchor the chosen controller architecture to the published references and review every deviation, particularly feedback placement, independent VD protection, failed-switch source interruption, inrush paths, and restart behavior. The current simulation findings cannot be transferred unchanged to a revised circuit.

Remaining hardware work includes the selected clamp characteristic, controller correlation, nonlinear magnetics, device and capacitor stresses, real auxiliary behavior, fuse/actuator clearing, and synchronized fault measurements. Cooking parity with the Control Freak additionally requires defined cookware, heat-up, temperature stability and disturbance-recovery tests; this PFC-only study does not establish pan power or cooking performance.

The retained SW-SHORT abort remains a coverage exclusion: its own normal prefix is accepted, but its full fault phase and post-event window are unavailable. BYPASS and the other completed faults are assessed only over their declared sampled horizons. A failed or indeterminate case is a completed assessment of that attempt, not evidence that the protection is adequate.
