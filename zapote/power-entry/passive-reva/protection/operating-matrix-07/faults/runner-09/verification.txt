Actual CLI smoke
----------------

An external synthetic tracker emitted a 42-column trace with 1001 points at
20 ns spacing through the five-argument tracker contract. The real adapter
and real checker binaries were used, not test doubles. The 20 us case returned
`SCHEMA_AND_EVIDENCE_OK` and `checker_pass`; stage1 and stage2 statuses were
all zero. The sole retained trace was 12 KiB `raw.trace.tsv.gz`; all FIFOs
were removed.

Failure controls
----------------

An adapter fixture that exits 19 before opening output FIFOs was rejected as
`adapter rejected trace` within the bounded timeout, and its downstream
decoder/checker were killed and reaped. A compressor fixture that exits 17 was
rejected before stage 2. Neither left FIFO paths behind. These controls verify
cleanup behavior, not electrical correctness.

A second 42-column fixture retained a channel current after a switch-short;
the real checker returned `PROTECTION_GAP`, and the supervisor classified it
as `expected_checker_protection_gap` with transport complete. A checker fixture
that exits 23 before opening its FIFO was rejected in about 0.4 seconds, with
downstream children killed/reaped and a structured stage-2 failure receipt. A
missing tracker executable produced a `missing_executable` receipt without
creating any FIFO.

Before launch, the supervisor refuses every expected output path if it already
exists. `source-identity.json` records the sorted supported local `.include`
closure (including nested include bytes) and SHA-256 for tracker, adapter,
checker and pigz. Unsupported `.inc`/`.lib` forms and root escapes fail closed;
the closure is rehashed after transport. The raw gzip SHA-256 is recorded in
`result.json` after a completed transport.

Checker status is deliberately distinguished: an explicit `PROTECTION_GAP`
is recorded as expected gap, while an ordinary checker rejection or parse
failure is returned as an error. There is no unconditional PASS path.
