# Fan and thermal fault dependency contract

This is a producer-side U1 interface inventory for a future cooling circuit. It is **not** a built, tested or joined protective circuit. Fan choice, protected supply and mechanical mounting remain open; electrical thresholds and timing are not selected by this document.

## Consumer and required default

The accepted standalone interlock's `zapote/interlock/INTERFACES.md` assigns J1 pin 4 to active-high **Heatsink fault**. A powered, valid remote producer must actively sink this line to ≤0.3 V when healthy and let it reach ≥2.7 V for a fault. Its local 10 kΩ ±1% pullup makes an open fault conductor assert under the stated 20 µA leakage budget. The proposed cooling producer must be high-impedance or fault-asserted when its supply, sensor or self-check is unavailable; an unpowered output that clamps the pullup low would defeat the claimed disconnect behavior. This power-off electrical property needs circuit-level proof. `SENSOR_LIVE` at J2 pin 5 must separately go low when relevant sensing is invalid; its producer is not yet qualified. The interlock's PERMIT receiver requires a driven high and a local pulldown, so loss of the interlock supply itself must disable heating at the receiving stage.

**Concrete output topology to construct:** one low-side open-drain/open-collector healthy sink at the interlock side, with a local gate/base pulldown and a hardware rail-valid qualification in series with the health command. Only a powered, verified `RUN_READY` state may turn it on. Loss of the cooling rail, controller reset, output-wire break or an unpowered remote board leaves it off so J1 pin 4 rises by the interlock pullup. The sink must hold ≤0.3 V while carrying the worst-case ~350 µA pullup current at 3.465 V and 9.9 kΩ; power-off leakage plus cable leakage must remain within 20 µA at the fault pin. An MCU GPIO wired directly to J1 is **not** this proof: its unpowered clamp and boot state must be measured. A separate sensing-valid output to `SENSOR_LIVE` is needed; neither output may be tied permanently healthy.

Fan fault is a **heating inhibit** for both power entry and inverter once integrated. Rev38's source-permission path and the inverter permit receiver must each be checked against this actual interlock PERMIT connection; standalone interlock acceptance alone does not prove the joined stop path. A cooling trip remains latched while its controller stays powered; reset requires fault removal and a fresh authorized reset edge, with inspection if product policy requires it. Tach return alone cannot reset the latch. The standalone interlock's reset timing and watchdog contract continue to apply.

## Producer inputs and faults to distinguish

| Input or state | Required interpretation | Evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fan supply at startup | Healthy only after selected supply reaches its valid window and each fan demonstrates the required response; a missing rail cannot read healthy. | Selected regulated rail, startup/stall current, allowed spin-up time and brownout behavior. The GBU two-Sunon concept requires regulated 12 V and at least 0.276 A combined maximum running current; do not connect it directly to 15 V (`bridge-cooling.md`). The GBJ Sanyo supply remains to be bound to its exact electrical data. |
| Each fan tach | Missing, implausible or stuck tach asserts a cooling fault after a justified startup grace interval. Two-fan concept needs independently monitored tach channels. | Fan tach electrical levels, pullup supply and power-off behavior, pulse-rate window, noise filter and fault latency. A two-wire legacy fan circuit cannot provide this by assumption. |
| Fin-path airflow | Tach spinning does not prove an open inlet or useful fin flow. Blocked inlet, clogged grille, broken blade and recirculation must be detected by independent airflow or thermal evidence. | Installed pressure/flow curve and selected airflow sensor or demonstrated temperature supervision with transient bound. |
| Bridge case/spreader and hottest board contact | Overtemperature, open/short sensor or implausible reading asserts fault. The GBU legacy screen would require case trip below 75 °C *minus* sensor error and shutdown overshoot; with illustrative 2 K + 3 K it is ≤70 °C, not a chosen setting. The modeled PCB neck is 107.86 °C against 110 °C, leaving only 2.14 K. | Actual sensor location, thermal lag/error, material ratings and transient shutdown overshoot. A nonempty full-power trip window is not established. GBJ's different package model requires its own sensor calibration and limits. |
| Host/power loss and restart | Missing sensing rail, MCU reset or disconnected cooling harness cannot be considered healthy; PERMIT must fall through the joined hardware path. Recovery of tach or temperature alone must not restart heat. | Electrical power-off and open-wire tests; joined Rev38/inverter permit wiring; latch/reset tests on the assembled board. |

The historical `elec/src/modules.ato::ThermalSystem` has a 60 mm, two-wire Sunon fan and 39 Ω dropper from 15 V. It cannot be used as the power/tach circuit for either current cooling candidate. Selecting a fan and rail precedes a native circuit; the standby, running, startup, stalled and rail-fault loads must be handed to the auxiliary supply owner.

## State and digital acceptance contract

| State | Entry and output | Permitted transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `UNPOWERED` | Default on supply loss/reset. Healthy sink **off**, `SENSOR_LIVE` low; no fan-start grace may mask this. A previous volatile cooling-fault reason is not retained after complete power loss; fresh fan/sensor qualification and the separate deliberate interlock/Rev38 reset sequence are required before heating can restart. Any inspection-before-restart requirement needs durable fault storage or a physical service-reset mechanism. | Valid rail and sensing self-check may enter `STARTING`, with interlock fault still high; heating still requires the interlock/Rev38 deliberate restart protocol. |
| `STARTING` | Fan commanded on; require rail in selected band, sensor validity and independently plausible tach from **every installed fan**. Healthy sink remains off. Start timer begins on a fresh power-on command, not on each tach edge. | Enter `RUN_READY` only after fan-start criteria and thermal/airflow plausibility pass within a bounded `T_start_max`. Timer expiry, invalid sensor or rail loss enters `FAULT_LATCHED`. `T_start_max` is unselected until cold/hot loaded starts are characterized. |
| `RUN_READY` | Healthy sink **on** only while all qualified conditions hold. Monitor each tach continuously; a missing/implausible tach is not given a new startup grace period. Thermal limit, airflow proxy failure, rail invalidity or watchdog/self-check failure removes healthy sink immediately under a proved output latency and latches fault. | Fault → `FAULT_LATCHED`. Normal commanded heating stop may keep fans running for cool-down but does not itself grant a new heating permit. |
| `FAULT_LATCHED` | Healthy sink off, `SENSOR_LIVE` low if sensing is invalid, fault reason retained. Fan may run for cooling if supply exists. Automatic fan motor recovery cannot clear the latch. | Only after fault removal, temperature below a validated restart threshold and a deliberate reset request may a *fresh* `STARTING` cycle begin. Inspection is additionally required only if the adopted product policy demands it. The interlock still requires its own fresh RESET_N edge after inputs have been healthy for ≥1 ms; holding reset low cannot restart. |

Digital acceptance vectors for the eventual Rust owner and joined circuit: (1) boot, brownout and remote power-off never sink J1-4; (2) zero or one tach on a two-fan concept never enters `RUN_READY`; (3) missing tach after ready latches without restarting `T_start_max`; (4) fan self-restart after a rotor lock does not clear the cooker latch; (5) stuck-high/stuck-low tach, disconnected temperature sensor and blocked inlet with tach still spinning do not read healthy; (6) thermal fault and cooling-rail failure each remove the sink regardless of MCU state; (7) reset held low through a fault/recovery does not recreate PERMIT; (8) each open wire and a powered-off remote output meets the interlock J1-4 voltage/leakage limits. Test the actual consumer chain through PFC and inverter enables before claiming a stop latency. No numeric trip, tach-frequency band or timer is accepted yet.

The fan's own lockout is volatile and auto-recovers. If the product requires *inspection* even after a complete mains power cycle, the cooling fault reason needs durable storage or a physical service-reset mechanism; this design has neither yet. At minimum, a cooling rail dropout or mains restart may never itself generate the interlock RESET_N edge or Rev38 authorization. The joined reset policy, including whether all fan faults require service inspection, is an integration decision and must be tested across power cycles.

Primary fan evidence: the exact Sunon `MF80251V1-1000U-G99` sheet (SHA-256 `7f959f5b7f8b3db41285325ca167ab3e6b7b23ec9b01f17b69e4a4dbd0f3eb0c`, pp. 3–8) gives 12 V rated, 4.5–13.8 V operating, 120 mA nominal/138 mA maximum running, 3200 rpm ±10% at rated voltage and an FG output with maximum 5 mA sink; its motor auto-restarts within 10 s after a rotor lock. The Sanyo `9RA1212E1001` catalog (SHA-256 `a9dd3971db4030adab9935759c83c4ca7d6ad785ff6692113a231ea97ab4962e`, printed pp. 181, 183, 616, 618) gives 12 V rated, 7–13.8 V operating, 0.47 A/5.6 W rated **in free air**, 3600 rpm and a pulse sensor. Its general pulse-sensor example has two pulses per revolution, open-collector output, VCE ≤30 V, Ic ≤5 mA and VOL ≤0.6 V at the stated test point; that page cautions specifications can differ by model. Thus ~107 Hz Sunon and ~120 Hz Sanyo are nominal tach expectations, **not** accepted speed windows. The Sanyo locked-rotor protection also restarts when obstruction clears (p. 616). Confirm exact tach/power behavior for the selected MPN over rail, load and temperature before choosing interface thresholds.

Physical qualification remains **NOT RUN**. The GBJ protocol at `zapote/power-entry/passive-reva/cooling/qualification-protocol.md` identifies the needed installed assembly and records: calibrated flow/pressure with final grille/enclosure, synchronized bridge electrical loss, case/spreader/board temperatures, fan tach, and separate controlled fan-stop or airflow-loss capture. Repeat with each fan lost, tach disconnected, blocked inlet and high inlet air as applicable. Prove the stop latency and peak temperature before assigning any trip delay or claiming cooling protection.
